Reference · The daily shape

The daily 8 hours

Eight hours is not eight hours of the same thing. Four hours of hard building, one hour of reading, one hour of drills, and the rest is recovery and record-keeping.

The one idea on this page: your working memory is tiny and your attention is the scarce resource, not your time. So the day is built around two protected two-hour build blocks. Everything else exists to make those four hours count.

The day, block by block

#BlockTimeWhat happens, exactly
1Retrieval warm-up30 min Recall yesterday's and last month's material from memory, before opening any notes. Spaced-repetition cards, or a blank page where you write what you remember and then check. This block is where long-term memory is actually built.
2Build block 1120 min The month's primitive. No email, no chat, no phone, no video. One tab of documentation allowed. When you hit a wall: write the question on paper first, keep going if you can, look it up only at the end of the block.
3Break20 min Away from the screen. Walk. No feeds — the brain is consolidating, and scrolling interrupts it.
4Build block 2120 min Same rules. Start by fixing the questions you wrote in block 1. This is where the hard debugging goes — you are warm now and it is the strongest part of the day.
5Lunch and walk60 min Real food, real daylight. Non-negotiable, especially on the days you feel you cannot spare it.
6Primary source60 min Read the month's one book or paper. Notes in your own words only — copying a sentence teaches you nothing. Every claim gets one question: how would I check this?
7Drills45 min Timed and uncomfortable on purpose: a coding problem with a clock, a query plan to read, a design sketched in 20 minutes, or an explanation spoken out loud and recorded. Mix in old months — this is the interleaving that makes knowledge flexible.
8Log and plan25 min Fill the evidence ledger below. Then write the first sentence of tomorrow's build block, so you start tomorrow without deciding anything.
Why this shape and not "just code for 8 hours" Learning has two different strengths. Fluency is being able to retrieve something right now — it grows fast and feels like mastery. Storage is still having it in six months — it grows only under difficulty: recalling from memory, spacing practice out over time, and mixing topics up. Block 1 and block 7 exist purely to build storage strength. Skipping them feels efficient and quietly wastes the year.

The week

DayShape
Mon–SatThe eight-hour day above. Six days, 48 hours.
SundayOff. Two optional light things and nothing else: publish the week's writing, and one hour in a community (ask one question, or answer someone else's). No building. Rest is part of the plan, not a gap in it.

The spacing schedule

Everything you learn gets reviewed on this schedule, in block 1, from memory first:

Review atWhat you do
Day 1 (next morning)Write yesterday's idea from a blank page. Compare to your notes. Fix what you got wrong, in your notes, in red.
Day 3Explain it out loud in three minutes, recorded. Listen back once. Where you hesitate is where you do not know it.
Day 7Re-implement the smallest version from scratch, no notes, 20 minutes.
Day 21Answer one interview-style question on it cold, timed.
Day 60Teach it to someone, or write the public post about it.
Every gate monthMonths 3, 6, 9, and 12 re-test all earlier months, mixed together, in random order. This is the interleaving that makes it real knowledge instead of a recent memory.

The evidence ledger

Twenty-five minutes at the end of every day. This is the file that decides whether a month passed, so it is written honestly or not at all. One entry per day, appended to a single file:

## 2026-08-11 · Month 1, Day 2 · 7h50 logged

Built:      pipe() + dup2 wiring in shell.py — `ls | wc -l` works
Evidence:   test_pipes.py 4/4 passing; screenshot of fd table on paper
Broke:      forgot to close the write end in the parent — hung forever
Learned:    a reader only sees EOF when EVERY write end is closed  <- the real lesson
Still don't understand: why the child needs its own close() at all
Tomorrow:   redirection (> and >>), then job control basics
Honest?     Block 2 lost 40 min to Slack. Phone in another room tomorrow.
The line that matters most "Still don't understand". If that line is empty for three days in a row, you are not working at the edge of your ability — you are doing comfortable work and calling it study. Make the work harder the same day.

Rules for the build blocks

  1. Phone in another room. Not face down. Another room.
  2. One documentation tab. No video, no forum, no AI answer during the block. Write your question down and carry on.
  3. Question before lookup. Written question first, then search. This turns a lookup into a memory instead of a copy.
  4. Type it, never paste it. Pasted code teaches nothing. Your fingers are part of the learning.
  5. When stuck for 30 minutes, change the question. Make a smaller version that fails the same way. A bug you can reproduce in ten lines is already half solved.
  6. End the block with something that runs. Even if it is wrong. Never stop on a blank screen — you will not want to come back to it.

The bad day rule

Some days you will be sick, or wrecked, or your paid work will eat the day. The rule is not "catch up tomorrow", because that never happens. The rule is:

The two-hour floor

On a bad day you do block 1 and one build block — two and a half hours — and then you stop, log it honestly, and go to bed. The streak survives. The plan survives. A zero day breaks the habit; a small day does not.

If eight hours a day is not actually available Then say so now, not in Month 3. If a full-time job also has to fit in the day, this plan should be recut to four hours a day across eighteen months, with the same twelve gates in the same order. The gates are what matter. The pace is negotiable — once, at the start, in the open.
Remember this
  • Two protected two-hour build blocks are the day. Everything else supports them.
  • Block 1 is recall from memory, before notes. That is where long-term memory is built.
  • Fluency ≠ storage. Feeling fluent today does not mean remembering it in June.
  • Review at day 1, 3, 7, 21, 60; gate months re-test everything, mixed up.
  • Question written down before any lookup. Type code, never paste it.
  • The ledger's "still don't understand" line is the health check. Empty for three days means the work is too easy.
  • Bad day = two and a half hours, logged honestly. Never a zero day.

If you remember one thing: protect the four building hours; the rest of the day is scaffolding.
