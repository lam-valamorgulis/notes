The big picture — how it all connects

Everything in Flutter grows from one fact: Flutter draws every pixel itself. It asks the operating system for a blank canvas, then paints the whole interface with its own engine. That single decision explains the identical look on both platforms, the app size, and why performance is your responsibility rather than the platform's. From it follows the second idea: a widget is a description, not a thing — so you never edit the screen, you change data and let Flutter re-describe it. Every hard part of Flutter is a consequence of those two ideas: rebuild loops, keys, constraints, state management, and jank.

You will learn it the only way learning is real: by building. Months 1–3 lay the mental model — enough Dart to be dangerous, what setState actually does, why layout behaves the way it does, and then real data from a real API. Months 4–6 turn screens into systems: Riverpod deeply, then the same feature again in BLoC so you can argue both in an interview, then clean architecture, routing, and offline-first — where the spine app you keep hardening all year begins.

Months 7–9 are production engineering, and they are where "developer" becomes "senior": tests that gate a pull request, the three trees and the frame pipeline used to kill jank with measurements instead of guesses, and native interop so "Flutter can't do that" stops being a blocker. Months 10–12 serve the second half of the mission — being able to sell the work. You ship to both stores from an automated pipeline, polish the interface until it wins contracts, and finish with mobile system design, timed mock interviews, and a real client estimate.

Interview practice is not saved for the end. Every lesson closes with spoken recall cards — you answer as if an interviewer just asked, then compare against a model answer. Speaking is a separate skill from knowing, and it is drilled from Lesson 1.
