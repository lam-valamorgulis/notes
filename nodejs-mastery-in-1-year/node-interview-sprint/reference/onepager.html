The night before

Everything for the last hour. Nothing new — only the things that must arrive without thinking.

Tonight's rule: learn nothing new. Read this page. Say your three stories out loud once. Check your equipment. Sleep.

The ten questions that always come up

1What is Node.js?

"V8 taken out of the browser, plus libuv, a C library that talks to the operating system. One thread runs my JavaScript. Slow work is handed away, and the result comes back as a queued callback."

2Explain the event loop.

"A fixed route over queues: timers, pending, poll, check, close. Between every step, Node drains nextTick fully, then microtasks fully. Then it advances one phase."

3nextTick or setImmediate?

"The names are backwards. nextTick runs much sooner, at the very next checkpoint. setImmediate waits for the check phase. Only nextTick can starve the loop."

4How do you handle async errors?

"Every await inside a try, or a .catch on every chain. A missing await is not caught — the process crashes with an unhandled rejection. I set process.exitCode rather than calling process.exit()."

5500 requests, without killing anything?

"Bounded concurrency. A small pool of workers pulling from one index, a timeout per request, and a retry with backoff and jitter. Never an unbounded Promise.all over the whole list."

6What is backpressure?

"write() returns false when the destination is behind. If I ignore it, the queue grows in memory and nothing errors. Measured: 172 MB peak ignoring it, 84 MB honouring it."

7pipe or pipeline?

"Always pipeline. It owns the whole chain, so one error destroys every stream in it. After a sink error, pipe left the source destroyed: false; pipeline gave true."

8How do you shut down cleanly?

"On SIGTERM: stop accepting, close idle connections, finish in-flight work, close the pool, set process.exitCode. Measured: server.close() took 3540 ms, and 538 ms with closeIdleConnections()."

9Worker threads or cluster?

"Worker threads for CPU work inside one process. cluster for serving more requests, one process per core. They share nothing — two cluster workers each reported counter = 1."

10Tell me about something you built.

"One sentence on what it is. Then the one hard constraint. Then what I personally did. Then a number. Then what I would change now."

The twelve rescue sentences

Did not understand

  • "Sorry, could you say that again a bit slower?"
  • "Just to check — you are asking about X, is that right?"
  • "I do not know that term. What does it do?"

Need a moment

  • "Let me think about that for a second."
  • "There are two ways to look at this. I will start with the simpler one."
  • "Can I draw this? It is easier to show than to say."

Do not know

  • "I have not used that. What I know that is close is…"
  • "I do not know. My guess is X, and I would check it by Y."
  • "I would rather look that up than give you a confident wrong answer."

Lost the thread

  • "Sorry, let me start that again."
  • "The short version is…"
  • "Does that answer it, or shall I go deeper?"

The live-coding opening — say this before your first keystroke

"Let me state my assumptions first. Here is what I will build. Here are the decisions I am making, and the failure cases I will handle. Is that the scope you want, or would you rather I focus somewhere specific?"

Then never go silent for more than about ten seconds. If you are stuck, say what you are stuck on. That sentence is worth more than the decision.

The debugging opening — say this before you guess anything

"Before I guess a cause, here is the first number I would collect. I want to know whether the event loop is blocked, because that splits the whole search in two."

Your six questions

  1. What does on-call look like here, and how often does it actually fire?
  2. What is the deploy story, and how long does a rollback take?
  3. Where does the Node service sit, and what is behind it?
  4. What was the hardest production incident this year?
  5. How do you decide between fixing something and rewriting it?
  6. What would a good first three months look like for this person?

The last sixty seconds

  1. Interest, and be specific. "This is the kind of backend I want — real load, real failure modes."
  2. One reason you fit. "What I bring straight away is measuring before I change anything."
  3. Next step. "What are the next steps, and when should I expect to hear?"

The only three things that matter tomorrow
  • Pause one second before every answer. It sounds calm, and it stops you answering the wrong question.
  • Think out loud. They cannot grade silence.
  • Say "I do not know" cleanly when it is true. It is worth more than a guess, every time.
  • Name a number when you have one. A measurement beats an opinion in every round.
  • Never apologise for your English. Slow down instead.

If you remember one thing: you are not taking an exam. You are showing someone what it is like to work with you.
