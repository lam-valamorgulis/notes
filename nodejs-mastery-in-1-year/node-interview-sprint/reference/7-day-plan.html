The 7-Day Plan

Seven days, about eight hours each. Every block has one job. Each one ends with something you must say out loud. Your ticks are saved in this browser.

0 / 0 blocks done
The whole week in one line: three days rebuilding the answers, one day turning your own service into your strongest material, one day on production, one day on scale and design, and one day saying all of it out loud.
The rule that makes this work Half of every day is spoken, not read. Reading a lesson and thinking "I know that" is the illusion this week exists to break. If a block says out loud, do it out loud. Alone in a room, at full volume. It feels ridiculous. It is the only part that transfers.

If you get fewer than seven days Cut in this order. Day 6 first, because a design round is rarely the whole interview. Then Day 3's second kata run. Then Day 2's reading. Never cut Day 4 or Day 7. Day 4 is the only material nobody can copy from you. Day 7 is the only day that turns knowledge into speech.
Trap — reading instead of speaking Every day here has more spoken hours than reading hours. That is deliberate, and it will feel wrong. Re-reading a lesson feels productive and changes almost nothing. Saying it badly out loud, once, teaches more than reading it well three times.
Remember this · the 20-second recap
  • Seven days, about eight hours each. Twenty-one lessons and four katas.
  • Half of every day is spoken, not read. That is the whole method.
  • Every kata runs twice. The second run is the one that tells the truth.
  • Day 4 decides the interview. It is the only material that is uniquely yours.
  • Day 7 adds nothing new. It converts what you know into speech.
  • If days are cut, cut Day 6 first. Never Day 4 and never Day 7.

If you remember one thing: the ticks are not the point. The recordings are.
