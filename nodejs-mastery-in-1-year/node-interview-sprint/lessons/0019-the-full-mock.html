Start from the problem

You can read every lesson in this sprint and still freeze in the room.

That is not a knowledge failure. Reading and speaking use different skills.

Reading is recognition. You see the answer and think "yes, I know that". Recognition feels like mastery and is not.

Speaking is retrieval. Nothing is on the page. You must pull the answer out of memory, in order, in English, while a stranger watches.

So the only honest test is a test that looks like the real thing. That means a clock, a score, and no notes.

The rule for today

If you did not say it out loud, you did not practise it. Reading the answer again is the thing that feels like work and is not.

Four rounds, and they are not worth the same

Most candidates prepare as if every round scores equally. They do not.

The three technical rounds test things thousands of people can also do. Round 4 tests something only you can do.

Round 4 outweighs the three trivia rounds put together. Say that to yourself before you start.

Say it in the simplest words — the Feynman check

1 · In plain words Knowing an answer and saying an answer are two different skills. Only one of them is tested tomorrow.

Today you practise the one that is tested. You say fifty answers out loud, mark each one, and repair the worst five.

2 · One analogy A musician who has read the score is not ready for the concert. Reading the notes is not playing them.

The last rehearsal is played at full speed, in the room, with mistakes left in. That is what today is.

…and where the analogy breaks A musician plays the same piece every night. You cannot know tomorrow's questions.

So you are not rehearsing answers. You are rehearsing the shape of an answer. State the rule, name the trade-off, give a number, say what you would measure. That shape survives a question you have never seen. A memorised answer does not.

3 · The sticking point People re-read their weak answers instead of re-saying them. Re-reading feels productive and changes almost nothing.

The gap only closes when the words leave your mouth. Fix five answers, then say each one out loud again before you stop.

4 · Now you explain it back

In two or three sentences, say why round 4 outweighs the technical rounds. Then say what you will do with your five worst answers. Send it to me and I will tell you where the gap is. Do not skip this. It is the last teach-back of the sprint.

Two tasks. Both are katas you have already run. This time you are marked on narration too.

Empty file. Talk before you type. See Kata 2 for the reference solution and the talking script.

Before round 4, say this to yourself

"This is the only material in the whole interview that nobody else in the queue has. I will not rush it, and I will give one real number."

The score sheet, and what to do with it

Mark every answer with one of three marks. Be honest. A generous mark today costs you tomorrow.

MarkMeansAction
✓Clean. Arrived without hesitation.Nothing. Leave it alone.
~Got there, but slowly or in a mess.Only fix it if it is in your worst five.
✗Lost it. Guessed, or went silent.Candidate for the worst five.
After the mock · the only repair rule

Take your five worst answers. Only five, even if you marked eleven badly.

Fix those five, then say each one out loud again before you stop. Trying to fix everything on the last day fixes nothing.
  • Write the five down, in order, worst first.
  • For each, find the one sentence you were missing.
  • Say the whole answer out loud, from the start, twice.
  • Do not re-read the lesson. Say the answer.
  • Then stop. Adding a sixth is how the first five get lost.

Remember this · the 20-second recap
  • Reading is recognition. Speaking is retrieval. Only retrieval is tested tomorrow.
  • Four rounds: live coding, debugging, system design, and the service you built.
  • Round 4 outweighs the other three together. It is the shortest and the most valuable.
  • Round 2 is marked on the order you check things in, not on the cause you name.
  • Round 3 is marked on the questions you ask before you draw anything.
  • Mark every answer ✓ ~ ✗, honestly. A kind mark today is a loss tomorrow.
  • Fix your five worst only, then say each one out loud again, and stop.

If you remember one thing: if you did not say it out loud, you did not practise it.
