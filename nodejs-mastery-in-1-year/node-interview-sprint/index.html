The big picture — how it all connects

A Node backend interview asks about six things. The runtime, async, streams, production behaviour, scale, and something you built.

You have met all six in the parent course. The gap this week closes is not knowledge. It is retrieval under pressure. So every day ends in something said out loud.

Day 1 rebuilds the two answers that always come first. What Node actually is, and what the loop does with your callbacks.

Day 2 is where real services fail. Async errors that vanish, and running five hundred things at once without killing anything.

Day 3 separates people who have moved real data from people who have read about it. Backpressure with a number attached, and why pipeline exists.

Day 4 is the day that decides the interview. Your spine API becomes an architecture you can draw. Then two bugs you can explain by mechanism, and a ninety-second story.

Everyone else in the queue can recite the event loop. Nobody else has your service.

Day 5 is production. Shutting down without dropping a request, knowing when to crash, and finding a real leak.

Day 6 is more than one core, the four pieces of a data layer, and a system on a whiteboard. Day 7 is the full mock.

Running underneath it are four timed katas. Live coding is a performance skill, not a knowledge skill. Each one has a clock, a talking script, and a checklist. Run every one twice. The second run is the one that tells the truth.

Read this one first The 7-Day Plan is the schedule — every block of every day, with ticks saved in your browser. It also says what to cut if you get fewer than seven days. The night-before one-pager is for the last hour only.
Every number here is real This sub-course quotes measurements, not opinions. All of them were run on Node v24.14.0 on 2026-08-28. Nothing was invented to make a point. If you quote one in an interview, say the version — it shows the number is yours.

Day 2 · Async under pressure

Nobody asks whether you can write await. They ask what happens when it fails.

Lesson 4

The async ladder

Callbacks, promises, await. What each rung fixed, what it cost, and why await does not block the thread.

Async errors and the combinators

The missing await that try/catch cannot catch. Four combinators, and why unbounded Promise.all kills a service.
