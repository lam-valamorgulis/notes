Glossary

Every term this course uses, in plain words, with its source. If a Lesson and this page disagree, this page wins.

How to use it. Two columns: the term, and what it means in plain words. The third column says where it comes from. A term marked ours is this course's own name for something real that has no agreed name. Do not use those in an interview without explaining them.

The shape of a startup

TermIn plain wordsSource
Startup A temporary organisation that is searching for a business model it can repeat and grow. It does not have one yet. Steve Blank
Company A permanent organisation that executes a business model it already has. The opposite job to a startup. Steve Blank
Search mode / execute mode The two states. In search you buy answers. In execute you deliver a plan. Running execute-mode process while still searching is the classic failure. Steve Blank
Burn rate How much money the company spends in a month, after any income. Standard usage
Runway How many months of life are left. cash / burn rate. This is the timer that sets every decision. Standard usage
The guess ours The one belief the whole company depends on. Written as: "we believe [who] will change [what they do today] to get [what benefit]". Elsewhere it is called the riskiest assumption. This course
An answer ours A fact about the guess that you did not have last month. The real output of a month in search mode. Features are how you buy one. This course
Product-market fit Being in a good market with a product that market wants. Named by Andy Rachleff, not by Marc Andreessen — Andreessen made it famous and credited Rachleff. a16z, Wikipedia
Pivot Changing the guess on purpose while keeping what you learned. Not the same as giving up, and not the same as drifting. Standard usage

The role

TermIn plain wordsSource
Founding engineer The first person accountable for the company being able to act: turning a vague idea into a real thing a stranger can use. That one-line version is ours — the sources say "takes full technical ownership of building the product". Not a legal or standard title either; it means different things at different companies. FCTO, Pragmatic Engineer
Equity A share of the company, given instead of cash. The sources disagree. Underdog.io reports 0.1%–2%, median near 0.33%. FCTO quotes 1%–3% pre-seed, 0.5%–1.5% seed, 0.25%–0.75% Series A. The stage moves it more than your negotiation does. Underdog.io
Vesting You earn your equity slowly over time, usually four years, instead of getting it all on day one. Standard usage
Cliff The date before which you earn nothing at all. Usually twelve months. Leave at month eleven and you leave with zero. Standard usage
Dilution Your percentage gets smaller each time the company sells new shares to raise money. Normal, and worth understanding before you sign. Standard usage

Finding the problem

TermIn plain wordsSource
The Mom Test A way to ask questions so that even your mother could not give you a false yes. Three rules: talk about their life not your idea; ask about specific past events not the future; talk less and listen more. Rob Fitzpatrick
Customer interview A conversation whose purpose is to learn, not to sell. If you described your product, it was a pitch, not an interview. YC
Compliment (as a warning) "That sounds great" is not data. It is politeness. Only past behaviour, money, and time are data. The Mom Test
Switching cost What it costs someone to change how they work: learning time, risk, explaining it to a colleague, admitting the old way was bad. A sale needs pain > switching cost. Standard usage; the one-line condition is ours
Evidence ladder ours Six rungs, ranked by what an answer cost the person to give: a compliment, a guess about the future, a feature request, showing you their workaround, a referral, money. Rungs ours; ranking-by-cost is Fitzpatrick's
The kill rule ours Written down before you start: the exact evidence that would make you stop. Without it, the runway decides for you. Elsewhere this is called kill criteria. Name ours; the idea is Annie Duke, Quit (2022) — "states and dates"

Building the smallest real thing

TermIn plain wordsSource
MVP
minimum viable product		 The smallest version that is still worth using once, by someone who is not your friend. Not a demo, not a prototype. ours — Lesson B2 says the usual definition is wrong before giving this one. This course, built on YC, Michael Seibel
Appetite How much time this work is worth, decided before you design it. The opposite of an estimate: you fix the time and vary the scope. Shape Up
Shaping Roughing out a solution before anyone commits to it — enough detail to judge the risk, not enough to be a spec. Shape Up
Pitch The one-page document you bet on. It names the problem, the appetite, the solution, the rabbit holes, and what is deliberately not included. Shape Up
Rabbit hole A part of the work that could quietly eat all the time. You name these in the pitch so nobody discovers one in week five. Shape Up
Betting table The meeting where pitches are chosen for the next cycle. There is no backlog: what is not chosen is simply not done. Shape Up
Boring technology Tools whose failure modes you already understand. That is the real argument, not age or fashion. Dan McKinley
Innovation token You get about three novel choices in total. Every unfamiliar tool spends one. Spend them where they win the company something. Dan McKinley
Walking skeleton A tiny version of the system that does one small job end to end, through all the real pieces. Thin, ugly, deployed. The architecture and the features then grow together. Alistair Cockburn
Quality boundary ours The line between "go fast here" and "be careful here". Fast wherever a mistake can be undone. Careful wherever it cannot: lost data, lost money, lost trust. This course

Users, money, staying alive

TermIn plain wordsSource
Do things that don't scale Recruit your first users by hand, one at a time. It cannot last, and that is fine. The unscalable work is research, not waste. Paul Graham
One Metric That Matters
OMTM		 The single number worth watching at your current stage. Everything else waits. Watching thirty numbers is the same as watching none. Lean Analytics
The five stages Empathy, stickiness, virality, revenue, scale. Each stage has its own OMTM. Chasing a later stage's metric early is a common trap. Lean Analytics
Retention Do they come back without being asked? The hardest number to fake, and the one that decides whether anything else is worth doing. Standard usage
Churn The share of customers who leave in a period. The mirror of retention. Standard usage
Cohort A group of users who joined in the same period, measured over time. Stops new sign-ups hiding the fact that older users left. Lean Analytics
Value metric The thing you charge per, chosen so it grows as the customer's value grows: a seat, a row, an order. Getting this right matters more than the number. Patrick Campbell
Stickiness The stage name for "do people come back?". The number underneath it is retention. Two words, one idea. Lean Analytics
Vanity metric A number chosen because it always rises. Total sign-ups, total downloads. It hides a fall, because the news is only in its slope. Lean Analytics
Squeeze-toy effect Push hard on one metric and another gets worse. That is not failure — the one that got worse tells you the next thing to work on. Short name ours; idea from Lean Analytics
Show HN A post on Hacker News showing something you made. Free, harsh, and the fastest real test of whether your one-sentence pitch makes sense. Hacker News

Running it alone

TermIn plain wordsSource
Restore drill Actually putting a backup back, into a scratch database, on purpose, on a calm day — and timing it. Until you have done this you do not have a backup. standard practice
RPO
recovery point objective		 How much recent work you can afford to lose. "Up to 24 hours" means a nightly backup is enough. Standard industry term
RTO
recovery time objective		 How long you can be down while putting it back. "Up to 4 hours" means a manual restore is enough. Standard industry term
3-2-1 Three copies, on two kinds of storage, one of them off-site. The newer 3-2-1-1-0 adds one copy nothing can change, and zero errors on a verified restore. standard rule
Panic note ours One paragraph on your phone: how to check it is up, where the logs are, how to roll back, how to restore. Its job is to slow you down for thirty seconds before you type under pressure. This course

Words to avoid saying without the plain version

Jargon check These are all real terms. Used alone, they signal that you read about the job instead of doing it. Always follow them with the plain sentence. lean, iterate, MVP, product-market fit, validate, de-risk, north star metric, growth hacking, disruptive.

Plain version, for example: instead of "we validated the MVP", say "we built the smallest version and eleven of the fifteen people we gave it to used it again the next week."

All links checked: 2026-08-23. Terms marked ours are this course's own names, invented because the real thing had no agreed name. Everything else is sourced.
