Tax: what Shopify does, and where you must not guess

The one area of the platform where most of your job is knowing not to touch it.

Read this first Nothing on this page is tax advice, and you must never give any. Tax is a legal and accounting matter and the merchant's accountant owns it. Your job is the platform mechanics and the sentence "that's a question for your accountant" — said without embarrassment, every time.
Your win today. You know exactly which tax questions are yours, which are the merchant's, and the three platform facts that most often catch developers out.

How it works

Derive it: why is tax not like shipping?

Shipping is a commercial decision — the merchant can charge whatever they like, and if the number is wrong they lose a little margin. Tax is a legal obligation to a government, calculated from rules you did not write, and getting it wrong is not a bug report — it is a liability.

So the correct instinct is the opposite of everywhere else in this Course. Elsewhere, a good developer takes ownership. Here, a good developer takes the platform's answer and refuses to compute anything.

YoursNot yours
Making sure Shopify's calculated tax reaches other systems intactWhether the rate is correct
Not overwriting or recalculating itWhere the merchant must register

Fact 1 · Tax is calculated at checkout, and the Cart API no longer even offers it

As of API version 2025-01, the tax and duty fields are deprecated in the Storefront Cart API. Shopify's stated reason is exact and worth quoting: tax and duties "are calculated at Checkout where they can be finalized with the complete context of the buyer's information, ensuring greater accuracy."

So the platform has removed your ability to show a tax figure in the cart, deliberately. That means:

product page → no tax context. you do not know where they are. cart → tax fields DEPRECATED. do not try. checkout → tax calculated, with the full address and customer context order → the finalised amounts. THIS is what you sync onward.

Any client request for "show tax in the cart" is a request to display a number the platform declines to guarantee. The honest answer is that you can show an estimate labelled as an estimate, or wait for checkout — and the second is what Shopify wants.

Fact 2 · Exemption is a per-jurisdiction value, not a checkbox

A customer is not simply "tax exempt". Shopify models exemption as a long enumeration of specific, jurisdiction-scoped entitlements — for example a reseller exemption in one US state, a Canadian provincial exemption, or the EU reverse-charge rule for cross-border purchases within the EU and from the EU to the UK.

The practical consequence: never model exemption as a boolean in your own system. A customer can be exempt from one tax in one place and fully liable elsewhere. Carry the specific values, or carry nothing and read them from Shopify when you need them.

Fact 3 · You cannot build a tax calculation app

Shopify's Tax Platform is available by invitation only, to selected tax software companies. So "we'll integrate our own tax engine" is not a scoping question — it is not available to you.

What that leaves is: use Shopify's own tax calculation, or use one of the invited partner apps. Knowing this in the first meeting saves a fortnight of investigation.

What actually reaches your integration

For a sync, tax is read-only output. It appears on the order, already calculated, and your only jobs are to carry it faithfully and never to recompute it.

DoNever
Read the tax lines from the order and pass them onRecalculate tax from a rate you looked up
Keep tax as integer minor units of the order's currencySum floats, or convert currencies
Preserve whether prices were tax-inclusiveAssume one convention. It differs by market
On a migrated order, send the historical tax explicitlyLet a default recalculate it at today's rates

That last row is the same trap as P6.6's omitted line price, and it is worse: an imported order silently taxed at today's rules misstates a historical financial record.

Answer before reading on A client asks you to show the tax-inclusive price on the product page, "so European customers see the real price". What is true, what is not, and what do you say?

What is true: prices can be presented tax-inclusively, and per-market pricing is a real platform feature — that part is legitimate and is a Markets question, not a tax-calculation one.

What is not: you cannot compute the tax yourself to display it. On a product page you do not know the customer's address, and the Cart API's tax fields are deprecated precisely because tax needs the full checkout context. So a number you calculate is a number you are guessing.

What you say: "We can present prices as tax-inclusive per market, which is a Shopify setting rather than something I calculate. What I won't do is work out a tax figure myself and display it — I'd be guessing without the customer's address, and if it's wrong it's your liability, not a display bug. Your accountant should confirm which markets you present inclusively."

That answer gives them most of what they wanted, refuses the part that creates liability, and routes the remaining question to the person who owns it.

Where it hurts

Costs money — recalculating tax anywhere You looked up a rate and applied it. It is wrong for some jurisdiction, product type or exemption you did not know about, and now the merchant has under- or over-collected tax across every affected order. Read Shopify's number; never derive one.
Costs money — a migrated order taxed at today's rules Omit the historical tax on an imported order and it is recalculated by current rules. Counts match, line items match, and the financial history is wrong in a way every automated check passes.
Fails with no error — exemption as a boolean Exemption is jurisdiction-scoped. A customer exempt in one state modelled as globally exempt stops being charged tax everywhere, silently, and it will not surface until an audit.
Fails with no error — assuming one tax-inclusive convention Whether prices include tax can differ by market. Code that assumes one answer produces figures that are right in one country and wrong in another, with no error anywhere.
Promising tax in the cart The Storefront Cart API's tax and duty fields are deprecated as of 2025-01. Building on them is building on something already withdrawn.
Scoping a custom tax engine integration The Tax Platform is invitation-only. Discovering that in week three, after it is in a proposal, is an avoidable and embarrassing conversation.
Judgement, not documentation — never answer a tax question, even when you know You will sometimes know the answer. Say it anyway: "that's one for your accountant." An answer from you creates reliance, and if it is wrong the liability is real and it is theirs — which makes it a relationship problem as well as a legal one. There is no upside to being right about this informally.

What you say to a client/PM

Any tax question at all That's one for your accountant rather than me. What I can tell you is the mechanical part: Shopify calculates the tax at checkout, once it knows the customer's address, and I make sure that number reaches your other systems unchanged. What I never do is work tax out myself — if I did and it were wrong, that's your liability. Declines cleanly, states what you do own, and gives the reason in terms of their risk rather than your caution. Nobody pushes back on this.
When asked to show tax earlier in the journey Shopify deliberately removed the tax fields from the cart, because tax needs the full address to be right. So anything I showed before checkout would be an estimate, and I'd want it labelled as one. If it's about European customers seeing inclusive prices, that's a different and better-supported thing — a per-market price presentation setting rather than a calculation. Explains the platform's reasoning, offers the legitimate alternative, and separates "display convention" from "calculation" — which is the distinction that matters.
When they want their own tax engine I checked, and that route isn't open to us — Shopify's tax platform is invitation-only for tax software companies. So the options are Shopify's own tax calculation, or one of the tax apps they've already approved. Worth your accountant picking between those two rather than me. A specific, checkable reason for a no, and it hands the remaining choice to the right person.

Remember this

  • Never calculate tax. Never advise on tax. "That's one for your accountant" — every time.
  • Tax is finalised at checkout, with the full buyer context. Not on the product page, not in the cart.
  • Storefront Cart API tax and duty fields are deprecated as of 2025-01. Do not build on them.
  • Exemption is jurisdiction-scoped, not a boolean. Carry the specific values or none.
  • The Tax Platform is invitation-only. A custom tax engine is not available to you.
  • For a sync, tax is read-only output. Carry it faithfully as integer minor units.
  • On a migrated order, send historical tax explicitly, or today's rules rewrite the past.

If you remember one thing: this is the one place where taking ownership is the wrong instinct — read the platform's number, pass it on unchanged, and route every judgement to the accountant.
