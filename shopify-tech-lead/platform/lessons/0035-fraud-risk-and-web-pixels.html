Track 1 · P10 · Lesson 4 · depth

Fraud, risk and web pixels

Two things you observe rather than own — and both have a boundary worth knowing precisely.

Your win today. You know where the fraud boundary sits, and you know the sanctioned way to track customer behaviour now that injecting scripts into checkout is over.

How it works

Part 1 · Fraud and risk: whose problem is it?

Mostly not yours — and knowing exactly where the line falls is the skill, because you will be asked.

SituationWhose
An order looks suspiciousThe merchant's decision, informed by Shopify's own risk assessment
A chargeback arrivesThe merchant's, with their gateway
Whether to cancel and refundThe merchant's. Never automate it without a written rule
Passing a risk signal to their ERP or ops toolYours. Read it, carry it, do not interpret it
Running a payments appYours, contractually — see below
The one case where fraud is genuinely your problem If you build a payments app extension, the requirements are explicit: partners are responsible for monitoring and managing risk and fraud. And an "unreasonably high percentage" of fraudulent or high-risk payments — determined at Shopify's sole discretion — can get your app removed from the public gateway list or your access to the payments ecosystem restricted.

So in payments, fraud stops being the merchant's problem and becomes a condition of your continued participation. That is verified in P8.2, and it is the reason a payments app is a company's product rather than a feature.

The rule for integrations

Treat risk exactly like tax in P7.3: read the platform's assessment, carry it faithfully, and never compute your own.

And never auto-cancel on it. A false positive cancels a real customer's order, and the customer's experience is that your client's shop accused them of fraud. If the client wants automation, get a written threshold and put everything above it in a queue for a person — the same control pattern as automatic refunds in P8.1.

Part 2 · Web pixels: the sanctioned replacement

Derive why they exist. Merchants want to track behaviour — page views, add-to-cart, purchases — for advertising and analytics. The old way was injecting a script tag into checkout. Shopify closed that, for the reasons in P5.2: arbitrary code between a customer and their card details is unauditable and unbounded.

Web pixel extensions are the replacement, and they are available on all plans except Shopify Starter — no Plus gate.

OLD NEW a script tag in checkout a web pixel extension arbitrary code a sandboxed, declared subscriber no consent model integrated with customer privacy sunset: the supported path 28 Aug 2025 Plus 26 Aug 2026 non-Plus

The dates, one more time, because this one is live

Script tags on the Thank you and Order status pages are sunset: 28 August 2025 for Plus stores, 26 August 2026 for non-Plus stores. So on any store you inherit today, old tracking there has already stopped or is about to.

That makes it a performance finding and a functionality finding at once — dead scripts may still download while doing nothing, and the tracking the client believes they have does not exist. Run the admin's checkout customisation report, as P5.2 says.

Consent is part of the mechanism, not an add-on

Tracking is privacy work. From A1.4: Shopify applies the same privacy rights to all personal data regardless of where the person is, not only in GDPR or CPRA territories. And customer-facing extensions can declare a collect_buyer_consent.customer_privacy capability, as seen in P9.3.

So the honest framing for a client: consent is not a checkbox you add at the end. It is part of how the tracking is allowed to work at all.

Answer before reading on A client asks you to automatically cancel and refund any order Shopify flags as high risk. What do you say?

You do not refuse, and you do not just build it. Both of those are wrong here.

What you say is the cost: "I can. Before we switch it on — a false positive cancels a real customer's order and refunds them, and from their side your shop has just accused them of fraud. That's a customer you probably don't get back. I'd rather flag them into a queue for someone to glance at, which takes seconds per order. If you do want it automatic, give me a value threshold so it only fires on orders where the loss would outweigh the risk of being wrong."

Three moves in one answer: yes, the specific consequence in the client's terms, and two options with a recommendation. And notice the fallback is not "don't" — it is a smaller, safer version of what they asked for, exactly as R3.4 teaches.

Where it hurts

Costs money — auto-cancelling on a risk signal A false positive cancels a genuine order and refunds a real customer. They experience being accused of fraud, and you lose them permanently. Queue it for a human, or set a written threshold.
Costs money — computing your own risk score Same rule as tax. Read Shopify's assessment; never derive one. A homemade score is a liability wearing a number.
Costs money — owning fraud without realising it Build a payments app and the fraud rate becomes your contractual problem, judged at Shopify's sole discretion. A business risk sitting inside a technical decision.
Fails with no error — tracking that stopped at a sunset date Script tags on Thank you and Order status are sunset. The scripts may still download and do nothing, so the client believes they have attribution data they do not have. Nothing errors, and the reports simply go quiet.
Treating consent as a later task Shopify applies the same privacy rights to all personal data regardless of location. Consent is part of how tracking is permitted, not a compliance step at the end.
Judgement, not documentation — never explain a risk score to a customer A merchant will occasionally ask you to tell a customer why they were flagged. Do not. Explaining the signals teaches someone how to evade them, and it is not your call to make on the merchant's behalf. Route it to the merchant, exactly as you would a tax question.

What you say to a client/PM

Where the fraud line sits Fraud decisions are yours rather than mine, and I'd keep it that way — Shopify gives you a risk assessment, and I'll make sure that flag reaches your operations tool so nobody has to go looking. What I won't do is invent my own score or cancel orders automatically. If you want automation, give me a rule in writing and I'll build exactly that. Draws the boundary, says what you will do, and offers automation on their explicit terms rather than refusing it.
On an inherited store's tracking Worth checking before you trust your numbers. Shopify retired the old way of adding tracking to the order confirmation page — for stores on your plan that was August 2026. So any tracking there has probably stopped, and your reports may be missing conversions without anything looking broken. There's a report in your admin that lists what you have; can I run it? Frames it as protecting the numbers they already rely on, which gets attention that "deprecated API" never would.

Remember this

  • Fraud is the merchant's decision. You carry the signal; you never compute one.
  • Never auto-cancel on risk. A false positive costs a real customer permanently.
  • Unless you build a payments app — then the fraud rate is contractually yours, at Shopify's sole discretion.
  • Web pixels are the sanctioned tracking path, on all plans except Starter. No Plus gate.
  • Script tags on Thank you / Order status are sunset: 28 Aug 2025 Plus, 26 Aug 2026 non-Plus.
  • So inherited tracking has probably already stopped — a functionality bug that looks like quiet reports.
  • Consent is part of the mechanism. Shopify applies the same rights to all personal data, everywhere.

If you remember one thing: carry the risk signal, never interpret it — and never let a false positive cancel a real customer's order.
