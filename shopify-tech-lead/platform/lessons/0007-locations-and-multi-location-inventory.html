Locations and multi-location inventory

Where stock actually lives, and why the count of locations disagrees with the list.

Your win today. You can audit a store's locations correctly — including the ones a default query hides from you.

How it works

A Location is not always a shop

A Location is any place where inventory is managed: retail shops, warehouses, popups, dropshippers. Two things follow that people miss.

First, an active location can fulfil online orders when configured with shipping rates, local pickup, or local delivery. So "active" and "can be shipped from" are separate properties — a location can be active and fulfil nothing.

Second, a FulfillmentService — a third-party warehouse app — automatically gets its own Location when created. So the location list mixes real places with apps, and a store locator built naively from it will offer customers a warehouse operated by software.

The trap: the count and the list disagree

Verified on a live Plus store during this Course:

locationsCount → 4 (EXACT) locations(first: 50) → 3 locations(first: 50, includeInactive: true) → 4 ← there it is the missing one was INACTIVE ── and it still had fulfillsOnlineOrders: true

The count includes inactive locations; the default connection excludes them. So code that sizes an array from the count and fills it from the list is off by one, silently.

Worse for a sync: an inactive location can still hold inventory. If your location map only knows the active ones, stock sitting at the inactive one is invisible to you and real to Shopify's totals. Your reconciliation then reports a difference it cannot explain.

One of four locations ships inventory. Three fulfil online orders, including the inactive one. Nothing here is a mistake by the merchant — it is what a real retail estate looks like. The lesson is that "how many locations?" is a useless question and "which location can do what?" is the useful one.

Answer before reading on Your sync builds a location map by looping locations(first: 50). Reconciliation later reports that total stock in Shopify exceeds the total you sent. Give the most likely cause.

Stock at a location your map does not contain — almost certainly an inactive one, excluded by the default connection. Shopify's totals include it; your sum does not. The give-away is that the difference is positive and stable: you are not missing updates, you are missing a place. Add includeInactive: true and decide deliberately whether that location is in scope, rather than letting a query default decide for you.

Where it hurts

Fails with no error — inactive locations excluded by default The count says 4, the list gives 3, and nothing warns you. Every total built from the list is quietly short.
Fails with no error — writing to an InventoryLevel that does not exist A variant not stocked at a location has no level there. Writing a quantity to a non-existent level is not the same as setting it to zero, and the failure is easy to swallow in a batch.
Treating "active" as "can ship" They are separate properties. A location can be active and fulfil nothing, or inactive and still be flagged for online orders. Read the specific capability, not the status.
A store locator built from the location list It will include warehouses and app-created fulfillment-service locations. Customers get offered places they cannot visit. Filter by what each location actually is.
Judgement, not documentation — deactivating a location moves stock Deactivating is not a display setting; it has consequences for the inventory held there. Never script it as part of a routine sync. Treat activation state as merchant-owned and read-only from your side.

What you say to a client/PM

During discovery Can I get a list of your physical locations, and separately what Shopify shows? They're often different — warehouses appear, apps create their own, and locations that were closed are usually still there. I need to know which ones actually hold stock you'd sell online, because that's the set I'll sync. Pre-empts a discrepancy the client does not know exists, and frames it as normal rather than as their mistake.
When totals do not match Shopify's total is higher than ours by a consistent amount, which points at a location we're not including rather than updates we're missing. There's a location in your account that's marked inactive but still holds stock. Do you want it in scope or excluded? Either is fine — I just need it to be a decision rather than an accident. Distinguishes a missing place from missing updates, which is the diagnosis that saves a day, and asks for a decision instead of guessing.

Remember this

  • A Location is any place inventory is managed — shops, warehouses, popups, dropshippers.
  • FulfillmentServices create their own Locations. Not every location is visitable.
  • locationsCount includes inactive; locations() excludes them. Use includeInactive: true.
  • An inactive location can still hold stock, and still be flagged for online orders.
  • "Active" and "can ship" are different properties. Read the capability, not the status.
  • A missing InventoryLevel is absence, not zero, and cannot be written to.
  • Never script location activation. It has inventory consequences; treat it as merchant-owned.

If you remember one thing: ask which location can do what, never how many there are.
