Payments, and what you must never touch

Three minutes to resolve, five days to settle, and a contract about your own pricing.

Your win today. You can tell a client whether their payment request is a configuration job, an app install, or a regulated build they should not attempt — and you know the three timing rules that catch everyone who does attempt it.

How it works

Derive it: why is payments the hardest thing in this Course?

It is the only area where every one of the five forces from the territory map applies at once:

1. not yours Shopify can remove your extension from the gateway list 2. money it IS the money. not a record of it 3. customer present a person is mid-purchase, watching 4. second system a bank, a processor, a fraud engine 5. merchant edits they can switch gateway on Tuesday

So the correct default is: do not build this. Almost every client request that sounds like payments is actually configuration or an existing app.

The lifecycle, and the ordering rule

Three extension types exist — offsite, credit card, and alternative — and they share one shape:

1. Shopify POSTs a payment session to you 2. you take payment details / redirect the customer (3-D Secure: paymentSessionConfirm, in an iFrame) 3. Shopify POSTs the CONFIRMATION RESULT to your confirm-session URL ── you MUST return 2xx. failure is retried; if it keeps failing, the CUSTOMER has to start their payment again 4. you finish it: paymentSessionResolve | Reject | Pending
The ordering rule people trip on Shopify fails your Resolve and Reject calls if it has not yet sent the confirmation result to your confirm-session URL. So you cannot resolve early, however confident you are. Step 4 strictly follows step 3.

The three clocks

ClockLimitWhat happens if you miss it
Resolve or reject after confirmation3 minutesShopify re-runs the confirmation process on resolve. It no longer guarantees the confirmation result — and may issue a refund or void after you resolve
Settlement5 daysA stated requirement of participating at all
Merchant termination notice7 daysYou must allow it, with no penalty

Read the first row twice. A slow resolve does not just delay the payment — it can produce a refund of a payment you thought you had taken. That is a money-losing outcome caused purely by latency, which is force 3 at its most expensive.

The requirements are commercial, not just technical

This is the part almost no developer knows, and it is why row four above is a company's product. Participating in the payments ecosystem means agreeing to:

RequirementDetail
SecuritySecurely collect and store payment information, per applicable law, PCI requirements and market regulations
SettlementSettle transactions within five days
Risk and fraudYou monitor and manage it. An unreasonably high fraud rate — at Shopify's sole discretion — can get you removed
Transparent pricingNo low promotional rates that later rise
NamingYou may not describe any fee as a "Shopify fee" on a merchant invoice
ExitMerchants may terminate with 7 days' notice, no penalty or fine

And the sanction, stated plainly: Shopify can remove your extension from the public gateway list, suspend or terminate your access to the payments ecosystem, "or take any other action deemed necessary".

Verified 2026-08-23: the session → confirm → resolve/reject flow, that Shopify must receive a 2xx on the confirmation callback and that repeated failure means the customer retries their payment, that Resolve/Reject fail before a confirmation result has been sent, the 3-minute resolve window with re-run confirmation and possible refund or void, and the CONFIRMATION_REJECTED reason code — About payment processing and Build an offsite payments extension. What you touch instead, most of the time

For integration work, payments is mostly read-only output, like tax:

DoNever
Read the transactions on an order and pass them onReconstruct what was charged from line items
Treat gateway names and references as opaque stringsParse or infer meaning from them
Use Functions to reorder or hide payment methodsTry to influence which one succeeds
Carry the payment reference to the ERP for reconciliationStore card details anywhere, ever
Answer before reading on You build a payments extension. Your processor is healthy but slow today — confirmations take about four minutes to come back from them. Every payment eventually succeeds. What does the merchant see?

Payments that succeed and then get refunded. Because you resolve after the 3-minute window, Shopify re-runs its confirmation process, no longer guarantees the earlier confirmation result, and may issue a refund or void after you have resolved. Your processor took the money; Shopify gave it back.

Nothing errored anywhere. Your logs show successful resolves. The processor shows successful captures. The merchant sees revenue arriving and then leaving, with no failure to point at.

The design consequence is the same as the carrier-rate lesson in P7.2: you cannot put a slow third party inside a hard deadline you do not control. If your processor cannot confirm inside three minutes, you resolve as Pending and finish it out of band — you do not wait.

Where it hurts

Costs money — resolving after 3 minutes Shopify re-runs confirmation and may refund or void a payment you already took. Money out, no error anywhere. Use paymentSessionPending rather than waiting.
Costs money — a non-2xx on the confirmation callback It is retried, and if it keeps failing the customer has to start their payment again. That is an abandoned checkout caused by your endpoint, at the last possible step.
Fails with no error — resolving before confirmation arrives Shopify fails the call. Code that assumes success and moves on marks the order paid internally while Shopify does not.
Costs money — building a payments app as a "feature" It carries PCI obligations, a 5-day settlement duty, fraud responsibility, pricing rules, and a 7-day merchant exit right. Scoping it as an integration task is a category error with legal consequences.
Owning the fraud rate without knowing it Partners monitor and manage risk. An unreasonably high fraud rate — judged at Shopify's sole discretion — can remove you from the gateway list. That is a business risk sitting inside a technical decision.
Fails with no error — parsing gateway references Gateway names and transaction references are opaque and vendor-specific. Code that infers meaning from their shape breaks silently when the merchant switches processor.
The 3-D Secure browsing context Documented precisely: navigate the redirect using the parent browsing context, not the top-level context directly. Get it wrong and the authentication breaks inside the iFrame.
Judgement, not documentation — never store a card number, for any reason Someone will eventually ask you to keep card details "so we can charge them later". The answer is no, and it is not a preference. Shopify and the gateway hold that; you hold a token at most. There is no version of this request you should accommodate.

What you say to a client/PM

When a client says "we need a payments integration" Let me find out which of three things you mean. If you want to accept cards, that's a settings job and I'm not needed. If your provider already has a Shopify app, we install it. If you want to offer your own payment method to other Shopify merchants, that's a regulated build with PCI obligations, a five-day settlement duty and fraud responsibility — that's a product, not a feature. Which is it? Three options, and two of them are free. Clients regularly discover they wanted the free one.
When asked to hide a payment method for some customers That one's genuinely doable and reasonably small — Shopify has a payment customisation function for reordering and hiding methods, and it doesn't need Plus. What I can't do is influence which payment succeeds; that's between the customer, their bank and the gateway. Says yes to the real request, and draws the boundary at the thing that is not yours.
When someone asks you to store card details No — and not because of a preference. Card data carries PCI obligations that neither of us wants to take on, and Shopify and your gateway already hold it. If the goal is charging a customer again later, that's a saved payment method held by the gateway, and I can work with the token rather than the card. Refuses in one word, gives the reason, and immediately offers the legitimate route to what they actually wanted.

Remember this

  • Payments is the only area where all five forces apply at once. Default to not building it.
  • Sort the request: configuration · install an app · a payment customisation Function · a regulated build.
  • Resolve or reject within 3 minutes of the confirmation callback, or Shopify may refund or void what you took.
  • Return 2xx on the confirmation callback. Repeated failure means the customer restarts their payment.
  • You cannot resolve before the confirmation result arrives. Shopify fails the call.
  • The requirements are commercial: PCI, 5-day settlement, your fraud rate, pricing rules, no "Shopify fee" naming, 7-day merchant exit.
  • Never store a card number. Not a preference — a boundary.

If you remember one thing: a slow resolve does not delay a payment, it can reverse one — so never put a third party inside a three-minute deadline you do not control.
