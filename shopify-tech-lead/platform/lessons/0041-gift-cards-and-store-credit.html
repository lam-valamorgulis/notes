Gift cards and store credit

Money that exists outside the payment gateway — so it reconciles differently from every other total.

Your win today. You know why a gift card is a promise rather than a payment, and you can read a store credit balance as a ledger rather than a number.

How it works

Derive it: where does this money live?

Normally money arrives from outside: a customer's card, through a gateway, into the merchant's account. Every total you reconcile assumes that shape.

A gift card breaks it. Somebody paid real money earlier, possibly months ago, possibly a different person. What exists now is a promise the shop owes. When it is spent, no money moves at all — the shop is settling a debt it already took payment for.

So a gift card sale and a gift card redemption are two different financial events at two different times, and neither looks like a normal payment. That is the whole reason this needs its own Lesson: revenue and bank figures diverge, correctly, and reconciliation that assumes they match will report a fault that is not there.

NORMAL card -> gateway -> merchant bank revenue and money move together GIFT CARD Day 1: card -> gateway -> bank, and a PROMISE is created Day 90: promise spent, NO money moves revenue is recognised with no bank movement

Store credit is a ledger, not a balance

Store credit is the same idea in a different shape, and Shopify exposes it as a list of transactions rather than a single figure. You read a storeCreditAccount and page through its transactions:

query storeCreditAccount($accountId: ID!, $first: Int!) {
  storeCreditAccount(id: $accountId) {
    id
    transactions(first: $first, sortKey: CREATED_AT, reverse: true) {
      edges { node {
        amount { amount currencyCode }
        balanceAfterTransaction { amount currencyCode }
        createdAt
        ... on StoreCreditAccountCreditTransaction {
          id expiresAt remainingAmount { amount currencyCode }
        }
        ... on StoreCreditAccountDebitTransaction { id }
        ... on StoreCreditAccountDebitRevertTransaction { id debitTransaction { id } }
        ... on StoreCreditAccountExpirationTransaction { creditTransaction { id } }
      } }
    }
  }
}

Read the four transaction types, because they tell you what can happen:

TypeMeaningThe bit that matters
CreditTransactionCredit addedCarries expiresAt and remainingAmount
DebitTransactionCredit spent—
DebitRevertTransactionA spend was reversedPoints at the original debitTransaction
ExpirationTransactionCredit expired unusedPoints at the creditTransaction that expired

Two consequences fall straight out. Credit expires — so a balance is only true as of a moment, and a cached one can go wrong without anything happening. And each credit has its own remaining amount, so an account balance is a sum over separately expiring pieces, not one pot.

Which is why you read the ledger. Asking "what is the balance?" throws away the information you need to answer "and how much of it disappears next month?"

The dev-store problem

From A2.5: development stores do not support store credit or gift cards. So this is one of the few areas you cannot fully rehearse on a Dev store. Plan the demo around it rather than spending an afternoon debugging a documented limitation.

Answer before reading on A finance team says January revenue is higher than the money that reached the bank, and nothing is broken. Give the gift-card explanation.

Gift cards sold in December brought money into the bank in December. Spending them in January recognises revenue with no bank movement at all, because the shop is settling a promise it was already paid for. So January shows revenue above receipts by exactly the value of vouchers redeemed. It is not a discrepancy — it is two events in two months, and any reconciliation that expects revenue to equal receipts will report it as a fault forever.

Where it hurts

Costs money — treating a redemption as a payment No money moves when a gift card is spent. Code that records it as an incoming payment inflates receipts, and the error compounds every month until somebody audits it.
Fails with no error — caching a store credit balance Credit expires. A cached balance goes wrong on its own, with no event, no webhook and nothing to react to. Read the ledger when you need the number.
Fails with no error — treating the balance as one pot Each credit carries its own expiresAt and remainingAmount. A balance made of two credits expiring in different months is not the same as one lump, and a single figure cannot express that.
Costs money — reconciling revenue against receipts With gift cards in play they are supposed to differ. A reconciliation that treats the gap as a fault will cry wolf until it is switched off — and then it is not watching the real faults either.
Costs money — promising a gift-card migration From P6.5: gift cards never move. They are outstanding promises on the old platform. Committing to carry them across is committing to something impossible, and it is normally discovered late.
Missing the revert transaction A spend can be reversed, and it appears as its own transaction pointing back at the original. Code that only understands credits and debits will double-count a reversal.
Testing it on a Dev store Development stores do not support store credit or gift cards. Discovering that halfway through a demo build is an afternoon lost to a documented limitation.
Judgement, not documentation — expiry is a legal question, not a setting Whether and when store credit may expire is regulated differently in different places, and the rules are not yours to interpret. Same rule as tax in P7.3: carry the platform's value, and send the question to the client's accountant.

Say it out loud

Explain this to a beginner

A customer pays with a gift card. Explain why the shop's revenue goes up and the money in its bank account does not change at all — and why that is correct.

Say it out loud. Then read on.

Now compare with a plain-words answer

Because the shop was already paid, months ago, by somebody else.

Think about what happens when a gift card is bought. Somebody hands over real money. That money goes into the bank straight away. But the shop has not sold anything yet — it has taken money and made a promise. It now owes somebody some goods.

Months later a customer walks in and uses the card. Now the shop has sold something, so revenue goes up. But no money arrives, because the money arrived in December. The shop is just settling the promise it made.

So the money and the sale happened in different months. And that is not a mistake to be corrected — it is genuinely what happened, and any accounting that insists revenue must equal money received will report it as broken forever.

Which is why this needs handling explicitly rather than being treated as a payment method. A card payment is money arriving. A gift card is a promise being paid off.

If you used any of these words, try again deferred revenue · liability account · accrual · settlement · payment instrument · breakage. "The shop was already paid, months ago, by somebody else" is the explanation. If you needed the word "liability" to say it, try again without it — the idea is a promise the shop owes.

Build it

Task · 45 minutes a real store, read-only

Dev stores do not support this, so read a real store rather than trying to create it.

  1. Query one storeCreditAccount and page its transactions.
  2. Find a credit with an expiresAt in the future. Note its remainingAmount.
  3. Add up the remaining amounts grouped by expiry month. That is the number a finance team actually wants and almost nobody produces.
  4. Find a DebitRevertTransaction if there is one, and follow it to the debit it reverses.

Proof it worked: you can state the balance and how much of it expires next month. If you only produced a single total, you threw away the expiry information the ledger exists to carry.

Fix the reconciliation · 45 minutes your Project
  1. Take your reconciliation report and add gift-card redemptions as an explicit expected difference between revenue and receipts.
  2. Make the report show that difference as a named line, not as drift.
  3. Then seed a real fault as well, and confirm the report still catches it.

Proof it worked: the report separates expected divergence from drift, and it still goes red for the real fault. A report that cries wolf gets switched off, and then it is not watching anything.

The migration note · 10 minutes, written no store needed

Write the paragraph you would send a client who assumed gift cards migrate: they do not, what the outstanding balance is, and the three options — honour manually, issue new cards, or keep the old platform readable for a period.

Proof it worked: the note names the outstanding balance as a real number and offers choices rather than a problem. Discovering this in week nine is what makes it expensive.

Remember this

  • A gift card is a promise the shop owes, not a payment. Money arrived when it was sold.
  • Revenue and receipts are supposed to diverge when cards are redeemed. That gap is not a fault.
  • Store credit is a ledger, not a balance. Read transactions, not one number.
  • Credit expires, so a cached balance goes stale with no event to react to.
  • Each credit has its own expiresAt and remainingAmount. A balance is a sum over separately expiring pieces.
  • Four transaction types — credit, debit, debit-revert, expiration. Missing the revert double-counts.
  • Dev stores do not support gift cards or store credit, and gift cards never migrate.

If you remember one thing: this is money that moved at a different time from the sale — so never record a redemption as a payment, and never treat the revenue-versus-bank gap as drift.

Ask questions. Then "next" for payouts — where the money actually lands, and the question an accountant will ask you.
