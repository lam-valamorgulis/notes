Track 1 · P8 · Lesson 4 · core

Fulfillment orders, and being a fulfillment service

The object that represents the work of an order — and the one you cannot create.

Your win today. You can model an order as work to be done rather than a thing to be shipped, and you know which of the three fulfilment scope pairs your integration needs.

How it works

Derive it: what is an order once it has been paid for?

An order is a customer's request. It is not a shipment, and it is not a job. Somebody still has to pick the items, from somewhere, and put them in a box.

Push on "from somewhere". A cart can contain a shirt in the warehouse and a mug in a shop. Those are two different buildings, two different sets of staff, two different parcels. So the work cannot be one thing attached to the order — the work has to be described per place.

That is exactly what a FulfillmentOrder is: the items from one order that are expected to be fulfilled from the same location. One order, several fulfillment orders. And there can be more than one per order per location.

Order the customer's request └── FulfillmentOrder the WORK, at ONE location ├── FulfillmentOrderLineItem └── Fulfillment the actual parcel, with tracking Order : "they want these things" FulfillmentOrder : "somebody at this place must pick these" Fulfillment : "this parcel left, here is the tracking"

So there are three layers and they answer three different questions. People collapse them into one and then cannot express a partial shipment.

The thing you cannot do

Shopify creates fulfillment orders automatically when an order is created. It is not possible to manually create them. There is no create mutation, and looking for one is how developers lose an afternoon.

You can move them, hold them, split them, and fulfil them. You cannot bring one into existence. That is a deliberate constraint: the work is derived from the order, so inventing work that no order asked for would make the two disagree.

The statuses, and the two that surprise people

StatusWhat it means
OPENReady to be fulfilled
IN_PROGRESSBeing worked on
ON_HOLDFulfilment cannot start until the hold is released
SCHEDULEDDeferred — becomes ready after the date in fulfill_at
INCOMPLETECannot be completed as requested
CLOSEDCompleted and closed
CANCELLEDCancelled by the merchant

ON_HOLD and SCHEDULED are the two that break naive code. Both are orders that look unfulfilled and are not waiting for you. A warehouse integration that treats every non-fulfilled order as "pick this now" will ship a pre-order six weeks early and release goods a fraud team deliberately held.

Note also that the Fulfillment object has its own, different status set — SUCCESS, CANCELLED, ERROR, FAILURE — and its OPEN and PENDING values are deprecated. Two objects, two status vocabularies, similar words.

Being the warehouse: the request/accept dance

If a location is managed by a fulfillment service — a third-party warehouse, possibly you — then work is not simply assigned. It is requested, and the service can accept or reject.

merchant / order-management app │ sends a fulfilment REQUEST ▼ fulfillment service ──► ACCEPT or REJECT │ │ after ACCEPT the merchant can no longer cancel it — │ they must submit a CANCELLATION REQUEST instead ▼ service packs, then calls fulfillmentCreate │ (tracking now, or later via fulfillmentTrackingInfoUpdate) ▼ Fulfillment exists

The line worth memorising: once the service accepts, the merchant cannot cancel — only ask. That is a genuine handover of control, and it is the moment your system stops being able to change its mind.

Three scope pairs, and picking the wrong one

Scope pairGrants access to fulfillment orders at…
read/write_merchant_managed_fulfillment_ordersmerchant-managed locations
read/write_assigned_fulfillment_orderslocations your service manages — this is the fulfilment-service one
read/write_third_party_fulfillment_orderslocations managed by other fulfilment services

These are not degrees of the same permission. They are three different roles. An order-management app needs the first; a warehouse app needs the second; an app that has to see the whole picture needs the third as well. Asking for the wrong one produces an empty list rather than an error, which reads exactly like "there is no work".

Verified 2026-08-23: the FulfillmentOrderStatus values and their meanings, that Shopify creates fulfillment orders automatically and they cannot be created manually, that there can be more than one fulfillment order per order per location, the accept/reject flow and that a merchant must submit a cancellation request after acceptance, fulfillmentCreate and fulfillmentTrackingInfoUpdate, the three access-scope pairs, and that FulfillmentStatus.OPEN and PENDING are deprecated — FulfillmentOrderStatus and FulfillmentStatus.

Answer before reading on A client says "we have 40 unfulfilled orders and the warehouse says it has nothing to pick". Name three explanations using the right object names.

One: they are ON_HOLD, so the work exists and is deliberately blocked. Two: they are SCHEDULED with a future fulfill_at, so they are pre-orders and not late at all. Three: the fulfillment orders are assigned to a location the warehouse app cannot see, because the app holds the wrong scope pair and receives an empty list rather than an error. All three look identical on an "unfulfilled orders" report.

Where it hurts

Fails with no error — looking for a create mutation There is not one. Fulfillment orders are created by Shopify automatically and cannot be created manually. Code written on the assumption that you create work is code that has to be redesigned, not fixed.
Costs money — treating SCHEDULED as "late" A pre-order deferred to a future fulfill_at looks unfulfilled. Ship it now and you have dispatched something the customer expected in six weeks, possibly before you have it.
Costs money — fulfilling something ON_HOLD A hold is somebody's deliberate decision — fraud review, a payment problem, a customer request. Fulfilment cannot start until it is released, and code that works around the hold ships goods a human stopped.
Fails with no error — the wrong scope pair The three pairs are three roles, not three levels. Hold the merchant-managed one when you needed the assigned one and you get an empty list, which is indistinguishable from having no work.
Costs money — assuming you can still cancel after acceptance Once a fulfillment service accepts a request, the merchant cannot cancel it — only submit a cancellation request the service may or may not honour. Any workflow with a "cancel" button that assumes immediacy is lying to whoever presses it.
Fails with no error — one order equals one shipment A cart spanning two locations produces two fulfillment orders and can produce two parcels. Downstream systems modelling one shipment per order will silently drop the second, and only for multi-location carts.
Confusing the two status vocabularies FulfillmentOrder and Fulfillment have different status sets with overlapping words, and FulfillmentStatus.OPEN and PENDING are already deprecated. Check which object you are reading before you branch on a status name.
Judgement, not documentation — decide who may release a hold Shopify will let your app release holds. Almost no client should allow that automatically, because a hold is usually a human protecting the business from something your code cannot see. Agree in writing who is allowed to release one.

Say it out loud

Explain this to a beginner

A customer buys two things and they are in two different buildings. Explain why Shopify needs a separate object for "the work", and why you are not allowed to create one.

Say it out loud, with no object names. Then read on.

Now compare with a plain-words answer

The order says what the customer wants. It does not say who has to do anything. Somebody in the warehouse has to pick the shirt, and somebody in the shop has to pick the mug. Those are two separate jobs, for two separate sets of people, ending in two separate parcels.

If you attached the job to the order, you could only ever describe one job — so you could never express "half of this is being handled over there". So the job is a thing of its own, and there is one per place.

On not creating them: the work is derived from what was bought. Shopify works it out and makes the job records the moment the order appears. If you could invent extra work, you would be able to create a job to pick something nobody ordered — and then the order and the work would disagree, with no way to say which was right.

So you can move a job, pause it, split it and finish it. You cannot make one up. Looking for a way to create one is the mistake — the work already exists, you just have to find it.

If you used any of these words, try again fulfillment order · line item · derived entity · immutable · assignment · scope. "The order says what they want; the job says who has to go and get it" is the explanation. A beginner does not need the word "derived" to understand that inventing work nobody ordered would be nonsense.

Build it

Task · 60 minutes Dev store, 2+ locations

Make one order become two jobs, with your own hands.

  1. Stock item A only at location 1 and item B only at location 2.
  2. Buy both in one order.
  3. Query the order's fulfillmentOrders. There are two, each with its own location and status.
  4. Fulfil one of them. Look at the order: it is now partially fulfilled, with one parcel and one outstanding job.
  5. Feed that order through your own order handler.

Proof it worked: your handler either dropped the second job or reported the order as fully shipped. Either way you have found the bug, and it only ever appears on multi-location carts — which is why it survives testing.

The two statuses that look like "late" · 45 minutes Dev store
  1. Put a hold on one fulfillment order. Note it is ON_HOLD and cannot be started.
  2. Create a deferred one so it is SCHEDULED with a future fulfill_at.
  3. Now run your "unfulfilled orders" report. Both appear.
  4. Fix the report so it separates waiting for us from deliberately not ready.
  5. Try to release the hold from code, then delete that code and write down who is allowed to.

Proof it worked: your report has three buckets, not one. A warehouse dashboard that shows held and scheduled work as "to pick" will get those items shipped, and both are somebody's deliberate decision.

Pick your role · 20 minutes no store needed

Write down which of the three scope pairs your integration needs, and why — merchant-managed, assigned (you are the warehouse), or third-party (you need to see other services' work).

Proof it worked: your note names the role, not just the scope. Then prove it: hold only the wrong pair and confirm you get an empty list rather than an error. That silence is the failure mode.

Remember this

  • A fulfillment order is the work — the items from one order expected to ship from one location. One order, several jobs.
  • You cannot create one. Shopify creates them automatically with the order. There is no create mutation.
  • Three layers: Order (what they want) → FulfillmentOrder (who must pick it) → Fulfillment (the parcel that left).
  • ON_HOLD and SCHEDULED are not "late". Both are deliberate. Never treat non-fulfilled as ready-to-pick.
  • Three scope pairs are three roles — merchant-managed, assigned, third-party. The wrong one returns an empty list, not an error.
  • After a service accepts, the merchant cannot cancel — only request cancellation. Control has genuinely moved.
  • FulfillmentOrder and Fulfillment have different status sets, and FulfillmentStatus.OPEN/PENDING are deprecated.

If you remember one thing: an order tells you what a customer wants; the fulfillment order tells you what somebody has to do — and Shopify decides that, not you.

Ask questions. Then "next" for gift cards and store credit — money that lives outside the payment gateway.
