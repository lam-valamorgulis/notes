Track 1 · P10 · Lesson 6 · depth

Marketing surfaces, and what not to build

Mostly the merchant's tooling. Worth knowing exists so you do not build it by hand.

Your win today. You know where marketing attribution data comes from, and you know that the app extension surface for it is deprecated — so you can stop a project before it starts.

How it works

Derive it: what does Shopify need to know about marketing?

A merchant sends an email, runs an ad, posts a link. Later, someone buys something. Shopify's question is narrow: which effort produced this order?

To answer it, Shopify needs the campaign to be identifiable in the link the customer followed. That is what UTM parameters are for, and the API models them exactly and minimally:

FieldWhat it is
sourceThe website or application where the referral link exists
mediumThe campaign medium
campaignThe campaign name

All three are non-null. That is a small detail with a real consequence: there is no such thing as a partially-tagged marketing activity in this API. If your integration cannot supply all three, it cannot report the activity at all — so decide the naming convention before you build, because you cannot fill it in later.

The two shapes, and the one that is going away

There are two families of input here, and the difference matters more than the names suggest.

ShapeMeaningState
MarketingActivityCreateInput / MarketingActivityUpdateInputAn activity managed through a Shopify app extensionDeprecated — "will be removed in the near future"
MarketingActivityCreateExternalInput / MarketingActivityUpsertExternalInputAn externally-managed activity — it lives in your system, you report it to ShopifyCurrent

The documentation is explicit: marketing activity app extensions are deprecated and will be removed in the near future.

So the shape that survives is the honest one: the marketing tool is not Shopify. The campaign lives wherever it lives — an email platform, an ad account, your own system — and you report it to Shopify so orders can be attributed. You are not building marketing tooling inside Shopify; you are telling Shopify what happened elsewhere.

Which is why this Lesson is called "what not to build". The instinct is to build the merchant a campaign manager. The deprecation is Shopify saying that is not the shape.

Where this connects

Attribution needs the customer's journey to be observable, which is P10.4: web pixels are the sanctioned tracking path, and script tags on Thank you and Order status are sunset — 28 Aug 2025 for Plus, 26 Aug 2026 for non-Plus. So a client can be confident they have attribution data they stopped receiving months ago.

And audiences come from P10.5: a segment is a saved question, so a campaign aimed at one is aimed at a moving target.

Verified 2026-08-23: the UTMInput fields campaign, medium and source, all String! non-null; and that MarketingActivityCreateInput and MarketingActivityUpdateInput both carry the note "Marketing activity app extensions are deprecated and will be removed in the near future", while the external variants (MarketingActivityCreateExternalInput, MarketingActivityUpsertExternalInput, marketingActivityUpdateExternal) describe externally-managed activities — UTMInput.

Answer before reading on A client asks you to build them a campaign manager inside their Shopify admin, so their team can plan and launch email campaigns without leaving Shopify. What do you say?

That the surface for doing that is deprecated and going away, so building it means building something with a known end date. The supported shape is the reverse: campaigns live in a real marketing tool, and Shopify is told about them so it can attribute orders. Then the useful question — which is not the one they asked — is what they actually want. If it is "see campaign performance next to orders", that is reporting and it is achievable. If it is "replace our email tool", that is a product, not an integration.

Where it hurts

Costs money — building on marketing activity app extensions Documented as deprecated and to be removed in the near future. Building a merchant-facing campaign manager on it is committing to a rewrite with a date attached.
Costs money — no naming convention before you start source, medium and campaign are all non-null. There is no partial activity. Decide the convention up front, because you cannot backfill an attribution you never sent.
Fails with no error — attribution that stopped at a sunset date From P10.4: script tags on Thank you and Order status are sunset — 28 Aug 2025 Plus, 26 Aug 2026 non-Plus. They may still download and do nothing, so the client is confident about data they no longer have.
Building marketing tooling inside Shopify The supported direction is reporting in, not managing inside. Fighting that direction means fighting the platform for the life of the project.
Fails with no error — a campaign aimed at a segment From P10.5: segment membership is recomputed. The audience for a scheduled send is not the audience you saw when you scheduled it.
Inconsistent UTM values across systems Two systems reporting the same campaign under slightly different names produce two campaigns in the reporting, each with half the numbers. Nothing errors; the analysis is just wrong.
Judgement, not documentation — ask what they want to see, not what they want to build "Build us a campaign manager" is almost always a reporting request wearing a tooling request's clothes. The question that saves the project is "what do you want to be able to see?" — and it is usually achievable.

Say it out loud

Explain this to a beginner

Shopify is deprecating the way apps add marketing campaign management to the admin. Explain what that tells you about the direction Shopify wants this to work.

Say it out loud. Then read on.

Now compare with a plain-words answer

It tells you Shopify does not want to be where campaigns are run. It wants to be where their results land.

Think about what a shop needs. It needs to know which effort produced which sale. That is it. It does not need the planning, the scheduling, the writing or the sending to happen inside Shopify — there are whole companies whose entire product is doing that well.

So the useful arrangement is: run the campaign wherever it is best run, then tell Shopify what you did, with enough labelling that Shopify can match orders back to it. Shopify becomes the place you find out whether it worked.

Removing the way to build campaign management into the admin is Shopify saying that clearly. The remaining supported shape is the one for activities managed somewhere else and reported in.

Which is worth reading as general advice, not just about marketing. When a platform removes a way to do something, that is usually information about the shape it wants, not just a maintenance decision. Pushing against it means fighting the platform for as long as the project exists.

If you used any of these words, try again attribution · UTM · app extension surface · externally-managed · sunset. "Shopify wants to be where the results land, not where campaigns are run" is the explanation.

Build it

Task · 30 minutes, written no store needed

The deliverable is a refusal with an alternative — the R3.2 shape.

  1. Take a real "build us marketing tooling in Shopify" request.
  2. Write the constraint plainly: that surface is deprecated and going away.
  3. Write the supported alternative: activities managed externally and reported in for attribution.
  4. Then ask the question that reframes it: what do you want to be able to see?

Proof it worked: your note ends in a question about reporting rather than a no. Most of these requests resolve into a report somebody can actually have.

Fix the convention first · 30 minutes no store needed
  1. Write the naming convention for source, medium and campaign. All three are required, always.
  2. Find every system that will report activities and make them share that convention exactly.
  3. Write down what happens to an activity you cannot fully label. (It cannot be reported. That is the answer.)

Proof it worked: two systems reporting the same campaign produce one campaign in the reporting, not two half-sized ones. That is the failure this document prevents.

Check what they actually have · 30 minutes a real store

Before promising any reporting, verify the tracking still works: list script tags on Thank you and Order status, check them against the sunset dates, and compare the client's numbers before and after.

Proof it worked: you either confirmed the data is real or found a gap the client did not know about. Building a report on tracking that stopped is worse than building nothing.

Remember this

  • Shopify wants to be where results land, not where campaigns run. Report in; do not manage inside.
  • Marketing activity app extensions are deprecated and will be removed in the near future. Do not build on them.
  • The external variants are the supported shape — activities managed elsewhere, reported to Shopify.
  • source, medium and campaign are all non-null. No partial activity, so decide the convention first.
  • One convention across every reporting system, or one campaign becomes two half-sized ones.
  • Verify tracking before promising reporting — the checkout script sunsets in P10.4 may already have stopped it.
  • A campaign aimed at a segment is aimed at a moving target — see P10.5.

If you remember one thing: when a platform removes a way to build something, that is information about the shape it wants — and here the shape is "report it in", not "manage it inside".
