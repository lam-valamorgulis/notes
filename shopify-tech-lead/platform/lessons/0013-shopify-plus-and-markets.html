Track 1 · P5 · Lesson 1

Shopify Plus, and Markets

What the enterprise price actually buys a developer — and why one store had 205 markets.

Your win today. When a client asks "do we need Plus for this?", you can answer per feature instead of in general — because the answer genuinely differs per feature.

How it works

Plus is a set of specific unlocks, not a general better

The useful mental model is not "Plus is the big plan". It is: certain named capabilities are gated, and the gate is different for each one. Here is the part that affects your work, all verified today:

Read the first two rows together, because that pairing surprises people. Extensions on the checkout steps need Plus; extensions after the purchase do not. So "can we customise checkout?" has two answers depending on which side of the payment you mean — and a client on Advanced can do more than they expect.

Note also that Functions are not Plus-gated. A great deal of genuinely powerful server-side customisation — custom discounts, payment and delivery customisation — is available without the enterprise plan.

Markets: a group of buyers, not a country

A Market is a merchant-defined group of buyers, identified by conditions such as their region, retail location, or company location. Each market configures a distinct localised experience: currency, pricing, product availability, web presence, and content translations.

The important word is group. A market is not required to be a country — it can be a region, a set of countries, or a retail context.

Found on a real store A live Plus store sampled during this Course had 205 markets, every one active, against only 8 publications. Almost one market per country, created by a cross-border commerce app, plus seven groupings — a retail one, a Europe one, an international catch-all, and four short handles that looked hand-made and older than the rest.

205 against 8 is the lesson. Markets and sales channels are different things, and code that loops "per market" does 25× the work of code that loops "per channel" on that store. That is the difference between a query that finishes and one that times out.

What Markets means for an integration

Because a market can override…Your integration must…
PriceKnow which price you are writing. A market override is not the base price
Product availabilityNot assume "published" is global. A product can be live in one market and not another
CurrencyNever compare money across markets without the currency. Compare integer minor units of the same currency
TranslationsTreat a title as per-market content, not as one string

Never hardcode a market count. Make it a variable, like the store id in A3.7. A store can go from 3 markets to 205 by installing one app.

Answer before reading on A client on the Advanced plan asks you to add a message on the checkout page, and separately a survey after the order is placed. Which can you do?

The survey, yes — extensions on the Thank you and Order status pages are available on all plans except Starter. The message on the checkout page itself, no — extensions on the information, shipping and payment steps are Plus only. So the answer is "half of it, on this plan", and the honest follow-up is what the Plus upgrade would cost them versus the value of that one message. Answering "you need Plus for checkout customisation" would have been wrong and would have cost them the survey they could have had.

Where it hurts

Costs money — promising a Plus-only feature on a lower plan Scope agreed, work started, then discovered. The client either upgrades a plan they had not budgeted for or loses a feature they were promised. Check the gate per feature before the estimate.
Costs money — assuming Plus is required and losing scope The reverse error, and just as real. Functions, Thank-you-page extensions and web pixels are not Plus-gated. Saying "that needs Plus" when it does not talks a client out of something they can have.
Fails with no error — a hardcoded market count Written for 3 markets, deployed to a store with 205. Loops become 68× longer, timeouts appear in jobs that used to finish, and nothing points at markets as the cause.
Fails with no error — writing a base price over a market override Both are real prices. Overwrite the wrong one and a whole country sells at the wrong number, while every other market looks perfect. Reconciliation that ignores market scope will not see it.
Comparing money across markets Two currencies, one comparison, no error. Always compare integer minor units within one currency.
Judgement, not documentation — the rate-limit gap between test and production If you build against a Plus store and deploy to a Basic one, your effective throughput drops by a factor of ten and the variant throttle applies where it did not before. Know which plan you tested on and which you are shipping to.

Say it out loud

Explain this to a beginner

A client asks "do we need to upgrade to Plus?" Explain why that question cannot be answered as asked. Then explain what a "market" is without using the word country.

Say it out loud. Then read on.

Now compare with a plain-words answer

On Plus: the top plan is not "Shopify, but better". It is a specific list of individual doors that are unlocked. Some things you would assume are locked are free on every plan, and some things you would assume are free are locked. So "do we need Plus?" has a different answer for every single feature, and answering it in general gets you wrong in both directions — you either tell a client to spend money they did not need to, or you promise a feature they cannot have.

The only correct answer is: name the feature, check that feature, write the date you checked.

On markets: a market is a group of buyers you want to treat differently. That is all. Usually the grouping is geographic, but it does not have to be. For that group you can override the currency, the prices, which products they can even see, which website they land on, and the language.

Which has a consequence people miss: the same product can genuinely have several different prices at the same time, and none of them is the "real" one. So comparing two prices without asking "which market?" is comparing nothing.

If you used any of these words, try again feature gating · plan tier · price override · localisation · web presence · publication. "A list of individual doors, and some are already open" explains gating. "A group of buyers you treat differently" explains markets.

Build it

Task · 30 minutes Dev store

Build the per-feature answer sheet, because the general answer does not exist.

  1. Pick the five features your next project needs.
  2. For each, find the plan requirement in the docs and write it down with today's date.
  3. Mark the two that surprised you. There will be at least one in each direction.
  4. Now query the shop's plan through the API and print it. Note the field name is publicDisplayName — displayName is already deprecated.

Proof it worked: your sheet has a date on every row. A plan claim with no date is a guess, and commercial tiers move more often than APIs do.

The market trap · 25 minutes Dev store
  1. List the store's markets. Do not assume there are a handful — one real store in this Course had 205.
  2. Create a price override for one variant in one market.
  3. Read that variant's price with no market context, then with the market. Two different answers.
  4. Write a comparison of two totals from two markets, and then delete it — that is the point.

Proof it worked: your code stores money as integer minor units plus a currency, and refuses to compare across currencies at all. If anything in your sync adds two amounts without checking the currency, you have found a real bug.

Remember this

  • Plus is specific gates, not a general better. Check per feature.
  • Checkout steps need Plus. Thank you and Order status do not (all plans except Starter).
  • Functions are not Plus-gated. Market overrides need Advanced.
  • Plus multiplies REST limits ×10 and is exempt from the extra variant throttle.
  • A Market is a group of buyers, overriding currency, pricing, availability, web presence and translations.
  • One real store had 205 markets and 8 publications. Never hardcode a market count.
  • Never compare money across markets. Integer minor units, same currency.

If you remember one thing: "do we need Plus?" has a different answer for every feature — answer per feature or you will be wrong in both directions.
