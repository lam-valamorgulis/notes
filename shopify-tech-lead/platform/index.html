Platform

What Shopify actually is, before you try to integrate with it.

The one win of this Track. When a client describes what they want, you can say within a minute whether Shopify already does it, whether an app has to do it, or whether the platform will not allow it at all. That single answer is most of what a tech lead is paid for.

The story of this Track

Everything starts with the object model. A product is not the thing you sell — a variant is. Stock is not on the variant either; it lives on an inventory level, which is a variant and a location together. Get that picture wrong and every later conversation about stock, tills and warehouses goes wrong with it. Chapter P1 draws it once, properly, then covers the two APIs and why the rate limit is a budget rather than a count.

Chapter P2 is custom data — metafields and metaobjects. The old course never taught this, which was a real hole: it is the answer to almost every "can Shopify store X?" question a client asks. Chapter P3 is inventory, locations and the till, which is where an online store meets a physical shop. Chapter P4 is the storefront, mostly marked as depth because you are already strong here — except one Lesson, because "Liquid or headless" is a client conversation, not a technical preference.

Chapter P5 is checkout, money and Plus: what you actually get for the enterprise price, and when Shopify Flow means you should not write an app at all. Chapter P6 is moving data in and out at scale — the matching key, bulk operations, the giant catalogue load, and the cutover where mistakes cost real money.

Chapter P7 is shipping, delivery and tax — the two subjects every client raises and the rest of the Track does not cover. Both live inside a customer's checkout, so both follow the harsh rules of that shape: no retries, a latency budget, and a decision to make in advance about what happens when something is unreachable. Tax is the one place in this whole Course where the correct instinct is to take less ownership, not more.

Depth Lessons in this Track Many of the storefront and theme Lessons are marked depth on purpose. You already build themes for a living. Skip them, and come back only if a job needs them.

The Chapters

P1 · The shape of Shopify

  • The Shopify object model, end to end core Shop, product, variant, inventory item, inventory level, location, order, fulfillment order, customer — one diagram you will reuse all year.
  • The two APIs, and the rate limit model core REST is legacy since 1 Oct 2024; new public apps must be GraphQL-only since 1 Apr 2025. The limit is a cost budget, not a request count.
  • API versioning and the quarterly upgrade core Why "we upgrade every quarter" is a line item in every estimate you ever give.

P2 · Custom data

Missing entirely from the old course. It is the answer to most "can Shopify store this?" questions.

  • Metafields, definitions, and the cost of skipping them core A real integration wrote metafields with no definitions. The admin showed nothing and nothing was queryable. Nobody noticed for months.
  • Metaobjects, and when data does not belong on the product core When to model a thing separately instead of bolting fields onto a product.

P3 · Inventory, locations and the till

  • Three ways in-store stock reaches the storefront core The most sync-shaped Lesson in this Track. Three architectures, three sets of lies they tell the customer.
  • Locations and multi-location inventory depth Where stock actually lives, and what "available" means when it lives in five places.
  • The till: POS Lite, POS Pro, the retail order, and UI extensions depth What changes when the sale happens in a shop, and how code runs on the till.

P4 · The storefront

  • Liquid or headless — the client conversation core Not a technical preference. A cost, hiring and speed decision you have to defend to a client.
  • Dawn, Horizon and theme blocks depth What is genuinely new, not Liquid from zero.
  • The Cart AJAX API depth Including the overselling trap in update.js.
  • Discounts on the storefront depth The theme displays discounts, it never decides them. Plus the deprecated price fields that make line totals stop adding up.
  • Hydrogen, Oxygen and building headless depth What you inherit when the P4.1 decision goes the other way. The expensive part is replacing every app that used to render itself into the theme.

P5 · Checkout, money and Plus

  • Shopify Plus, and Markets core What the enterprise price actually buys, and how multi-currency and multi-language really work.
  • Checkout extensibility depth What you may change on the checkout page, and what is now permanently closed.
  • Shopify Functions depth Server-side logic Shopify runs for you: discounts, payment and delivery customisation.
  • Flow: when automation replaces your code depth Knowing when not to write an app is a tech-lead skill.
  • Bundles and cart transform Functions core One line becomes several, so price and stock stop matching what your sync wrote. The failure makes your monitoring more confident, not less.
  • Checkout branding, and the API that is already deprecated depth Needs Plus for a live shop and works free on a dev store. Also a worked example of why "it is documented" and "it is current" are different claims.

P6 · Getting data in and out

  • The matching key, and custom IDs core Shopify's own documented answer to "how do I find this record again?" — and the one most integrations never use.
  • Three ways to push it, and bulk operations core CSV, one-by-one, and bulk. When each is right, and the limits on each.
  • The huge catalogue load, and the throttle that only starts at 500,000 core The extra variant throttle begins at 500,000 variants on the store, then caps new ones at 10,000 a day; Plus is exempt. Below that threshold the ordinary rate limits are what actually slow you down. Verified on both shopify.dev pages 2026-08-23 — they used to disagree, and one of them was corrected.
  • The cutover and the proof core The night it goes live. What you check, in what order, and what you refuse to do at 1am.
  • What a migration is, and what never moves depth Passwords never move. Neither do several things clients assume will.
  • Orders, money, and the Shopify → Shopify replatform depth Historical orders, backdating, and the omitted line price that silently rewrites what a customer paid.

P7 · Shipping, delivery and tax

The two things every client asks about that the rest of the Track does not cover. Both are "decision during checkout" work, which is the hardest shape.

  • Shipping rates and delivery profiles core Five nested objects between a product and a price. "No shipping available" is almost never a bug — it means one link in the chain does not cover that cart.
  • Carrier-calculated rates: your server inside checkout core Shopify calls you, with no retry, and the timeout shrinks as the store gets busier — 10s down to 3s. Plus the documented fail-open to backup rates.
  • Tax: what Shopify does, and where you must not guess core The one area where taking ownership is the wrong instinct. Never calculate it, never advise on it — and know the three platform facts that catch developers out.
  • Market-driven shipping: the model replacing delivery profiles core Live change to what P7.1 teaches. deliveryProfiles is being deprecated for merchant-owned configuration. Every merchant by July 2027 — and the test is whose profile you touch.

P8 · After the sale

This Chapter was the top gap on the territory map before it was written. Everything before checkout is a proposal; everything here amends a completed financial record.

  • Returns, refunds and order editing core A refund moves money, stock and tax at once. Shopify now requires an idempotency key on refunds — it stopped trusting developers to make money-moving writes safe to repeat.
  • Payments, and what you must never touch core The only area where all five forces apply at once. Three minutes to resolve or Shopify may reverse what you took — plus commercial rules about your own pricing.
  • Draft orders: selling outside the storefront core Phone orders, quotes, wholesale and B2B invoices are one object. It can charge any amount for anything, so your code is the only price validation.
  • Fulfillment orders, and being a fulfillment service core The object that represents the work — and the one you cannot create. Two statuses look like "late" and are somebody's deliberate decision.
  • Gift cards and store credit core Money that moved at a different time from the sale. Revenue and bank figures diverge correctly, and store credit is a ledger rather than a balance.
  • Payouts and finance reconciliation depth Where the money lands. Four reasons an order total can never equal a bank deposit, and the bridge report finance actually wants.

P9 · Other ways to sell

Three commercial models that each break an assumption made everywhere else in the Course.

  • B2B: companies, catalogs and price lists core The price stops living on the variant. Sync only the variant price and reconciliation reports zero differences while every wholesale customer pays last year's rate.
  • Subscriptions and selling plans core Four policies, and one inventory choice that decides whether you show sold out or oversell. Uninstall the app and every plan is deleted 48 hours later.
  • Customer accounts and identity core Two versions, and the legacy one supports none of this. Check CustomerAccountsV2 before scoping anything.

P10 · Conversion, content and risk

The surfaces that convert, and the two areas you observe rather than own.

  • Search, filtering and discovery core Filters must be created in the admin before code can use them, and an undefined metafield is not filterable. "We have the data" and "customers can filter on it" are different statements.
  • Storefront performance core The cause is almost always apps, and the fix is almost never a rebuild. Measure before quoting anything.
  • Content, files and translations depth Low pain per item, high pain in aggregate. Translations multiply every number you have estimated.
  • Fraud, risk and web pixels depth Carry the risk signal, never interpret it. And the sanctioned tracking path now that checkout script tags are sunset.
  • Customer segments, and the query that defines them depth A saved question, not a saved list. Membership moves on its own, and anything you attach to it is attached to a moving target.
  • Marketing surfaces, and what not to build depth The app-extension surface is deprecated. Shopify wants to be where results land, not where campaigns run — so this Lesson is mostly a refusal with an alternative.
