Sales channel apps

Publishing to a channel is ordinary. Being the channel is a different app type.

Your win today. You can tell the two jobs apart, you know the six-step connection flow, and you know the one reason a non-marketplace app might still want to become a channel.

How it works

Derive it: what is a channel, from Shopify's side?

A shop sells in more than one place: its website, a till, a marketplace, a social app. Shopify has to answer two questions about each of those places. Which products appear there? And which orders came from there?

That pair is what a channel is. Not a feature — a named place, with a set of products published to it and orders attributed to it.

So there are two completely different jobs available to you, and they are confused constantly:

JobWhat you doDifficulty
Publishing to a channelRead or set which products appear in an existing channel, using publicationsOrdinary API work
Being a channelRegister as a place things are sold, receive product feeds, attribute orders backA different app type with its own object model

P4.5 already met the first one from the other direction: the Storefront API is channel-scoped, so a product missing from a headless site is usually a publication question. That is publishing.

Publications, which you will meet either way

A Publication is a group of products and collections published to an app. Each publication manages what displays on its associated channel. Three details worth carrying:

  • autoPublish — merchants can have products published automatically when created. So "the product appeared in a place I did not expect" can be a setting rather than your code.
  • Scheduled publishing — future publish dates are supported for online store channels, so merchants can coordinate launches. A product can exist and be deliberately not visible yet.
  • The catalog field links to pricing and availability rules for that publication's context. Which is the same idea as B2B catalogs in P9.1: visibility and price travel together.

Becoming a channel: the six-step flow

The connection is not a single call. A sales channel app integrates channel connections as part of its post-authentication flow:

1. merchant installs the app, completes OAuth 2. app presents the EXTERNAL platform's login (for example, a marketplace seller account) 3. merchant authenticates with their external account 4. app calls channelCreate with the specification handle and the account details -> Shopify creates PRODUCT FEEDS -> Shopify creates a channel-based ORDER ATTRIBUTION definition 5. app subscribes to product feed webhooks 6. app triggers a FULL SYNC For multiple connections, repeat 2-6 per connection.

Read step 4 carefully. channelCreate does not just record a name — Shopify creates the product feeds and the order attribution definition for you. So the plumbing you would expect to build is provisioned by the platform, and your job becomes consuming it correctly.

And steps 5 and 6 together are the pattern from A3.4 and A3.5, arriving again: subscribe to the stream, then do a full sweep. The webhook tells you about changes from now on; the full sync establishes the baseline. Doing only one of them is the bug.

Beyond the connection there is a channel config extension for describing your channel's capabilities, order attribution configuration for attributing orders to connections or custom sources, and contextual product feeds for subscribing to feed events.

The reason a non-marketplace app might still want this

Here is the connection people miss. P7.4 covered market-driven shipping, and for reading merchant shipping configuration Shopify's recommended path is Contextual Product Feeds — which is available if your app is a sales channel or can become one, and which Shopify says has several advantages over reading the Markets API directly.

So "should we become a channel?" is not only a marketplace question any more. If your app reads merchant shipping configuration, becoming a channel is the recommended route rather than a stretch goal.

Answer before reading on A client says "our products are not showing up on the marketplace app we installed". Name three explanations, and say which one is your code.

One: the products are not published to that channel's publication — a merchant setting, not your code. Two: they are published with a future publish date, so they exist and are deliberately not visible yet. Three: the channel's product feed sync has not run, or the app subscribed to feed webhooks without ever doing the initial full sync — that one is your code, and it is the classic version of A3.5's missing baseline. Checking the publication first costs a minute and rules out two of the three.

Where it hurts

Costs money — quoting "add a marketplace" as ordinary API work Publishing to a channel is ordinary. Being a channel is a different app type with its own object model, a connection flow, feeds, attribution and a config extension. Confusing the two understates the job substantially.
Fails with no error — subscribing to feed webhooks without a full sync Step 6 exists for a reason. Webhooks tell you about changes from now on; without the initial sweep you have no baseline, and every product that never changes again is permanently missing.
Fails with no error — blaming your sync for autoPublish Merchants can have products published automatically on creation. "Products appeared in the channel and we did not put them there" is often a setting, and hunting for it in your code wastes a day.
Fails with no error — a future publish date Scheduled publishing is supported for online store channels. A product that exists and is invisible may be correctly waiting, not broken.
Costs money — ignoring the publication's catalog The catalog field carries pricing and availability rules for that context. Same trap as B2B in P9.1: the price you reconciled is not necessarily the price in that channel.
Building one connection when the client wants several Multi-connection apps repeat the authentication and sync steps per connection. A single hard-coded connection is the "store 17 of four hundred" problem from A3.7, one level up.
Judgement, not documentation — becoming a channel is a commitment, not a shortcut It is now the recommended path for reading merchant shipping configuration, which makes it tempting for apps that are not marketplaces at all. It is still a different app type with its own review surface and its own ongoing obligations. Decide it deliberately, and price it.

Say it out loud

Explain this to a beginner

Explain the difference between an app that puts products into a marketplace and an app that is the marketplace, from Shopify's point of view.

Say it out loud. Then read on.

Now compare with a plain-words answer

Shopify only cares about two things per selling place: which products are available there, and which orders came from there.

An app that puts products into a place is just ticking boxes on a list Shopify already keeps. Shopify knows the place exists. You are saying "these ones, please". That is ordinary work.

An app that is the place has to be introduced first. Shopify has never heard of it, so there is no list to tick and nowhere for orders to be attributed. So the app registers itself, and in return Shopify sets up two things: a feed telling the app which products it should show, and a way to label orders as having come from there.

After that the app has an ongoing job. It listens for changes to the feed, and — the part people forget — it also has to ask for everything, once, at the start. Otherwise it only ever hears about products that change, and anything that never changes again is invisible to it forever.

So: one job is ticking boxes on an existing list. The other is being introduced, being given a feed, and keeping up with it.

If you used any of these words, try again publication · channel · product feed · order attribution · specification handle. "One ticks boxes on a list Shopify already keeps; the other has to be introduced first" is the explanation.

Build it

Task · 45 minutes Dev store

Do the ordinary job first, and meet the two settings that get blamed on code.

  1. List the store's publications and which products are in each.
  2. Unpublish one product from a channel. Confirm it disappears there and nowhere else.
  3. Turn autoPublish on. Create a product. Watch it appear with no action from you.
  4. Publish a product with a future date. Confirm it exists and is not visible.
  5. Read the publication's catalog and note the pricing rules attached to it.

Proof it worked: you have produced "the product is missing" and "the product appeared on its own" deliberately, and neither was your code. Those are two of the three explanations in the recall question.

Then the baseline bug · 60 minutes Dev store

This is the one that is genuinely yours.

  1. Subscribe to product feed webhooks. Do not run a full sync.
  2. Change one product. Confirm your app hears about it.
  3. Now list what your app knows. Every unchanged product is missing.
  4. Add the full sync. Run it. Compare again.

Proof it worked: before the sweep your app knew about one product; after it, all of them. Same lesson as A3.5 — the stream tells you about changes, and only a sweep establishes what is true now.

The decision, written · 30 minutes no store needed

Answer in writing: should this app become a sales channel? Cover the marketplace reason, and the P7.4 reason — Contextual Product Feeds being the recommended read path for market-driven shipping.

Proof it worked: your note treats it as a deliberate commitment with its own review surface and ongoing obligations, not as a shortcut to a shipping read. Both answers are fine; an accidental answer is not.

Remember this

  • Two different jobs: publishing to a channel is ordinary work; being a channel is a different app type.
  • A channel is "which products appear here" plus "which orders came from here". That pair is the whole idea.
  • channelCreate provisions the plumbing — Shopify creates the product feeds and the order attribution definition.
  • Subscribe and full-sync. Feed webhooks alone leave every unchanged product permanently missing.
  • autoPublish and future publish dates explain most "missing" and "unexpected" products, and neither is your code.
  • A publication's catalog carries pricing and availability — visibility and price travel together, as in P9.1.
  • Multi-connection apps repeat the flow per connection. One hard-coded connection is A3.7 one level up.

If you remember one thing: publishing to a channel ticks boxes on a list Shopify already keeps; being a channel means being introduced, given a feed, and keeping up with it — and the full sync is the step everyone forgets.
