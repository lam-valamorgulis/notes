Track 2 · A1 · Lesson 4

Scopes, protected customer data & compliance

What you are allowed to ask for, and what you must promise in return.

Your win today. You can tell a client exactly which data the integration will touch, why you are asking for less than they offered, and what obligations arrive the moment a customer's name enters your database.

How it works

Scopes: ask for the minimum, and be able to defend it

Scopes are the permissions your app requests. They live in your app configuration, in your repo, so a scope change is a reviewable diff. The rule is not "ask for what you might need" — it is ask for the minimum that makes the feature work. Shopify approves protected data access only when the request is the minimum required to deliver the functionality.

For the Project in this Course, that is genuinely short:

read_products
write_products
read_inventory
write_inventory
read_locations

Notice what is absent. No customer scopes, no order scopes. A catalogue-and-stock sync does not need to know who bought anything — and staying at that line keeps the whole next section from applying to you.

Protected customer data: three levels

LevelWhat you touchWhat you owe
0No customer dataNothing. No action required
1Customer data excluding name, address, phone and email Request access in the Partner Dashboard · implement the level 1 requirements
2Customer data including name, address, phone or email Request access and the specific fields · implement level 1 and 2 · participate in data protection reviews

The jump from 1 to 2 is the expensive one, and the trigger is small: a single name field. This applies to the review process for a public, published app.

Verified 2026-08-23: the three levels, the exact field list that separates 1 from 2, the data-protection-review obligation at level 2, and the minimum-necessary approval rule — Work with protected customer data.

Mandatory compliance webhooks

Any app distributed through the Shopify App Store must respond to data subject requests whether or not it collects personal data. Three topics:

TopicMeans
customers/data_request A customer asked the store owner for their data. You supply it to the store owner, not to the customer
customers/redact Delete or redact that customer's data
shop/redact The store is gone. Delete its data

The obligations: acknowledge with a 200-series status code, and complete the action within 30 days. There is one carve-out worth knowing precisely — if you are legally required to retain the data, you should not complete the redaction.

And a timing rule that surprises everyone. For customers/redact: if the customer has not placed an order in the past six months, Shopify sends the payload 10 days after the deletion request. Otherwise the request is withheld until six months have passed.

Verified 2026-08-23: the three topics, the App-Store requirement regardless of data collection, the 200-series acknowledgement, the 30-day completion window, the legal-retention exception, and the 10-day / six-month redaction timing — Privacy law compliance. Shopify applies the same privacy rights to all personal data regardless of where the individual is located, rather than only in GDPR or CPRA territories.

Answer before reading on A customer asks to be deleted today. They ordered last week. When does your app receive the customers/redact webhook?

In about six months. Because they ordered within the past six months, the request is withheld until six months have passed. Only for a customer with no order in that window does it arrive after 10 days. If you promised a client "deletions flow through within a day", you promised something the platform does not do — and the reason is sound, because a merchant still needs order records for returns, tax and chargebacks.

Where it hurts

Costs money — one name field moves you to level 2 Level 1 excludes name, address, phone and email. Include any one of them and you are at level 2: request the specific fields, implement both levels' requirements, and take part in data protection reviews. Teams walk into this by adding a customer name to a dashboard "just for support".
Required even when you collect nothing Mandatory compliance webhooks apply to every App Store app regardless of whether it collects personal data. "We don't store customers" is not an exemption from having the endpoints.
Fails with no error — the 30-day clock nobody owns You return 200 and the request is acknowledged. Then a human has 30 days to actually delete the data, and nothing chases them. An acknowledged request that was never completed looks identical to a completed one from the outside. Track them in your own database with a due date.
Data goes to the store owner, not the customer On customers/data_request you supply the data to the store owner directly. Sending it to the customer would be sending personal data to whoever controls that email address — which is not the same person you think it is.
Sometimes all you get is an email address Shopify's own note is that in some cases a customer record contains only the email address. Code that assumes a customer id will always resolve to a full record will throw on exactly the requests you least want to fail.
Judgement, not documentation — scope creep is permanent Adding a scope is easy. Removing one means merchants re-approve. So every "we might want orders later" costs you either an unused permission on a security review, or a re-consent campaign. Ask for what today's feature needs.

What you say to a client/PM

When a client offers full admin access "to make it easier" I'd rather take less. The sync only needs products, inventory and locations — no customers, no orders. That means if our credentials ever leaked, nobody's personal data is exposed, and it keeps us out of a whole category of privacy obligations. It's not extra work for you; it's a shorter list. Turns "least privilege" into two things a client cares about: their customers' exposure, and less paperwork.
When a client asks for a customer name on the sync dashboard I can, but I want you to know what it costs before you decide. Adding a name moves us into a higher tier of Shopify's customer-data rules — extra approval and a data protection review. If support just needs to find an order, an order number does the job with none of that. Which do you want? Never refuse outright and never comply silently. Price it, offer the cheaper option, hand back the decision.
When someone promises a client "deletions happen within 24 hours" I need to correct that before it's in writing. If the customer has ordered in the last six months, Shopify holds the deletion request for six months before it reaches us — the merchant still needs those records for returns and tax. We have 30 days to act once it arrives. Let's say that instead. Catching a wrong promise before it is written down is worth more than anything you will build that week.

Remember this

  • Ask for the minimum scope. Approval depends on it, and removing a scope later means re-consent.
  • Level 0 costs nothing. A catalogue-and-stock sync can stay there by never touching customers or orders.
  • Name, address, phone or email = level 2. One field makes the jump, and level 2 adds data protection reviews.
  • Three compliance topics: customers/data_request, customers/redact, shop/redact. Required for App Store apps even if you store nothing.
  • 200-series to acknowledge, 30 days to complete. Unless you are legally required to retain the data, in which case do not redact.
  • Redaction timing: 10 days if no order in six months, otherwise withheld for six months.
  • Data requests go to the store owner, never to the customer. Sometimes only an email address exists.

If you remember one thing: the cheapest privacy programme is not collecting the data — decide your scopes so level 0 stays true.
