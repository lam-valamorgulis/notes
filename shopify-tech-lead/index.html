Why this Course exists. Clients hire the person who has already run a sync, not the person who can describe one. Reading cannot build that. Building can. So this Course has a running Project at its centre, and everything you read exists to serve the build.

The big picture — how it all connects

A Shopify store on its own is simple. One system owns everything. The moment a second system appears — an ERP, a till, a warehouse, a supplier feed — you have to answer one question for every single field: who owns this? Almost every production failure in this trade is that question answered badly, or answered twice.

So the Course is three Tracks, in the order the question gets harder.

Track 1 — Platform teaches what Shopify actually is: the object model, the two APIs and the cost-based rate limit, custom data, locations and inventory, the storefront, checkout, shipping and tax, and how data gets in and out at scale. This is the vocabulary. Without it, no integration conversation makes sense, and you cannot tell a client why their idea is a three-day job or a three-month job.

Track 2 — Apps & Integrations is the heart. You integrate Shopify by building an app, so the app is the vehicle and the sync is the job. This Track teaches field ownership, safe replay, why a webhook is only a hint, clock skew, tombstones, backlogs and reconciliation — and then makes you build all of it. The Project is a real service, in a real repo, running against a real Shopify Dev store, loaded with a synthetic catalogue at real scale.

Track 3 — The Room is every human-facing conversation: the client who wants a date, the PM who wants a smaller number, the junior whose pull request is wrong, the interviewer. It is the same muscle each time. This Track includes fixed English sentences to drill out loud, because knowing the answer and being able to say it under pressure are two different skills.

The three Tracks meet in the Project. Track 1 tells you what the platform will let you do. Track 2 tells you how to sync it without lying to anyone. Track 3 is how you explain the trade-off to the person paying for it.

Core path, and optional depth

Three different ideas, and it is worth keeping them apart core and depth say whether a Lesson is on the main path of its own Chapter. spine 07 says the Lesson is one of the 28 that stop you shipping, and gives its position in the reading order. The territory map rates areas by pain, which is a different question again.

So a Lesson can be core and not on the spine — B2B pricing, for instance, is core to selling wholesale and is not what stops you shipping a catalogue sync. Follow the spine first. Then use the territory map to pick what your actual client needs.

92 Lessons is the honest total. 62 are the core path and 30 are optional depth, marked on every Track Map. At 10–15 hours a week the core path plus the Project is roughly four months. All 92 is six to seven.

Depth Lessons are not filler. They are the ones you can skip because of what you already know — Liquid, themes, front end — or the ones you only need when a specific job appears, like a Shopify-to-Shopify replatform.

Reading the pills core on the path · depth optional. All 92 Lessons and 17 References are written — including every Project step. What remains is not writing but doing: the Project is yours to build, and Step 3 onward needs a Dev store and a public tunnel. An item with no link does not exist yet, so this Map never sends you to a dead page.
