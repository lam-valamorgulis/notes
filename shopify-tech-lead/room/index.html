Track 3 of 3 · the conversations

The Room

Client, PM, mentee, interviewer. It is the same muscle every time.

The one win of this Track. You can be asked a hard question you do not have the answer to, and still leave the room trusted. That is a set of sentences, and sentences can be practised.

The story of this Track

A tech lead is not the person who knows the most. It is the person the client believes. Belief comes from three things: explaining a design so a non-engineer can follow it, giving numbers that turn out to be true, and being honest about what you do not know yet.

Chapter R1 is explaining systems out loud — the whiteboard answer, aimed at a client who wants to understand rather than a grader who wants to score you. Chapter R2 is owning the work: ambiguous scope, estimates, two projects at once, and a client who does not reply for five days. Chapter R3 is the part most courses skip. English is your second language and the hard conversations happen live, so this Chapter is fixed sentences to drill out loud — for buying thinking time, for saying no, for giving a range, and for disagreeing with a PM without sounding difficult.

Chapter R4 is mentoring and pressure. Chapter R5 is interviews — kept, but as one Chapter in a Course about being good at the job, not as the reason the Course exists.

Why R3 exists at all Knowing the answer and being able to say it under pressure are two different skills. The "What you say to a client/PM" block in every Lesson gives you the content. It does not give you the sentence when your heart rate is up. R3 gives you the sentence.
