The full mock

Answer before you reveal. Printable score sheet. No marks for reading ahead.

Your win today. An honest measurement of where you are — which is only honest if you answer out loud before looking at what follows each question.

How it works

The rule that makes this worth doing Say your answer out loud, in full, before reading the "looking for" line. Reading first feels productive and teaches nothing — recognising an answer is not producing one. If you read ahead, the score is meaningless and so is the exercise.

The one question that is not scored Which of the fifteen did you most want to skip? That is your next re-read — reluctance is a more reliable signal than a score.

Wanting to skip a question almost always means one of two things: you know you cannot produce it, or you can produce it but not confidently. Both are the same instruction — that Lesson, out loud, this week.

Where it hurts

Reading the "looking for" column first Recognition is not production. You will score well and learn nothing, and the false confidence is worse than a low score.
Answering in your head Silent answers are always fluent. The failure mode this mock exists to find is the gap between knowing and saying, and that gap is invisible unless you speak.
Fails with no error — repeating the mock instead of re-reading Your score rises because you remember the questions. Nothing was learned. Repeating a test measures recall of the test.
Scoring 1 as 2 Hesitating counts as 1. Under real pressure a hesitation becomes a 0, so be strict — a generous score here costs you later.
Judgement, not documentation — the mock cannot test the thing that matters most It measures whether you can say the answers. It cannot measure whether you have built one. A 30 out of 30 with no running Project is exactly the position that lost an interview once. The Project is the part the mock cannot score.

What you say to a client/PM

When you scored well but the gap is real I can talk through all of that, and I want to be straight about the difference between talking and having done it. I've built a sync end to end against a dev store — real rate limits, real webhooks, deliberate failures. What I haven't done is a live cutover with real money moving. That's the piece I'd want support on first time. Uses the honesty from R5.1 rather than letting fluency imply experience. Fluency without that sentence is exactly what reads as overselling.
When you are asked something you scored 0 on I don't have that one at my fingertips. What I'd do is check Shopify's documentation and note the date, because these limits move — I've seen one change by a factor of ten in three weeks. I'd rather look it up than give you a number I half-remember. Turns a genuine gap into a demonstration of the right habit. Far stronger than a half-remembered fact delivered confidently.

Remember this

  • Answer out loud before reading the "looking for" line. Recognition is not production.
  • 2 = fluent and volunteered extra. 1 = hesitated. 0 = could not say it.
  • Below 20, re-read — do not repeat the mock. Repeating measures recall of the test.
  • The list of questions you could not answer aloud matters more than the total.
  • The question you wanted to skip is your next re-read.
  • Score strictly. A hesitation here is a zero under real pressure.
  • A perfect score with no running Project is the position that lost an interview.

If you remember one thing: this mock scores what you can say — the Project is what changes what you can honestly claim.
