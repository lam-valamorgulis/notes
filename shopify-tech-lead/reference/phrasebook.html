Reference · drill these OUT LOUD

Client / PM phrasebook

Every sentence from Track 3 on one page. Reading these does nothing. Saying them does.

How to use this page Pick one sentence per situation and say it out loud five times, today, while doing something else. Under pressure, choosing between options is the thing that fails — so you want one automatic sentence, not twelve you have read.

Buying ten seconds

Pick one Let me think about that for a second.
Good question — let me get this right rather than fast.
Can I take that in two parts? A narrated pause reads as care. An unexplained one reads as not knowing.

Getting the question again

Pick one Sorry, could you say that again?
Just to make sure I understood — you're asking about X, is that right?
When you say "sync", do you mean both directions or just one? The second one confirms and buys time simultaneously.

Not knowing

The most valuable sentence in the Course I don't know yet. What I'd do is load a sample at your real volume and measure — about half a day.
I'd have to check. I wouldn't want to guess at a number that ends up in a plan. "I don't know" plus a method plus a cost. Nobody penalises that.

Losing the thread

Pick one Let me start that again more simply.
Sorry — the short version is…
I'm overcomplicating it. The point is… "The short version is" almost always produces a better answer than the one you abandoned.

Estimating

The four-slot shape [LOW] if [CONDITION]. [HIGH] if not, because [CONSEQUENCE]. I'll know [WHEN]. Learn the shape, not the examples. Then you can fill it for anything.
Worked Three working days if their API can tell me what's changed since a given time. Eight if it can't, because then I'd compare full lists every night. I'll know within an hour of seeing their docs — can you get me those?
When they insist on one number I'll give you a number on Thursday. To get it I need their API docs and half a day to test. What I can commit to today is that Thursday date.

Saying no

Not possible That one isn't available on your plan — Shopify restricts app content on the payment step to Plus. What I can do on your plan is everything after the order is placed. Attribute platform limits to the platform. Never own them as your decision.
Possible but unwise I can build it. Before you decide — that number includes stock already sold but not shipped, so customers could buy things you can't ship. I'd use the sellable figure instead. Same work. Shall I? Never refuse outright, never comply silently. Price it and hand it back.
Possible but not now — the most reusable sentence here Yes — about three days. Where would you like those three days to come from? Refuses nothing and makes the cost unavoidable.

Disagreeing upward

The three-clause shape I want that too. The one thing I'd check first is [specific concern], because [specific consequence]. Your call — I'd [recommendation]. One concern, not three. And offer a cheaper version of your own position so they have a way to agree.
Overruled Understood — I'll build it that way. I've noted the concern so we know where to look if it comes up. Say it once, record it once, commit fully. Never half-execute.
Escalating something unsafe I'm not comfortable making this call at my level. It affects customer data and it can't be undone, so I'd like it confirmed by whoever owns that risk. I'll keep working on everything else meanwhile.

Getting a reply from a busy client

The four-line message Need a call from you before Thursday. [ONE QUESTION.] [Option A costs X; option B costs Y.] I'd do A. If I don't hear by Thursday I'll go with A and we can revisit. Ask first, context last. Always recommend — approving is far cheaper than deciding. Default to the reversible direction.
The weekly note Done this week: … Next week: … Blocked on you: … (chased Tuesday, still waiting). The blocker line, repeated and dated every week, is the whole value.

A live problem

First two sentences, always in this order Let's stop it getting worse first — I'll pause the sync so nothing else gets overwritten. Then give me two product codes that are wrong and I'll trace exactly what we sent and when. Mitigate, then reproduce. That order marks you out as someone who has done this before.
Asked "what's causing it?" with no evidence yet Two possibilities and I don't want to guess between them yet. Either we sent the wrong value, or we sent the right one and something changed it after — and our record of what we sent will tell us which. Ten minutes.
Asking for the room Give me twenty minutes on my own and I'll come back with either a cause or a much better question. It's paused, so nothing's getting worse.

Explaining the sync in thirty seconds

The Feynman test — if you can't say it this way, you don't have it Your ERP decides what the products are and what they cost. Shopify decides how many you have left, because that's where sales happen. We move each piece one direction only, so the two can never fight. Every update we send is a complete statement rather than an instruction, so re-sending is always safe. And every night we compare the two and report any differences.

The experience gap

Three parts: name it · what you did · what's still missing Not in production, no. What I've done is build one end to end — a service syncing a fake ERP with a Shopify dev store, with switches to make the other side fail on purpose so I could handle it rather than hope. What I haven't had is a real merchant's data wrong on a Monday morning with someone on the phone. That's the part I can't practise. The third part is what makes the first two believable.

Words to never use

NeverInstead
"That's impossible""Shopify doesn't allow that on this plan"
"That's a bad idea""The thing I'd worry about is…"
"You don't need that""What's the outcome you're after?"
"It's not best practice"Name the specific failure it causes
"It depends on the data"Name the measurable property — match rate, blank rate, variant count
"Sorry, my English…"Nothing. Use short sentences instead
