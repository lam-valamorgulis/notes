Reference · write yours once, reuse everywhere

Behavioral STAR worksheet

Six stories, written once. Almost every behavioural question maps to one of them.

Why six Interviewers ask dozens of questions that reduce to six situations. Write these once, properly, and you stop inventing under pressure. Aim for 60–90 seconds spoken each — write it, then time yourself saying it.

The format, and the two parts people skip

LetterContentSeconds
SituationEnough context to make the problem real. No more15
TaskWhat was yours to do. Say "I", not "we"10
ActionWhat you actually did, specifically35
ResultA number if you have one, honest if you do not15
+ LearnedWhat you would do differently10
+ ChangedWhat you changed afterwards so it could not recur10
The last row is the one that separates senior from mid "I learned to communicate earlier" is a feeling. "I added a weekly note with a blockers line, and I've used it on every project since" is a changed system. Interviewers are listening for the second.

Story 1 · Something went wrong and it was partly yours

Answers: "tell me about a failure" · "a time you made a mistake" · "a project that went badly" · "how do you handle being wrong" S ______________________________________________________________ T ______________________________________________________________ A ______________________________________________________________ R ______________________________________________________________ Learned ________________________________________________________ CHANGED ________________________________________________________

Rule: own a real share of it. A failure story where nothing was your fault is not a failure story, and everyone can hear that.

Story 2 · You disagreed with someone more senior

Answers: "conflict with a manager" · "pushed back on a decision" · "disagreed with a client" · "how do you handle disagreement" S ______________________________________________________________ T ______________________________________________________________ A ______________________________________________________________ R ______________________________________________________________ Learned ________________________________________________________ CHANGED ________________________________________________________

Rule: include the case where you were overruled and committed anyway. That is stronger than only winning.

Story 3 · You owned something ambiguous

Answers: "worked without direction" · "took initiative" · "unclear requirements" · "how do you prioritise" S ______________________________________________________________ T ______________________________________________________________ A ______________________________________________________________ R ______________________________________________________________ Learned ________________________________________________________ CHANGED ________________________________________________________

Rule: name the decisions you made without asking, and the one thing you did ask. That contrast is the whole point.

Story 4 · You helped someone else get better

Answers: "mentoring" · "code review" · "helped a struggling colleague" · "how do you develop others" S ______________________________________________________________ T ______________________________________________________________ A ______________________________________________________________ R ______________________________________________________________ Learned ________________________________________________________ CHANGED ________________________________________________________

Rule: the result is about them, not you. And if you turned a repeated review comment into a rule or a check, say so — that is the systems answer.

Story 5 · Something broke in production

Answers: "an incident" · "pressure" · "a tight deadline" · "an angry client" · "how do you debug" S ______________________________________________________________ T ______________________________________________________________ A ______________________________________________________________ R ______________________________________________________________ Learned ________________________________________________________ CHANGED ________________________________________________________

Rule: show the order — mitigate first, then diagnose. That sequence is the signal. And say what you refused to do (untested change, fix you could not explain).

Story 6 · You said no, or reduced scope

Answers: "an unreasonable request" · "managing expectations" · "a trade-off you made" · "a time you pushed back on a client" S ______________________________________________________________ T ______________________________________________________________ A ______________________________________________________________ R ______________________________________________________________ Learned ________________________________________________________ CHANGED ________________________________________________________

Rule: the best version is one where you turned down work you could have billed for, or offered a cheaper option. Those are memorable.

Checks before you use these

✓Check
☐Each one is 60–90 seconds spoken. Time it, do not estimate it
☐The Action section says "I", not "we". "We" hides what you did
☐Every story has a Changed line. No exceptions
☐No story blames a named person. Describe the situation, not the villain
☐At least one Result is honestly "it still wasn't great, but…"
☐Six different projects if possible — the same project six times reads thin
☐You can say each one out loud from the prompt alone, no notes
The Project counts If your six stories feel thin, the Project from Track 2 is a legitimate source for stories 1, 3 and 5 — the bug you caused on purpose, the ambiguity you resolved, the failure you handled. Be precise that it is your own build rather than production work, exactly as in R5.1. Precision buys credibility everywhere else.
