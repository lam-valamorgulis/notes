Reference · scripts for practising alone

Mock run sheets

Four sessions you can run on yourself. Each one is timed, spoken, and scored.

The only rule that matters Out loud, timed, and no notes. Practising in your head is always fluent, which is exactly why it teaches nothing. If you cannot record yourself, at least stand up and say it to a wall.

Sheet 1 · The design set piece · 10 minutes

Score: /7 sections delivered without stalling. Below 5, re-read A4.2 and run it again tomorrow, not now.

Sheet 2 · The client conversation · 15 minutes

Play both parts. Be a difficult but reasonable client.

They sayYou must
"We want them to match."Convert it into a per-field question they can answer
"How long?"Four-slot range. Low, high, the one cause, when it collapses
"Just give me a number."Commit to a date for the number
"Can we show exact stock per shop, live, on every page?"Push back on "exact" and on "every page". Offer bands, deferred, product page
"Sync our warehouse stock into Shopify."Ask: sellable, or everything on the shelf?
"Skip the reconciliation report, we're tight."Agree the goal, one concern, hand it back, offer a cheaper version
"Just scrape their website."Name the legal and maintenance risk. Offer the export. State the condition under which you would
"Why is the site saying no when the shelf says yes?"Promises, then buffer, then freshness — and hand them the dial
"All 40 shops at once, please."Argue cohorts by variety, naming what each stage proves
"Have you done this before?"Name the gap · the work · what is still missing

Score: /10. Any answer where you used a technology name, said "best practice", or gave a bare number scores 0 regardless of content.

A weekly rhythm that works

Mon Sheet 1 — the design set piece 10 min Tue Phrasebook — one situation, out loud 5 min Wed Sheet 3 — one debugging scenario 20 min Thu Phrasebook — a different situation 5 min Fri Sheet 2 or 4, alternating 15 min ── under an hour a week. the Project is where the rest of the time goes.
