The spine

The shortest honest route to building a Shopify integration on your own. Twenty-eight steps, in order.

What this page is for. The Course has 92 Lessons. This is the 23 of them, plus the 5 Project steps, that you cannot skip — ordered by what depends on what, not by Track.
Read this bit before using the list The numbers are a reading order, not Chapter positions. So the path jumps between Tracks on purpose: you need the object model before app auth, and the sync discipline before the database that holds it. Each step also carries its spine number on the Track Maps, so you can always see where you are.

This is not a shorter course. The other 64 Lessons are real and several of them will decide a project — B2B, subscriptions, tax, payments, migration. They are just not what stops you shipping. Read the spine first so the rest has somewhere to attach.
How this list was chosen, and the honest caveat One test, applied to every Lesson: if you did not know this, would it stop you shipping an integration? Not "is it useful" — everything here is useful. Stop-you-shipping.

That test is a judgement, and it encodes a bet: that most Shopify integration work is catalogue and inventory moving between two systems, with orders coming back. If your actual job turns out to be B2B pricing, or a migration, or payments, then the relevant Lesson is off this path and you should go and read it. The spine is a starting order, not a ranking of importance.

Stage 1 · The vocabulary — Nothing else in Shopify parses until these four are automatic.

01 The object model, end to end P1.1 — Every later Lesson uses these five nouns. Skip it and you will write a stock number to the wrong object.
02 The two APIs, and the rate limit model P1.2 — You will be throttled in week one. Knowing it is a cost budget, not a request count, is what makes you ask for less rather than wait longer.
03 API versioning and the quarterly upgrade P1.3 — Falling forward means a retired version answers with no error. Without this you cannot tell a healthy integration from a silently drifting one.
04 Metafields, definitions, and the cost of skipping them P2.1 — Custom data with no definition is stored and inert. This is the most common permanent mistake in the whole platform.

Stage 2 · Your app can talk to Shopify — The minimum to have a service that is allowed to act, and hears about changes.

05 App auth: session tokens and token exchange A1.2 — A sync needs its own identity. Use a staff member's and it dies when they go on holiday, at 3am, silently.
06 Webhooks done right A1.3 — The richest failure area in the Course: raw-body HMAC, the 5-second deadline, and the retry budget that ends in your subscription being deleted.
07 Scopes, protected customer data, and the mandatory webhooks A1.4 — Decides your compliance burden before any code exists. One field moves you up a level.

Stage 3 · Where stock actually lives — One Lesson, because overselling is the most expensive failure in this Course.

08 Locations and multi-location inventory P3.2 — A missing inventory level is absence, not zero — and you cannot write to a level that does not exist. Also: ask which location can do what, never how many there are.

Stage 4 · The sync discipline — The heart of the Course. If you only read one stage, read this one.

09 Shopify alone, and what a second system adds A3.1 — Establishes the four questions that every later Lesson answers. Also kills "two-way sync" as a requirement.
10 Ownership, per field not per record A3.2 — The single most useful idea here. If the answer to "who decides this field?" is "both", you have found the next bug.
11 Absolute writes, safe replay, and tombstones A3.3 — Makes every retry free, which is what every other technique assumes. And deletion must be sent, never implied by silence.
12 Webhooks are a hint — clocks, order and staleness A3.4 — Deletes an enormous amount of complexity: read current instead of trusting the payload, and out-of-order delivery stops mattering.
13 The backlog A3.5 — What you do after an outage. And why an empty queue afterwards is a warning, not a success.
14 Reconciliation, and numbers that do not lie A3.6 — The only thing that measures correctness. Everything else measures effort.

Stage 5 · The service that runs it — Enough backend to hold the discipline above. Not a backend course.

15 A Postgres schema for a sync store A2.2 — Mapping, position, what you sent, what failed. The shadow table is what answers "did we break it, or did they change it?"
16 One queue, with retries and a dead-letter A2.3 — Includes the fact that throttled GraphQL returns HTTP 200 — so if (response.ok) is not an error check.
17 Run it: docker-compose, CI, and what to log A2.4 — The four numbers, and why "no errors" is not the same as working.

Stage 6 · Identity and volume — How you know which record is which, and how you move ten thousand of them.

18 The matching key, and custom IDs P6.1 — Never match on anything a human can edit. Write your own identifier into Shopify instead.
19 Identifier mapping and the mapping registry A4.4 — Identity is recorded, never computed. Treat Shopify ids as a cache — an option change can recreate variant ids.
20 Three ways to push it, and bulk operations P6.2 — The import is not finished until you have downloaded the results and counted them.

Stage 7 · The work an order creates — Two Lessons that decide whether your design is the right shape at all.

21 Fulfillment orders, and being a fulfillment service P8.4 — The most common integration you will actually be handed. And two statuses look like "late" while being somebody's deliberate decision.
22 Integration patterns: ERP, CRM, payments, fulfillment A4.1 — One question decides everything: does this live inside a customer's checkout? Get it wrong and you designed the wrong kind of system.

Stage 8 · More than one store — One Lesson, and it has to be read before you write the first function.

23 Store 17 of four hundred A3.7 — Pass the store id from the very first line. Everything else here can be retrofitted; this cannot.

The Project · now build it — Reading is finished. This is the part that changes what you can honestly say.

24 Step 0 — the Dev store and the generator A5.1 — Shape crosses, contents do not. And pick a non-Plus store so you meet the limits while it is safe.
25 Step 1 — the Fake ERP A5.2 — You write the other side so you can break it on demand. Control beats realism when the skill is handling failure.
26 Step 2 — catalogue out A5.3 — The bulk load, then keeping it fresh. A load that reports success is not a load that worked.
27 Step 3 — webhooks in, inventory back A5.4 — The first inbound step. The five seconds is for saying yes, never for doing the work.
28 Step 4 — the queue, the dead-letter and the proof A5.5 — Break the ERP on purpose and prove the report catches it. This is the evidence that you can build one alone.
Where the rest goes Once the spine is done, the honest way to pick next is by what your actual job touches. The territory map rates every area by pain, and it names the seven things this Course still does not cover. Work down it, or jump to whatever the current client needs.
The one thing this page cannot do for you Reading all 28 changes nothing on its own. Every Lesson has a Build it block ending in a proof, and Stage 9 is five steps of actual construction. The Course was rebuilt around building precisely because the reading-only version is what failed. Finish the Project.
