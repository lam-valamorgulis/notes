The Four Hats

"What does a manager actually do all day?" Here's the map — four jobs in one, and the reason the year is ordered the way it is.

Your win today: you'll be able to name the four parts of the manager's job, give a real example of each, and spot which "hat" any situation is asking you to wear.

One job, four hats

In Lesson 1 we saw the job is about the team's output. But "raise the team's output" is still vague. In practice, the work sorts into four hats you put on and take off all day:

The four hats, one line each

HatThe question it answersA real task this week
PeopleIs each person growing and supported?Run a 1:1; give one piece of feedback.
DeliveryWill we ship what we said, roughly when?Unblock a stuck task; sanity-check an estimate.
DirectionAre we working on the right thing at all?Re-order the backlog by real priority.
ProcessDoes the way we work help or hurt us?Fix a meeting that wastes the team's time.
Example

Priya manages five engineers on a billing team. Look at one Tuesday. She got the database admin on a call to unblock a stuck migration — Delivery. She told Marco his design doc was too long for anyone to read, then showed him a two-page one that worked — People. She cut the Thursday status meeting from 45 minutes to 15 and moved the rest to a written thread — Process. She moved the refund bug above the new dashboard in the backlog, because refunds were losing money every day — Direction. Four hats, one day, and she wrote no code.

The two traps hidden in this picture

Trap 1 — you wear only the hat you came from. Engineers arrive strong at Delivery and Direction (the "work" side). So new managers pour energy there and quietly neglect People and Process (the "human" side) — the exact hats that most decide whether a team thrives. The year is deliberately ordered People-first (Months 2–6) to correct this pull.1

Trap 2 — you forget you're switching hats. Answering a Slack message about a broken build (Delivery), then a report in tears about burnout (People), then a roadmap doc (Direction) — in fifteen minutes. That switching is tiring and easy to do badly. Naming the hat you're putting on ("right now this is a People moment") helps you show up correctly for each.

Example

Nadia leads eight engineers on a mobile app. One morning she spent an hour in a release war room, still typing fixes into Slack. Then Tom joined their 1:1 and said he had not slept properly in two weeks. Her first reply was "how long until it's fixed?" — she was still wearing the Delivery hat. She stopped, said out loud "wrong hat, this is a People moment", closed her laptop, and asked him what the last two weeks had actually felt like. Tom told her later that the laptop closing was the part he remembered.

Remember this
  • The manager's job splits into four hats: People, Delivery, Direction, Process.
  • People = grow each person · Delivery = ship predictably · Direction = choose what matters · Process = shape how the team works.
  • You switch hats many times a day — the switching is real work, so name the hat you're wearing.
  • Trap 1: new managers over-wear Delivery/Direction (the "work" hats) and neglect People/Process.
  • That's why this year is ordered People-first (Months 2–6) on purpose.
  • Every module in the plan is one region of this map.

If you remember one thing: when you feel lost, ask "which hat does this moment need?"
