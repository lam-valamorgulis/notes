Start Now: The Pre-Manager Playbook

"But I don't have any reports yet." Good news: you don't need a title to start managing. You need reps — and they're all around you.

Your win today: you'll leave with four concrete reps you can start this month with zero reports, each one practicing a real manager skill and building evidence for when you interview or ask for the role.

You don't need the title to build the skill

Waiting to be made a manager before practicing management is like waiting to be hired as a chef before you ever cook. The skills are learnable now, in your current job. Camille Fournier's whole model treats the pre-manager stages — mentor, tech lead — as where management is actually learned.1

Even better: each rep maps onto one of the four hats. So this month isn't abstract prep — it's you wearing each hat once, for real.

The four reps, in detail

1. Mentor someone People hat

Offer to mentor a newer or more junior engineer. Set a light weekly check-in. Practice the core loop: listen more than you talk, ask questions before giving answers, give one specific piece of feedback each time. This is a real 1:1 in miniature — the exact skill of Month 2.

2. Tech-lead a project (or a slice) Delivery hat

Volunteer to lead a piece of work end-to-end: break it into tasks, coordinate the people involved, unblock them, and send a short weekly status update. You'll feel the pull to just do it all yourself — notice it. That's the Bottleneck trap, and resisting it is the practice.

3. Run the retro / fix one ritual Process hat

Offer to facilitate the team's retro or standup. Making a meeting genuinely useful — and cutting or fixing one that isn't — is pure Process-hat work. Bonus: it makes the whole team's time more valuable, which is leverage.

4. Write a proposal Direction hat

Pick something you think the team should do differently and write a one-page proposal: the problem, the options, your recommendation, the tradeoffs. Arguing for a priority in writing is the everyday craft of the Direction hat — and it's visible to leadership.

The foundation: be a "manager of one" Before any of this, manage yourself well: do what you said, communicate proactively, be someone others can rely on without chasing. If you can't manage a team of one (you), a team of five won't fix it. This is the quiet prerequisite everyone notices.2

Two things to set up once

  • Start an evidence log (a "brag document"). A running note of leadership things you did: "mentored X, they shipped Y"; "led project Z, delivered on time." Memory fades; a promo case or EM interview needs specifics. You'll thank yourself in Month 12.3
  • Tell your manager the goal. Say plainly: "I want to grow toward engineering management this year. Can you give me stretch reps and feedback?" Most managers will happily hand you leadership work — and now they're looking for chances to sponsor you.
Watch out Glue work (running retros, unblocking, docs) is essential but can be invisible — you do it, nobody notices, and it doesn't count at promotion time. Make it visible: put it in your update, your evidence log, and your manager 1:1. (Tanya Reilly's talk "Being Glue" is the classic warning.)4

Practice: pick the highest-leverage rep

Scenario

You have ~4 spare hours a week and want the rep that both builds the weakest manager muscle for an ex-strong-IC and gives you real evidence. A new grad just joined and is clearly a bit lost. There's also a gnarly refactor you'd personally enjoy leading solo.

Remember this
  • You can practice management now, with no reports — the skills are learnable in your current job.
  • Four no-authority reps, one per hat: mentor (People), tech-lead a project (Delivery), run the retro (Process), write a proposal (Direction).
  • Foundation under all of it: be a reliable "manager of one" first.
  • Keep an evidence log — you'll need specifics for the promo case or EM interview.
  • Tell your manager your goal so they can hand you reps and sponsor you.
  • Make glue work visible, or it won't count.

If you remember one thing: don't wait for the title — collect the reps that earn it.
