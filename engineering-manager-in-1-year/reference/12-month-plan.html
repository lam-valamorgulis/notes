The 12-Month Plan

One page that holds the whole year. Four phases, twelve monthly modules. Tick each skill as you can do it — not when you have read about it.

How to use this: The Map tells the story; this page tracks what to tick off. Each month has a small number of skills and one decision drill — a real situation where you pick the best move. Your ticks are saved in this browser automatically.

Progress: 0 / 0 skills ticked

Pacing at 6–10 hrs/week Roughly: ~2 hrs reading the primary source, ~2 hrs on the lesson + drills, ~2–4 hrs applying it at work (a 1:1, a piece of feedback, leading a slice of a project). The plan works even before you have reports — each month has a "do it now" version using mentoring, tech-lead work, and glue work.

Phase 1 · Months 1–3 · Foundations & the transition

Change how you see the job, then learn the two rituals every manager lives on: the 1:1 and feedback.

M1The Manager's Mindset & the Transition

Objective: understand that the job changes from "my output" to "the team's output," and know the traps that catch new managers.

Decision drill: You are promoted Friday. Monday, your old teammate asks you to review their PR "like before." What do you do — and what does it signal?

Primary: The Manager's Path ch 1–4 · The Making of a Manager ch 1–2. Lessons: 0001–0004.

M2One-on-ones & Building Trust

Objective: run a weekly 1:1 that the report owns, builds trust, and surfaces problems early.

Decision drill: A report says "1:1s feel like a status update, can we skip them?" How do you respond and what do you change?

Primary: Lara Hogan — 1:1 resources · High Output Management (one-on-ones) · 97 Things ch 39. Lessons: 0005–0008.

M3Feedback & Radical Candor

Objective: give timely, specific praise and correction that people can act on — and take feedback well yourself.

Decision drill: An engineer keeps interrupting others in standup. Write the exact feedback you would give, out loud.

Primary: Radical Candor · Lara Hogan — feedback resources.

Phase 2 · Months 4–6 · Growing people

Move from steering one conversation to growing a person over time — and to choosing who joins the team.

M4Coaching, Delegation & Career Growth

Objective: help people grow through delegation and coaching without becoming the bottleneck.

Decision drill: You could fix a bug in 10 minutes; a junior would take a day and learn a lot. Ship date is in a week. Choose.

Primary: The Manager's Path (managing people) · Lara Hogan (sponsorship).

M5Motivation & Performance Management

Objective: understand what drives people, and handle underperformance early, fairly, and humanely.

Decision drill: A once-strong engineer has slipped for two months. What do you do first — and what would be a mistake?

Primary: High Output Management · The Manager's Path · Radical Candor.

M6Hiring, Interviewing & Onboarding

Objective: run a fair, structured hiring loop and onboard a new hire so they succeed fast.

Decision drill: Two interviewers disagree strongly on a candidate. How do you run the debrief to a fair decision?

Primary: The Manager's Path (hiring) · An Elegant Puzzle · Google re:Work — hiring.

Phase 3 · Months 7–9 · The team & delivery

Zoom out from individuals to the team as a system: its health, its output, and its technical direction.

M7Team Health, Psychological Safety & Culture

Objective: build a team where people feel safe to speak up — the top predictor of team performance.

Decision drill: In a retro, nobody raises real problems. What does that tell you, and what do you change next week?

Primary: Google re:Work (Project Aristotle) · Peopleware.

M8Execution, Planning & Delivery

Objective: help the team ship predictably, and explain delivery to leadership with evidence.

Decision drill: Leadership wants a firm date; the work is genuinely uncertain. What do you commit to?

Primary: DORA / Accelerate · An Elegant Puzzle · High Output Management.

M9Technical Leadership as a Manager

Objective: stay technical enough to earn trust and make good calls — without doing all the work yourself.

Decision drill: A senior engineer wants a 3-week refactor; product wants features. How do you decide and explain it?

Primary: StaffEng · Google SRE book · An Elegant Puzzle.

Phase 4 · Months 10–12 · Scaling your impact

Manage up and out, shape the org, manage yourself — and land the role.

M10Managing Up, Stakeholders & Communication

Objective: build a strong relationship with your boss and stakeholders, and communicate with influence.

Decision drill: Your skip-level asks, in front of others, why a project is late. How do you answer?

Primary: The Manager's Path · The Pragmatic Engineer.

M11Strategy, Org Design & Scaling Teams

Objective: think one level up — how teams are shaped, how work is split, and how Conway's law bites.

Decision drill: Your team of 9 is slowing down and stepping on each other. Split it — and how?

Primary: Team Topologies · An Elegant Puzzle.

M12Managing Yourself + Landing the Role

Objective: keep yourself effective and sustainable — then make the case and get the EM role.

Decision drill: In an EM interview: "Tell me about a time you gave hard feedback." Tell the story in 90 seconds.

Primary: Managing Humans (Rands) · The First 90 Days.
