Month 1 · Lesson 1 · The Role

The Tech Lead Role — Leading by Influence

The one idea the whole year rests on. Get this, and every other tech-lead skill has a reason to exist.

Your win today: you'll be able to say, in one sentence, how a Tech Lead is different from a senior engineer and from a manager — and why your power now comes from influence, not from a title.

Three roles, not one ladder

When a strong senior engineer looks "up", it can seem like one ladder: senior → lead → manager. It isn't. There is a fork. A Tech Lead and an Engineering Manager are two different jobs, and you have chosen the technical one.1

What a Tech Lead actually is

A useful one-line definition, from Pat Kua, who helped define the role: a Tech Lead is a senior engineer who is still responsible for a system's technical direction and quality, while also leading the people building it — and who keeps a hand in the code.2

The key shift is the same one every leader makes, but with a twist. As a senior engineer, your value was what you build. As a Tech Lead, your value becomes the quality of what the whole team builds — its architecture, its standards, its speed. This is called leverage: one good decision you shape can improve months of other people's work.

Andrew Grove · High Output Management

"A manager's output = the output of their organization + the output of the neighboring organizations under their influence."

That formula is true for a Tech Lead too — but read the last word: influence. That is the twist. A manager can, in the end, decide. A Tech Lead almost never can. You have no direct reports and no authority. If the team follows your architecture, it is because they trust your judgment, not because they must. So every skill in this year is really one skill wearing different clothes: how do I make the right technical thing happen when I can't order anyone to do it?3

The shapes technical leadership takes

"Technical leader" is not one thing. Will Larson's StaffEng describes four archetypes. Knowing them helps you see what your team needs from you right now:4

ArchetypeWhat they mostly do
Tech LeadGuides one team's technical direction and execution. Your entry point — this course's focus.
ArchitectOwns the direction and quality of a whole critical area, across teams.
SolverDrops into the hardest, gnarliest problems and untangles them.
Right HandActs as an extension of a senior leader, running big efforts.
Reframe You are not leaving engineering. You are changing what you engineer — from a feature to a team that builds features well. Architecture, a design system, a performance culture: these are things you build, and they need a real engineer to build them.

The trap waiting for you

The ex-strong-engineer's classic failure is the bottleneck: you keep the hardest, most interesting work for yourself, or every decision waits for you. It feels responsible and productive. But the team stalls whenever you're busy, no one else grows, and your influence never scales past your own two hands. Your calendar being the critical path is a bug, not a badge.5

Watch out The move that feels good right now — grabbing the hard ticket, rewriting someone's PR your way, being the hero who saves every incident — is usually the move that keeps the team dependent on you. Real leverage is making the team good without you in the room.

Practice: read the situation, pick the move

Leadership is judgment under real conditions. So every lesson ends with a drill: a true-to-life situation, then a choice. Decide out loud before you look, then check.

Scenario

You have no title, but you can see the team is about to standardize on a state-management library you believe is the wrong fit — it will slow them down within six months. You cannot overrule anyone. The decision is basically being made in tomorrow's standup.

What is your most effective first move?

Say it out loud

The highest-value practice for the interview and the promo case is being able to say the core idea cleanly. Answer this aloud, then flip the card and compare.

Remember this
  • Senior IC → Tech Lead → Manager is not one ladder — Tech Lead and Manager are a fork into two different jobs.
  • A Tech Lead owns a team's technical direction and quality and still codes (roughly 30–70%).
  • Your impact is now the team's output, not your own — that's leverage (Grove's formula).
  • But you have no authority: people follow you because they trust your judgment, so the meta-skill is leading by influence.
  • Technical leadership has shapes (StaffEng: Tech Lead, Architect, Solver, Right Hand) — Tech Lead is your entry point.
  • The main trap for an ex-strong-IC is the bottleneck: keeping the hard work and decisions to yourself.

If you remember one thing: your job is to make the right technical thing happen when you can't order anyone to do it.
