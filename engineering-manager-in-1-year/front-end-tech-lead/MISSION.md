# Mission: Become a Front-End Tech Lead within 1 year

## Why
Lam is a strong senior individual-contributor engineer (frontend + backend, TypeScript)
with no direct reports. The goal is to be **ready and credible to step into a
Front-End Tech Lead role within 12 months** — the person who owns the technical
direction of a front-end team and grows the people around them through influence,
not by being their boss. This is not "become a manager"; it is "be the obvious
technical leader a front-end team looks to." Whether the role arrives by internal
promotion or a move to a new company, Lam should be the natural choice.

A Tech Lead is different from an Engineering Manager on purpose:
- **Still hands-on in code** — a Tech Lead keeps writing code, roughly 30–70% of the time.
- **Leads by influence, not authority** — usually no direct reports; the power comes from
  technical credibility, clear writing, and trust, not from a reporting line.
- **Owns the technical direction** — architecture, standards, quality, performance,
  accessibility, and the long-term health of the front-end.

## Success looks like
- Explain in one sentence how a Tech Lead differs from a senior IC and from a manager.
- Drive a real technical decision to a clear conclusion **without having authority** — by
  writing a short RFC (a written proposal), hearing objections, and getting the team aligned.
- Own a front-end architecture choice (rendering strategy, state, data fetching, component
  structure) and defend the tradeoffs to engineers and to product.
- Diagnose and fix a real performance problem using evidence — the Core Web Vitals
  (LCP, INP, CLS) — not guesses.
- Raise the quality bar for a team: a shared design system, a testing strategy, and an
  accessibility (WCAG) baseline — and get them adopted without a mandate.
- Mentor engineers and run a code-review culture that teaches instead of gate-keeps.
- Read a real technical-leadership scenario and pick the **most effective** move, and say
  why the other options are worse.
- Have concrete evidence of technical leadership (led an architecture, drove a performance
  win, ran a migration, mentored someone) for a Tech Lead / Staff interview or promo case.

## Constraints
- **Time:** 6–10 hours per week, alongside a full-time engineering job. Aggressive pace is fine.
- **Starting point:** senior IC, no formal authority; leads informally at best.
- **Target:** broad Front-End Tech Lead foundations that work in a startup/scale-up **or** a
  larger company (specialise later). Front-end is the domain; the leadership is general.
- **Format:** matches the other courses — an `index.html` "Map" as the single source of
  truth, HTML lessons, quick-reference docs, and learning records. Judgment-first,
  scenario-driven (read a situation → pick the best move → know why the rest are worse).
- **Practical over theoretical:** every lesson must include a real use case to act on this
  week, even with no reports.

## Relationship to the parent Engineering Manager course
This course lives in a sub-folder of the Engineering Manager course but is **separate and
self-contained**. The two share a mindset (your impact is now through others) but aim at
different roles: the EM track leads by **authority** and does people-ops; this track leads by
**influence** and owns **front-end technical direction**. The mindset lessons overlap; the
skills diverge. See `learning-records/0001-*` for the reasoning.

## Out of scope (for now — protects focus)
- **Full people management** (hiring loops, performance reviews, PIPs, headcount, org design).
  A Tech Lead touches some of this lightly; the heavy version is the Engineering Manager track.
- Deep backend / infrastructure architecture beyond what a front-end lead must coordinate on
  (API contracts, backend-for-frontend boundaries).
- Becoming a people-manager instead — this workspace assumes the **technical leadership**
  track (Tech Lead → Staff+) is the goal.
- Company- or country-specific HR and compliance detail.
