The 12-Month Plan

One page that holds the whole year. Four phases, twelve monthly modules. Tick each skill as you can do it — not when you have read about it.

How to use this: The Map tells the story; this page tracks what to tick off. Each month has a small number of skills and one decision drill — a real situation where you pick the best move, usually without the authority to just decide. Your ticks are saved in this browser automatically.

Pacing at 6–10 hrs/week Roughly: ~2 hrs reading the primary source, ~2 hrs on the lesson + drills, ~2–4 hrs applying it at work (write an RFC, run a performance audit, propose a design-system rule, review a PR to teach). The plan works before you have the title — each month has a "do it now" version using influence, writing, and real front-end work.

Phase 1 · Months 1–3 · Become a lead without a title

Learn what a Tech Lead is, how to move a group when you can't order anyone, and how to grow people through mentoring and code review.

M1The Tech Lead Role — Leading by Influence

Objective: understand what a Tech Lead is (and is not), how it differs from a senior IC and a manager, and that your power now comes from influence and technical credibility, not authority.

Decision drill: You have no title, but you see the team about to pick a state-management library you think is wrong. You cannot order anyone. What is your most effective move?

Primary: Pat Kua, The Definition of a Tech Lead · The Manager's Path (Tech Lead chapter) · StaffEng. Lesson: 0001.

M2Influence, Decisions & Communication

Objective: drive a technical decision to a clear conclusion without authority — through clear writing, listening to objections, and building alignment.

Decision drill: Two senior engineers deadlock on REST vs GraphQL for a new feature and the deadline is close. How do you drive a decision without authority?

Primary: The Staff Engineer's Path (Reilly) · StaffEng · The Pragmatic Engineer (RFCs/design docs).

M3Technical Mentoring & Code-Review Culture

Objective: grow engineers through mentoring and reviews that teach — raising the bar without gate-keeping or becoming the bottleneck.

Decision drill: A junior keeps opening PRs with the same accessibility mistakes. You could just fix them in review. What is the higher-leverage move?

Primary: The Manager's Path (mentoring) · Radical Candor · Google Code Review guide.

Phase 2 · Months 4–6 · Own the front-end technical direction

Move from leading conversations to owning the architecture, speed, and consistency of the front-end.

M4Front-End Architecture & Rendering

Objective: own the big front-end choices — how pages render, where state lives, how data is fetched, and how components are structured — and defend the tradeoffs.

Decision drill: Product wants a highly interactive dashboard AND great SEO and fast first load. Which rendering strategy, and how do you explain the tradeoff?

Primary: patterns.dev (Osmani & Hallie) · React docs · Next.js docs.

M5Web Performance & Core Web Vitals

Objective: diagnose and fix real front-end performance problems with evidence — the Core Web Vitals — not guesses, and make speed a team habit.

Decision drill: The homepage LCP is 4.2s and leadership says "make it fast." What is your first move — and what would be a mistake?

Primary: web.dev — Core Web Vitals (Google) · patterns.dev (performance).

M6Design Systems & Component Libraries

Objective: build (or lead) a shared design system and get it adopted across teams — an influence problem wearing a technical hat.

Decision drill: Three teams each built their own button component. You want one shared design system. How do you get adoption without a mandate?

Primary: Brad Frost — Atomic Design · Storybook · Design Tokens (W3C).

Phase 3 · Months 7–9 · Quality, accessibility & delivery

Raise the bar for the whole team's output: tests that give confidence, UI everyone can use, and predictable shipping.

M7Testing Strategy & Front-End Quality

Objective: set a front-end testing strategy that gives real confidence without slowing the team, and lead the team to adopt it.

Decision drill: A team ships fast but bugs keep reaching production, and they resist tests ("they slow us down"). What strategy do you push, and how?

Primary: Kent C. Dodds — Testing Trophy · Playwright.

M8Accessibility & Inclusive UI

Objective: make accessibility a team baseline, not a last-minute fix — and know enough WCAG to lead it credibly.

Decision drill: A designer's beautiful new modal fails keyboard and screen-reader use, and the ship date is tomorrow. What do you do?

Primary: web.dev — Learn Accessibility · W3C WAI / WCAG.

M9Planning & Delivery for a Front-End Domain

Objective: help the team ship predictably and explain delivery with evidence — without owning people-management.

Decision drill: Leadership wants a firm date for a big redesign that is genuinely uncertain. What do you commit to?

Primary: DORA / Accelerate · An Elegant Puzzle (Larson) · The Pragmatic Engineer.

Phase 4 · Months 10–12 · Scale technical impact & land the role

Grow beyond one codebase: shape how the front-end scales, lead big changes, and make the case for the role.

M10Scaling the Front-End — Boundaries, Monorepos & Micro-Frontends

Objective: reason about how front-end work is split as it grows — module boundaries, monorepos, and when micro-frontends help or hurt.

Decision drill: A 9-person front-end team keeps stepping on each other in one big repo. Do you split into micro-frontends? How do you decide?

Primary: Micro Frontends (Fowler / Cam Jackson) · Team Topologies · Nx / Turborepo.

M11Technical Strategy & Leading Change

Objective: write a simple front-end technical strategy and lead a risky change (a migration or big upgrade) to a safe landing.

Decision drill: You want to migrate from a 6-year-old framework to a modern one. It is risky and long. How do you make the case and reduce the risk?

Primary: lethain.com / StaffEng (engineering strategy) · The Staff Engineer's Path (Reilly).

M12Managing Yourself + Landing the Tech Lead Role

Objective: stay effective as a player-coach, and make the case that gets you the Tech Lead (or Staff) role.

Decision drill: In a Tech Lead / Staff interview: "Tell me about a time you drove a technical decision without authority." Tell the story in 90 seconds.

Primary: The Staff Engineer's Path (Reilly) · StaffEng (promo packets) · Julia Evans — brag documents.
