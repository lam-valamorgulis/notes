Day 7 · Lesson 20 · The rehearsal

The full mock

Everything from six days, in one 90-minute run. Out loud, on a clock, recorded. This is the only lesson that cannot be done by reading.

Your win today: you have said every answer out loud at least once, under time pressure, and you know exactly which three answers are still weak.

How to run it

  1. Out loud. Reading the questions and thinking "I know that" is the trap this whole day exists to prevent. Fluency in your head is not fluency in your mouth.
  2. Recorded. Your phone. You will listen back to two minutes of it, not all of it.
  3. On a clock. 30 seconds for rapid-fire, 90 seconds for a story, 25–30 minutes for a kata.
  4. Mark each answer as you go: ✓ solid · ~ got there slowly · ✗ could not answer.
  5. At the end, take your five worst and only those. Re-answer them tomorrow morning.
Better: get the questions asked to you Say "run the full mock on me" to your teacher. You get them one at a time, in interviewer voice, with follow-ups when an answer is thin — which is closer to the real thing than a list you can scan ahead in.

Round 1 · Warm-up (5 minutes)

  1. Tell me about yourself. (60 seconds — see lesson 12)
  2. What are you working on at the moment?
  3. Why are you looking to move?
  4. What kind of front-end work do you want more of?
  5. What do you know about what we do?

Round 2 · React core — rapid fire (30 seconds each)

  1. What happens when I call setState?
  2. Why does setCount(count + 1) three times only add one?
  3. Is the virtual DOM faster than the DOM?
  4. What is a key for, and why not the array index?
  5. When do you use useEffect?
  6. My effect runs twice in development. Why?
  7. What is a stale closure?
  8. Name three effects that should be deleted.
  9. useRef versus useState?
  10. Why can't a hook be inside an if?
  11. What is a custom hook, and what does it share?
  12. Controlled versus uncontrolled input?
  13. What is useSyncExternalStore for?

Round 3 · State management (60 seconds each)

  1. Explain Redux to a junior developer.
  2. What does Redux Toolkit add over plain Redux?
  3. What is Immer doing inside createSlice?
  4. How do you handle async in Redux?
  5. Can Context replace Redux?
  6. Why does my whole app re-render when the auth context changes?
  7. Redux, Zustand or Context — how do you choose?
  8. When would you not use Redux at all?

Round 4 · Data & APIs (60 seconds each)

  1. Describe your API layer.
  2. Why does fetch not throw on a 500?
  3. Walk me through a 401 token refresh.
  4. What is a race condition in a search box, and how do you fix it?
  5. What is CORS and how do you fix it?
  6. Server state versus client state?
  7. Implement an optimistic update. What can go wrong?

Round 5 · CSS, performance, build (60 seconds each)

  1. Flexbox or grid?
  2. How do you approach responsive design?
  3. How would you keep CSS maintainable on a large product?
  4. How do you optimise a slow React app?
  5. Should you wrap everything in useMemo?
  6. What does React.memo compare?
  7. Webpack versus Vite?
  8. Our production bundle is 4 MB. What do you do?
  9. Is an API key safe in a front-end env var?

Round 6 · The project deep-dive (10 minutes)

This is the round that decides the interview. It is one question with three levels, and you prepared all three in lesson 11.

LevelThe questionWhat is being tested
1"Tell me about the most technically difficult thing you've built."Can you compress a complex system into 90 seconds?
2"Give me a specific bug from that and how you fixed it."Were you actually there, or are you describing a diagram?
3"What would you do differently now?"Do you have judgement, or only memory?
Trap — the answer that dies at level 2 Plenty of candidates describe an architecture beautifully and then cannot name one real bug. It reads as second-hand knowledge, and everything they said before it gets re-weighted. Have two specific bugs ready with the mechanism, the symptom, and the fix. Yours are the device-change race and the stale mute state.

Round 7 · Behavioural (90 seconds each)

  1. Tell me about a disagreement with a teammate.
  2. Tell me about something you broke.
  3. What do you look for in a code review?
  4. A reviewer asks for a change you think is wrong. What do you do?
  5. How do you work with backend engineers?
  6. A task turns out to be much bigger than estimated. What now?
  7. How do you work across time zones?
  8. How do you handle a ticket with unclear requirements?

The night before

Do not learn anything new. Re-read your three stories and the "Remember this" card at the bottom of each lesson. Check the camera, the microphone, the link and the room. Sleep. A tired candidate who knows nine things beats an exhausted one who half-knows twenty.

Remember this
  • Out loud, recorded, on a clock. Reading the questions is not practice.
  • Mark every answer ✓ ~ ✗ and only work on the five worst.
  • Round 6 — the project deep-dive — is worth more than all the trivia rounds together.
  • Have two specific bugs ready. An architecture with no bugs sounds second-hand.
  • Three behavioural stories is enough: the hard thing, the disagreement, the failure.
  • In the kata, never go silent for more than about ten seconds.
  • The night before: no new material. Stories, recap cards, equipment check, sleep.

If you remember one thing: you are not being tested on recall — you are being tested on whether you can think out loud.
