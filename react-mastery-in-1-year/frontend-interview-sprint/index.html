The big picture — how it all connects

The job description asks for seven things: React, modern JavaScript, HTML and CSS, state management, REST integration, build tools, and the ability to work with a distributed team in English. You have shipped all of it. The gap this week closes is not knowledge — it is retrieval under pressure. So every day ends with something said out loud, and half of the material is drills rather than reading.

The week has a shape. Days 1 to 3 rebuild the answers to the questions that always come first: what actually happens when you call setState, what an effect is for, how to explain Redux rather than just use it, and why most "global state" is really a cache. Day 4 is the one that decides the interview: your real-time video product, turned into an architecture you can draw, two bugs you can describe, and a ninety-second story. Most candidates have a CRUD app; this is material nobody can copy from you. Day 5 covers the craft around React — performance, CSS, the build. Day 6 is the half of the job description people skip: code review, working across time zones, and English when your English deserts you. Day 7 is the full mock — nothing new, everything out loud.

Running underneath it are four timed katas. Live coding is a performance skill, not a knowledge skill, so each one has a clock, a talking script, and a checklist. Run them twice. The second run is the one that tells you the truth.
