The story of the tree

Everything a browser does for your users rests on three layers of physics. A single-threaded engine runs your code one call at a time — the call stack, its memory, its event loop, its types (Stratum 1 · Language & Runtime, 32 cores). That code exists to change pixels, and pixels come out of a fixed pipeline — parse, style, layout, paint, composite — with a 16.67ms budget per frame (Stratum 2 · Rendering, 26 cores). And nothing arrives to run or render at all except over a network you must treat as slow, flaky, and expensive (Stratum 3 · Network & Delivery, 26 cores).

On top of that physics sits every framework you will ever use. Reactivity, component models, state, routing, rendering strategies, build systems — these are not magic; they are engineering answers to Strata 1–3 (Stratum 4 · Architecture, 30 cores). Once an app works, four constraint layers separate it from one you can ship: it must work for every human (Stratum 5 · Platform & Users, 20 cores), survive hostile input (Stratum 6 · Security, 12 cores), be testable, observable, and debuggable by a team (Stratum 7 · Engineering Practice, 17 cores), and — new to the job description — integrate probabilistic AI services without letting them wreck determinism, cost, or trust (Stratum 8 · AI Integration, 12 cores).

Traversal is depth-first from Stratum 1: physics before architecture, architecture before constraints. We are at the very first leaf.
