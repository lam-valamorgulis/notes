The Track B Interview Drill

"We've bought a platform like stockinstore — integrate it." That question has a script, the same way "design stockinstore" does. This is the script, the rapid-fire drill, and the six answers that mark someone as junior.

Your win today: deliver the integration answer end to end — a 60-second opener, a seven-step whiteboard script, an eight-sentence summary, and correct answers to eighteen follow-ups. Then score yourself against a rubric you can apply honestly.

The build-it capstone is Lesson 10. This is its Track B twin. If you only have one evening, drill whichever track the client actually described — and if you don't know which, §1 is how you find out in one sentence.

1. Three question shapes, and how to spot them

What they sayWhat they're askingYour opening move
"Design a system like stockinstore"Build it — the engine is the answerGo to Lesson 10's seven steps.
"We use stockinstore / we've bought a platform — how would you integrate it?"Track B — the plumbing is the answerThe script in §3.
"Should we build this or buy it?"Judgement, with criteriaLesson 19 — and finish with what would change your mind.
The one clarifying question, asked once "Before I design — are we building the availability platform, or integrating one the business has already bought? The engineering is quite different, and I want to answer the right one." Ask it once, early, then commit. Asking it shows you know there are two answers. Asking it three times looks like stalling.

2. The 60-second opener

Say this first, then draw

"Right — so the vendor owns the omnichannel logic: availability rules, reservations and order allocation. What we own is the retailer-side plumbing, and that's where integrations actually fail. There are four connections plus one thing that isn't a connection at all. The connections: stock in from their POS or ERP to the vendor; the storefront surfaces, meaning the widget and the pickup step in checkout; the webhooks coming back; and the write-back of stock and the sale to the POS and the ledger. The thing that isn't a connection is the identifier mapping — Shopify, each POS and the vendor all name the same shoe and the same shop differently, and if that's wrong nothing else matters, because the widget just says 'not available nearby' and nobody gets an error. So I'd design the connector and the mapping properly first, place the surfaces carefully, close the loop with idempotent write-back and reconciliation, and treat the vendor as a dependency I have to survive — timeouts, a circuit breaker, and a documented fallback per surface. Shall I draw it?"

3. The seven-step whiteboard script

┌── STEP 1 ── Frame the split (60s) │ vendor owns: availability · reservations · allocation │ I own: feed in · mapping · surfaces · webhooks · write-back │ ├── STEP 2 ── Draw the four connections + the registry in the middle │ POS/ERP ──feed──► [MY CONNECTOR] ──► VENDOR ◄──widget── SHOPIFY │ ▲ ──webhooks──► me │ [MAPPING REGISTRY] ──write-back──► POS │ ├── STEP 3 ── The connector, in detail ← spend the most time here │ adapter per source · normalise · SHADOW TABLE · absolute sends │ micro-batch · token bucket · retry w/ jitter · DLQ │ 3 triggers: event · micro-batch · scheduled reconciliation │ ├── STEP 4 ── The mapping registry │ hub not pairwise · alias table per namespace · valid_from/valid_to │ match ladder: id → barcode → exact SKU → normalised → human │ coverage weighted by UNITS · unmapped report with an owner │ ├── STEP 5 ── The Shopify surfaces │ app block (target "section") for the PDP widget · variant-change bridge │ native local pickup (all plans) · checkout-step UI ext = PLUS only │ cart attribute carries the chosen store → order.attributes │ ├── STEP 6 ── Close the loop │ 4 webhook streams · 2 signature schemes · ack in 5s · outbox │ subscription reconciler (8 failures / ~4h = deleted) │ write-back: stock movement vs SALE · DERIVED idempotency key │ 3-way reconciliation, winner per fact │ └── STEP 7 ── Survive the vendor 1.5s timeout · circuit breaker returns UNKNOWN not ZERO fail OPEN where they add information, fail CLOSED where they promise Level 2 protected customer data · DPA · retention in days façade in front of the vendor = one place to swap them
Where to spend your minutes Steps 3 and 4 are half your time. Everyone can name "install the app and place the widget"; almost nobody designs the connector and the registry properly. Step 5 is where you drop two or three exact platform facts to show you have actually built on Shopify. Step 7 is what separates a senior answer from a competent one — and it takes ninety seconds.

4. The eight-sentence summary

The whole answer, compressed — memorise this

"One. The vendor owns availability, reservations and allocation; I own the retailer-side plumbing, and that's where integrations fail. Two. The connector is the real build: a relay from the POS or ERP into the vendor's API, and because I can't inspect or repair their state I send absolute quantities rather than deltas and keep a hashed shadow table of what I last sent — which gives me change suppression, a staleness report, proof of what I sent, and a clean resend after an outage. Three. I run three triggers — event-driven, micro-batched, and a scheduled full reconciliation, because feeds always drop messages and nothing else repairs silent drift. Four. Identifier mapping gets its own component: every namespace mapped to one canonical key I own, a match ladder from explicit id to barcode to exact SKU to normalised, fuzzy matching only ever suggesting to a human, and coverage reported weighted by units — because 97% of fifty thousand SKUs is fifteen hundred silently invisible products. Five. On the storefront I use a theme app extension app block targeting section, bridge the theme's variant change because the block renders the variant once on the server, and hold the widget to a reserved-space, deferred, time-boxed performance budget. Six. Click & Collect is three layers with three plan answers — native local pickup on all plans, checkout-step UI extensions Plus-only, and the vendor's own C&C product possibly Plus-only too — and pickup fulfilment needs write_merchant_managed_fulfillment_orders, with any code reading fulfillmentOrders[0] now broken because an order can mix shipping and pickup. Seven. I close the loop with four webhook streams and two signature schemes, ack inside five seconds via an outbox, run a subscription reconciler because Shopify deletes a subscription after eight consecutive failures over about four hours, write back with a derived idempotency key — separating the repairable stock movement from the unrepairable sale — and reconcile three ways with a documented winner per fact. Eight. And I treat the vendor as a dependency I must survive: a hard timeout and a breaker that returns unknown rather than zero, fail-open where they add information and fail-closed where they make a promise, Level 2 protected-customer-data obligations named explicitly, and my own thin API façade in front of them so swapping or in-sourcing later changes one layer."

5. Rapid-fire drill — eighteen follow-ups

Cover the answers. Say each one out loud before you look. Anything you cannot say in two sentences, go back to its lesson.

#QuestionThe answer, in one or two sentences
1"We do the integration" — so what's left for you?They integrate their logic. Nobody at the vendor has credentials to the retailer's till in Ballarat, so the connector, the mapping, the surfaces and the write-back are all mine.
2Why absolute quantities, not deltas?A lost or duplicated absolute send self-heals on the next push; a lost delta is permanent silent drift and a duplicate is wrong by one. I can't inspect or repair the vendor's state, so I choose the naturally idempotent message.
3The POS only reports movements. Now what?Apply the delta to my own shadow count and send the resulting absolute number. The delta stays inside one system; the absolute crosses the boundary.
4Why the shadow table?Change suppression by payload hash, a per-location staleness report, proof of what I sent when the retailer disputes it, and a targeted resend after an outage. Written only on a 2xx.
5What if you write the shadow row before the send succeeds?Suppression then hides that row forever — I believe the vendor knows something it never received. Confirmed sends only.
6Shopify's Admin API rate limit?GraphQL is cost-based on a leaky bucket: 100 points/second Standard, 200 Advanced, 1000 Plus, 2000 Enterprise, and no single query over 1,000 points. Bulk operations are exempt, so they're the tool for the first full load.
7Can you join on SKU?Carefully. ProductVariant.sku is case-sensitive and Shopify guarantees no uniqueness, so a join can return two variants or none. I count duplicates and blanks first and match down a ladder.
8Where does the retailer's store code live in Shopify?Nowhere by default — Location has no store-code field. It lives in my registry, or in a metafield on the location. Never matched on name, because shops get renamed.
9Coverage is 97%. Ship it?No. On 50,000 SKUs that's 1,500 products silently showing "not available nearby" with no error anywhere. The bar is ≥99.5% of units mapped with a named owner for the remainder.
10App block or app embed block for the PDP widget?App block, target: "section" — inline, placed by the merchant, Online Store 2.0. App embeds target head/compliance_head/body, are global, and are the only one of the two that supports vintage themes.
11Merchant installed it and nothing appeared.Documented default: app blocks are disabled and app embeds deactivated after install. It's a step in the rollout plan, not a bug.
12Widget shows stock for the wrong size.The block rendered selected_or_first_available_variant once on the server. I bridge the theme's variant-change event with a ?variant= fallback, because there's no single cross-theme event.
13Does Click & Collect need Shopify Plus?Three answers. Native local pickup: all plans. Checkout UI extensions on the information/shipping/payment steps: Plus only. And the vendor's own C&C app may itself target Plus — a commercial gate, not a platform one.
14Which scope marks a pickup order ready?fulfillmentOrderLineItemsPreparedForPickup needs write_merchant_managed_fulfillment_orders, because a retail store is a merchant-managed location. The narrower-sounding assigned scope is for a registered fulfillment service.
15What broke in 2026 for pickup integrations?Ship and pickup can now be in one order on Plus and Enterprise. Nothing errors — code reading fulfillmentOrders[0] or a single order-level isPickup flag just gets half the order right.
16Webhooks stopped arriving.Shopify deletes a subscription after eight consecutive failures over about four hours. A boot-time and daily reconciler recreates missing topics, and the recreation itself is an alert — there's a window of lost events to recover.
17Vendor API times out. What does the shopper see?On the PDP, fail open: the widget hides or says availability is temporarily unavailable, and add-to-cart still works. At checkout, fail closed: pickup disappears, shipping stays. And the breaker returns unknown, never zero.
18How do you avoid being locked in?Lock-in is the shape of the data, not the contract. Registry in my database, store code on every order as a cart attribute, export and deletion terms signed up front, and a thin façade so swapping changes one layer.

6. Six answers that mark someone as junior

The junior answerWhy it failsWhat you say instead
"It's a vendor app, so there's not much engineering."It's the classic integration-project failure, and an interviewer is often testing exactly this."The vendor removes the engine, not the plumbing — and the plumbing is 40–60% of the work."
"We just join on SKU."SKU is a free-text field with no uniqueness guarantee, case-sensitive, sometimes blank."A match ladder — explicit id, barcode, exact SKU, normalised SKU — and a human for the rest."
"We push the change events to the vendor."A lost delta is permanent silent drift with no error."Absolute state, plus scheduled reconciliation, because feeds always drop messages."
"If the vendor is down we show out of stock."Renders unknown as zero and quietly destroys conversion."Unknown is not zero. Fail open on the PDP, fail closed at checkout."
"We paste their snippet into the theme."Theme-code edits don't survive theme upgrades and don't clean up on uninstall."A theme app extension app block, placed in the theme editor."
"Privacy is the vendor's problem — they have a DPA."Pickup PII is Level 2 protected customer data and the retailer remains accountable."Level 2, so I ask for the retention period in days and whether they keep an access log — and I design my connector to hold no PII at all."

7. Score yourself honestly

LevelWhat it sounds like
Not readyYou describe installing an app and placing a widget. The connector and the mapping don't appear as components.
CompetentYou name all four connections plus mapping, draw the diagram, and know idempotency matters. Platform details are vague.
StrongYou design the connector and registry in detail, argue absolute-over-delta from failure cases, and quote at least three exact Shopify facts correctly.
SeniorAll of the above, plus: fail-open vs fail-closed per surface, Level 2 protected customer data named unprompted, the mixed ship-and-pickup order change, the façade for reversibility, and acceptance criteria as numbers — including two owned by retail operations.
The three things a senior says that nobody prompts for (1) "Unknown is not zero." (2) "Coverage weighted by units, because 97% is fifteen hundred invisible products." (3) "Two of my acceptance criteria belong to retail operations, because this feature dies in the shops if nobody picks the orders." Any one of those, unprompted, changes how the rest of the conversation goes.
