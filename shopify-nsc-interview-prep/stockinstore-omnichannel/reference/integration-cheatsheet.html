Cheat Sheet — "Integrate a platform like stockinstore"

One page to skim before the interview: the split, the connection points, every exact platform number, and the failure table. Full detail in Lessons 11–20.

Lead with the split: "The vendor owns availability, reservations and allocation. I own the retailer-side plumbing — the stock feed, the identifier mapping, the storefront surfaces and the write-back — and that's where integrations actually fail."

DRAW POS/ERP → [connector] → vendor ← widget ← Shopify ▲ [mapping registry] → webhooks → me → POS 3. REGISTRY hub not pairwise · alias per namespace · valid_from/valid_to ladder: id → barcode → exact SKU → normalised → human coverage weighted by UNITS · unmapped report with an owner 5. SURFACES app block target "section" · variant-change bridge local pickup = ALL plans · checkout-STEP UI ext = PLUS cart attribute carries the chosen store → order.attributes 6. LOOP 4 webhook streams · 2 signature schemes · ack in 5s · outbox subscription reconciler · stock movement vs SALE DERIVED idempotency key · 3-way reconciliation 7. SURVIVE 1.5s timeout · breaker returns UNKNOWN not ZERO fail OPEN on information, fail CLOSED on a promise Level 2 protected customer data · façade for reversibility Steps 3 and 4 = half your time. Decisions that win

  • Absolute, never delta, across any boundary you can't inspect — a lost delta is permanent silent drift; a lost absolute heals on the next send.
  • Shadow table, written only on 2xx: change suppression by payload hash, staleness report, proof of what you sent, targeted resend.
  • Derived idempotency keys, never uuid() — a retry must collide, not insert.
  • Hub mapping, not pairwise. 3 systems = 3 mappings, not 6. Close mappings with valid_to; never DELETE.
  • Coverage weighted by units. 97% of 50,000 SKUs = 1,500 silently invisible products and no alert.
  • Fuzzy matching only suggests. 99% accurate on 50,000 SKUs = 500 wrong products with no error.
  • Unknown ≠ zero. Type the availability result as nullable so a timeout can never render as "out of stock".
  • Fail open on information, fail closed on a promise. Widget degrades; pickup selection disappears.
  • Stock write-back is repairable; the sale is not. Different idempotency rigour, different failure policy.
  • Source of truth per fact: POS owns physical stock · vendor owns reservations · Shopify owns the order and the money · you own the mapping.
  • Your own façade in front of the vendor: one place for timeout, breaker, cache and metrics — and swapping them later changes one layer.
  • Two acceptance criteria belong to retail operations — pickup-ready SLA and unpicked-order age — or the feature dies in the shops.

"The vendor removes the engine, not the plumbing — and the plumbing is most of the risk. Relay absolute stock with a shadow table and scheduled reconciliation; treat identifier mapping as a real component and report coverage weighted by units; place the surfaces with a theme app extension and know which of the three C&C layers your plan actually gates; close the loop with idempotent, derived-key write-back that separates the repairable stock movement from the unrepairable sale; and survive the vendor with a timeout, a breaker that returns unknown rather than zero, fail-open on information and fail-closed on a promise, and my own façade so the decision stays reversible."
