stockinstore Sub-Course · Standalone Study Plan

5 Days Through the stockinstore Domain

All 20 lessons, 5 katas, and 5 reference cards are already built. This plan is the order to move through them so ideas stick — concept, then code, then recall — ending in a mock for each of the two tracks.

The plan in one line. Days 1–3 are the build-it track: the data foundation, then the transaction and reliability layers, then one whiteboard answer under a mock. Days 4–5 are Track B: integrating a platform the client already bought — the connector and mapping, the Shopify surfaces, then vendor risk, the project, build-vs-buy, and a second mock.
Short on time? Pick the track the client actually described If they said "we're building an omnichannel platform" → Days 1–3, and read L11 once so you can name the fork. If they said "we use stockinstore / we've bought a platform" → Day 1 for the domain, then jump straight to Days 4–5. If you don't know which, do Day 1 and ask them; that one question is itself a good signal in an interview. Only do all five days if you have the time or don't yet know the brief.
Why the order differs slightly from the Map index.html groups the build-it katas under Chapter 6 ("The Interview") because that is where they sit in the site's table of contents. This plan instead does each kata right after its matching lesson (K1 after L2, K2 after L4, K3 after L5, K4 after L6, K5 after L12) — coding the idea while it is fresh beats coding several ideas back-to-back at the end.

Day 1 — The domain & getting stock in~3.5–4 hours

Goal: know why store stock is hard to trust, how it gets ingested cleanly, and how a shopper's "in stock near me?" gets answered fast.

learnL1 · The omnichannel problem & domain — four products, six actors, the one hard truth. (20m)
learnL11 · Integrating Shopify with the stockinstore SaaS — the Track B map, so you can name the fork today. The depth is Days 4–5. (20m)
learnL2 · Inventory ingestion & multi-source sync — feeds → canonical model → idempotent upsert. (25m)
codeK1 · Idempotent inventory upsert, typed from blank, narrating each guard out loud. (45m)
learnL3 · The stock availability engine — on-hand vs ATP, thresholds, "call to confirm". (25m)
learnL4 · Find in Store — the read path — geospatial query + pre-computed availability + cache. (25m)
codeK2 · Find-in-store query API, typed from blank. (45m)
drillRecall check: say the ingestion pipeline and the ATP formula out loud, no notes. (15m)

Day 2 — Buying, fulfilment & reliability at scale~4–4.5 hours

Goal: know how a browse becomes a kept promise (reservation, pickup, allocation) and what keeps all of it correct under load and failure.

learnL5 · Click & Collect / BOPIS order flow — reserve, PICK_UP order, pick, notify. (25m)
codeK3 · Click & Collect reservation, atomic all-or-nothing, typed from blank. (45m)
learnL6 · Ship from Store & the OMS fairness allocation — score, prefer one shipment, reassign. (25m)
codeK4 · Order-allocation fairness scorer, typed from blank. (45m)
learnL7 · Event-driven backbone — queues, work-queue vs pub/sub, the four reliability rules. (25m)
learnL8 · Data model & consistency — schema, source-of-truth per fact, the outbox. (30m)
learnL9 · Scale, multi-tenancy & failure modes — three workloads, tenant isolation, SLOs. (30m)
drillRecall check: say the four reliability rules and one row of the failure table out loud. (15m)

Day 3 — The interview: one system, one story~3 hours

Goal: turn nine lessons of detail into one confident whiteboard answer, then prove it holds up under a mock. This is the build-it capstone; Day 5 is the Track B one.

learnL10 · "Design stockinstore" — the 7-step walkthrough and the 8-sentence summary. (40m)
drillDesign cheat sheet — say the 7 steps and 3 paths out loud, cover-and-recall. (20m)
drillData model card — recall the schema and the consistency split from memory. (15m)
drillGlossary — cover-and-recall every acronym (SKU, POS, ERP, ATP, OMS…). (15m)
drillRapid-fire Q&A — full pass, ~22 questions, answer before reveal. (35m)
drillSay "mock me on stockinstore" — a full "design stockinstore" whiteboard mock with "why?" follow-ups. (45m)
learnFix pass: re-read whichever lesson the mock exposed as thin. (20m)

Day 4 — Track B: the plumbing you actually build~4–4.5 hours

Goal: be able to design the connector and the mapping registry in detail, and place the Shopify surfaces correctly. This is where integrations fail, so it gets the most time.

learnL12 · The vendor stock-feed connector — a relay is not an ingest: absolute sends, the shadow table, rate limits, reconciliation. (35m)
codeK5 · The vendor feed connector, typed from blank, then the debug-and-fix extension. (50m)
learnL13 · Identifier mapping & the registry — hub not pairwise, the match ladder, Shopify's SKU and Location traps, coverage by units. (35m)
drillRecall check: draw the registry schema from memory, then walk the six-step "widget says no stock" diagnosis out loud. (20m)
learnL14 · The storefront surface — app blocks vs app embeds, the real limits, the variant-change bug, the performance budget. (30m)
learnL15 · Click & Collect at checkout — three layers, three plan answers, the pickup scope trap, mixed ship-and-pickup orders. (30m)
learnL16 · Webhooks, write-back & reconciliation — four streams, two signature schemes, derived idempotency keys. (30m)
drillRecall check: say the two target-value sets for the block types, and the three plan answers for C&C, with no notes. (15m)

Day 5 — Track B: the senior layer, then the mock~3.5 hours

Goal: add the things nobody prompts you for — failure policy per surface, privacy level, project discipline, build-vs-buy judgement — then deliver the whole integration answer out loud.

learnL17 · Vendor risk — scope review, Level 1 vs Level 2 protected customer data, the breaker that returns unknown, fail-open vs fail-closed. (35m)
learnL18 · The integration project — the discovery questions, the four-number audit, eight phases, cohorts by POS variety. (30m)
learnL19 · Build vs buy, lock-in & the exit — what buying really removes, the decision rules, the façade. (25m)
learnL20 · The Track B interview drill — the opener, the seven-step script, the eight-sentence summary. (35m)
drillTrack B cheat sheet — cover-and-recall every exact platform number in the table. (20m)
drillL20's rapid-fire: all 18 follow-ups, answered before reveal. Anything you can't say in two sentences, re-read its lesson. (30m)
drillSay "mock me on Track B" — the full integration mock, then score yourself against L20's rubric. (45m)
How this fits the round-2 week The main round-2 plan's Day 3 reviews "Shopify ↔ ERP sync" as an abstract design — stockinstore is that exact design made concrete. Run Days 1–3 before round-2 Day 3 (e.g. over a weekend, or as extra evening sessions) so the "it's like stockinstore" hint is fully loaded before that review. If time is short, Day 3 is the one build-it day to never skip — it turns ten lessons into one spoken answer — and Day 5 is its Track B equivalent. And if the client's brief ever says they already own a platform, Days 4–5 become the priority, not the extra.
