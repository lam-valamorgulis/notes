The Four Hard Moments

Lesson 29 teaches the habit. This page is the rehearsal. Four moments, twelve prompts, one score sheet — so that on the day, the right words come out before you have time to feel afraid.

How to use this: do not read it silently. Read one prompt, start a timer, and speak your answer to an empty room. Then read the model move. The goal is not a perfect answer — it is that your first sound is always a sentence, never silence.

This is the drill block for Day 1 of the 7-Day Plan. The depth is in Lesson 29 · Part A. Read the lesson first, then come here.

The one rule under all four moments Never go silent. A hard question is not the problem. Three seconds of nothing is the problem, because the interviewer cannot see thinking — they only see a stuck face. Every move below is a way to keep talking while your brain catches up.

Moment 1 — Buy time on a question you half-know

They ask something you know a little about, but not enough to answer straight away.
WrongA long pause, eyes up, then a rushed guess. Or the worst version: "Umm…" for five seconds.
RightSay a bridge sentence immediately, then think inside it. The bridge buys 5–15 seconds and sounds calm and structured.
Say"Let me think about this out loud for a second." · "Let me restate the problem to be sure I have it right." · "There are two ways I could go — let me weigh them quickly." · "I want to get this detail right, give me a moment to reason it through."
Drill it
  1. "This store has forty apps and the product page is slow. Where do you start?"
  2. "How exactly does Shopify calculate the cost of a GraphQL query?"
  3. "Would you put the ERP sync in the same service as the storefront API, or split it?"

Moment 2 — Climb the recovery ladder when you are stuck

You are genuinely stuck. The whole problem feels too big and you cannot see the first line of code.
WrongJumping straight to "I don't know", or trying to design the perfect full solution in your head before saying anything.
RightClimb the five rungs in order, out loud. You almost always get free by rung 3. 1 Restate · 2 Shrink it · 3 Brute force, then improve · 4 Ask a scoping question · 5 Bound the unknown.
Say"Let me start with the smallest version — one order, no retries — and add the hard parts back after." · "Let me get any working version first, then optimise. A correct slow answer beats a clever broken one." · "Do you want the failure case now, or the happy path working first?"
Drill it
  1. "Sync inventory in both directions, Shopify and the ERP, without creating an update loop."
  2. "Write something that tells us when Shopify and the ERP have drifted apart."
  3. "We need to import 800,000 historical orders. Go."

Moment 3 — The senior "I don't know"

They name an API, a tool, or a number you have honestly never touched.
WrongTwo traps, both fatal. Bluffing a confident wrong fact — a senior spots it, and now nothing else you said is trusted. Or a bare "I don't know" with a full stop, which reads as no ownership.
RightThree parts, always in this order: admit it plainly · give your mental model · say exactly how you would confirm it. Confidence about your method beats false confidence about a fact.
Say"I haven't used that exact API, so I won't pretend I have. Based on how the rest of the Admin API behaves I'd expect it to work like this… and I'd confirm it on shopify.dev and test it against a dev store before I relied on it."
Drill it
  1. "Have you worked with the Fulfillment Orders API?"
  2. "What's the rate limit on the ERP side — say it's NetSuite?"
  3. "How do Shopify Functions handle a discount that conflicts with a script?"

Moment 4 — Recover from a bug you just wrote

You are typing, and you see it: the code on the screen is wrong. They may have seen it too.
WrongA long apology, going quiet, or quietly deleting the line and hoping nobody noticed.
RightThree beats, fast. Own it in one calm sentence · explain the fix as you make it · name the check that would have caught it earlier. That third beat is the senior reflex almost nobody does.
Say"Ah — I see the bug. My handler isn't idempotent yet, so a duplicate webhook would double-apply. Let me key it on the webhook id instead. And a test that sends the same event twice would have caught this."
Drill it
  1. You deduped with an in-memory Set. Then you remember there are two workers. Recover out loud.
  2. You wrote the webhook handler and forgot to verify the HMAC. Recover out loud.
  3. Your retry loop has no maximum. Recover out loud.
The four in four words — Bridge · Shrink · Bound · Own Bridge: "Let me think out loud for a second." · Shrink: "Let me get any working version first, then improve it." · Bound: "I won't pretend I've used it — here's how I'd confirm it." · Own: "I see the bug — my handler isn't idempotent yet. Let me fix that."

The two-hour session

Run it in this order. The clock matters — pressure is part of the drill.

TimeWhat you doPass bar
0:00–0:15Say the four bridge phrases and the five ladder rungs from memory, page covered.All nine, no peeking, under two minutes.
0:15–0:55Moments 1 and 2. Three prompts each. Answer each one cold, out loud, no notes.Your first sound is a sentence every time.
0:55–1:05Break. Stand up. Do not read anything.—
1:05–1:40Moments 3 and 4. Three prompts each, same rules.Zero bare "I don't know". Zero long apologies.
1:40–2:00Record one prompt twice — once at your normal pace, once about 20% slower. Listen back.You can hear which take sounds calm. Keep that pace.

The recording check

You cannot hear your own pace while you speak. You can hear it on playback. Listen once for each line below, not all at once.

Listen forBadGood
The first second after the questionSilence, or "umm"A bridge phrase
PaceSpeeding up as the question gets harderSteady, about 20% slower than your normal talking speed
End of sentencesTone rising, so statements sound like questionsTone going down. It sounds decided.
PausesFilled with "umm", "like", "you know"Silent, short, on purpose
The "I don't know" momentsA full stopModel plus a way to confirm it
The trap of practising in your head Thinking an answer feels the same as saying it, but it is not. In your head there are no pauses, no rising tone, and no "umm". Every rep on this page must be spoken. Reading this page quietly is worth almost nothing.
