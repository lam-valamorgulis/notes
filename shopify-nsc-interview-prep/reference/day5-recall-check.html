The Day 5 Recall Check

Not a study session. A test. You have spent five days putting things in. This hour finds out what actually comes out when nothing is on the screen in front of you.

The rules — all four, no exceptions. 1. Everything closed: no notes, no lesson pages, no this page after you start a block. 2. Everything spoken out loud, in full sentences, as if someone is listening. 3. Everything on a timer. 4. You mark yourself honestly — a generous tick today is a bad surprise on the day.

This is the recall block for Day 5 of the 7-Day Plan. The source material is L26 · Be the one they pick, the STAR worksheet, and PM talk prep. Do not open any of them until the hour is over.

Why this hour is uncomfortable on purpose Re-reading feels like learning. It is not. It is recognition — the page looks familiar, so your brain says "I know this". Recall is different and harder: pulling the answer out of an empty room with a clock running. Only recall predicts what you can do in the interview. If this hour feels easy, you are cheating somewhere.

Block A — Your four differentiators20 minutes

Say the four out loud from memory first. Then, for each one, without looking: name it · two concrete moves you will actually do · one line you will say in the room.

  1. Owner mindset — you speak in outcomes and risk, not tickets.
  2. Clear communication — you can make a hard thing feel obvious to a non-engineer.
  3. Deep Shopify — you have shipped it, not read about it.
  4. Proactive and reliable — nobody ever has to chase you.

A "concrete move" is something you can watch someone do. "I communicate well" is not a move. "I restate the question in one line before I answer it" is a move.

Pass bar: all four named in under 30 seconds, and for each one you produce two moves and a line without a long pause. Eight moves, four lines, twenty minutes.

Block B — Your three STAR stories25 minutes

Say each story straight through, timed. Aim for 2 minutes. Hard stop at 2:30. After each one, tick the four boxes before you start the next.

  • S — the situation lands in one or two sentences, with no wandering start.
  • T — it is clear what you personally had to do.
  • A — the actions are specific and technical, with at least one exact detail.
  • R — the result has a number, or a clear before-and-after, plus what you would do differently.

That last part is the senior marker. A story with no reflection reads as "I did a task". A story with a trade-off and a "what I'd change" reads as someone who has run things.

Pass bar: three stories, each under 2:30, each with all four boxes ticked, none of them started with "so, basically…".

Block C — The frame: open, position, ask, close15 minutes

These four are the bookends of the interview. They are also the four most candidates improvise and blur. Say each one cold.

  1. The opening — "tell me about yourself". Timed. 30–45 seconds. Who you are → your through-line → why this role.
  2. The position — "why should we pick you?" One crisp claim, about 20 seconds. No list.
  3. Your three questions — the ones you ask them. Say all three from memory, and say why each one is a good question.
  4. The close — one warm memorable line, then ask about next steps.

Pass bar: the opening lands inside 45 seconds, the position is one sentence and not four, and your three questions are about their problem — not about salary, tools, or process.

What to do with each miss

A miss is information, not a problem — as long as you act on the right one. Match the miss to the fix.
