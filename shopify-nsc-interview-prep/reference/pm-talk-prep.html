Your talk with the PM

This is not the interview. It is the talk that tells you what the interview will be. Walk in to learn; walk out with a target.

The one job tomorrow. Turn "client stack unknown" into a concrete list — the tools, the interview format, and what the client cares about. Everything else (coaching, logistics, a good impression) rides on top of that. Come out of this call and I re-point the whole 7-day plan at the client's real stack.
Remember who you are talking to The PM works for the staffing company that placed you. They only get paid when the client hires you. So they are on your side. You can ask them things you could never ask the client — the real stack, the round-1 feedback, what the client liked and worried about. Ask directly. They want you to be ready.

What this talk is for (three goals)

GoalWhat "good" looks like
1. Client & JD intelYou leave knowing the stack, the interview format, who is in the room, and the round-1 feedback.

A. Client & JD intel — the big prize

Ask these first. If the call gets cut short, these are the ones that matter. Ask for the written JD up front — then use the questions to fill the gaps it leaves.

The stack (this re-targets my whole plan)

  • "Can you send me the full job description so I prepare against the exact requirements?"
  • "What is the client's backend stack? Node with NestJS, or plain Express? Something else?"
  • "What database do they use — PostgreSQL, MySQL, MongoDB? One main DB or several?"
  • "Do they use a queue or message broker? Kafka, RabbitMQ, AWS SQS, BullMQ/Redis?"
  • "Which cloud — AWS, GCP, Azure? Docker and Kubernetes, or something simpler?"
  • "On the Shopify side, is it a custom app, a public app, or headless storefront? Which APIs — Admin GraphQL, Storefront, webhooks?"
  • "Is there a real ERP / CRM / fulfillment system they sync Shopify with? Which one?"

The interview format (so I build the right drills)

  • "How many rounds, and what is each round about — system design, live coding, behavioral?"
  • "Will there be live coding? If yes: build-from-blank, or fix-broken-code? Which language/framework? A shared editor, or my own machine sharing screen?"
  • "Is there a take-home task instead of, or on top of, live coding?"
  • "Will they run a system-design round? At what level — whiteboard a sync architecture, or deep on one piece?"
  • "Roughly how long is each round?"

The people and the feedback

  • "Who interviews me each round — an engineer, a tech lead, the CTO, a manager? Names or roles?"
  • "What did the round-1 feedback say — what did they like, and any concern I should close?"
  • "What is the client really looking for in this hire? What problem are they hiring to solve?"
  • "Is there a bar they are strict about — for example strong system design, or clean live coding?"
  • "Am I competing with other candidates, or is this mine to lose?"
Turn the JD into my next move Whatever the PM says the stack is, paste it back to me after the call. I re-point the 7-day plan the same day: right queue, right database, right live-coding kata. Until then the plan assumes NestJS + SQL + a queue — a good guess, but a guess.

B. Coaching & focus

  • "With the time I have left, where should I focus — system design, live coding, or behavioral?"
  • "Have you placed people with this client before? What tripped them up?"
  • "Is there anything about how this client interviews that I should know — pace, style, trick questions?"
  • "How senior do they expect me to sound — hands-on builder, or someone who leads and mentors too?"
  • "Anything I should not say or should downplay?" (e.g. how much is Node vs Shopify-theme).

D. Sound senior while you ask — the fourth goal

You are being read even in a "quick talk". A few habits make you sound like the senior they want:

  • Ask about trade-offs, not just facts. "Do they sync Shopify to the ERP in real time, or in batches?" sounds more senior than "what ERP is it?".
  • Lead with your strength, bridge to the gap. Say it once, calmly, so the PM can repeat it to the client.
  • Take notes out loud. "Okay — NestJS, Postgres, SQS. Got it." Shows you listen and organize.
  • End with a clear plan. "Great, I'll focus my last days on live coding a webhook worker and a system-design walkthrough. I'll send you any questions by tonight."
Your positioning line (say it once, keep it warm)

"My core is Shopify — six years, deep on the platform: the Admin GraphQL API, webhooks with HMAC and idempotency, rate limits, the whole integration model. The last stretch I've moved that same domain knowledge to the server side — Node and NestJS, queues, syncing Shopify with back-office systems reliably at scale. So I'm not learning Shopify on the job; I already own the hard part. I'm applying it as a backend engineer."

If the PM asks "are you comfortable with live coding?" Say yes, and frame it honestly: "Yes — building real backend pieces, like a webhook handler or a small sync worker, and fixing broken async code. That's the work, so I'm happy to do it live and talk through my choices. I don't do algorithm-puzzle grinding, but that's not this role." Confident, not defensive.
