The Two Mock Run Sheets

A mock is not a quiz you take at your own pace. It is a rehearsal of a specific three-hour event. These are the run sheets for both mocks, the grading sheets, and the debrief that turns a bad mock into a good interview.

What the two mocks are for. Mock 1 (Day 6) is diagnostic — run it cold, find what is still shaky while there is a whole day left to fix it. A bad Mock 1 is a good outcome; it did its job. Mock 2 (Day 7) is a dress rehearsal — the whole arc, harder follow-ups, so the real round feels like a repeat of something you have already done.

These are the mock blocks for Days 6 and 7 of the 7-Day Plan. The question bank lives in L16 · Full mock interview. Composure is graded against L29 · Part A. Say "mock me — mixed" or "mock me — client style" and I run it live.

Five rules that make a mock real 1. Do not pause the clock. Not to look something up, not to restart an answer. 2. No notes on screen. Nothing open but the code editor. 3. Everything out loud, in full sentences, camera on if you can. 4. Record it. You cannot grade your own pace from memory. 5. No feedback until the end — a mock that stops for coaching after each answer trains nothing, because the real room never does that.

Mock 1 — The mixed roundDay 6 · 3 hours

Four surfaces in one sitting, the way a real client round mixes them. Stamina is part of what is being tested — most people are fine for an hour and fall apart in the third.

TimeSegmentWhat happens
0:00–0:10Set upEditor open and empty, timer running, recording on. Say your opening once to warm your voice, then stop.
0:10–0:20Open"Tell me about yourself." Then two background questions about your Shopify experience.
0:20–1:05Live coding (45 min)One kata, typed from a blank file, narrated. Clarify → sketch the plan → build the happy path → harden. Expect one interruption in the middle.
1:05–1:15BreakStand up. No phone, no notes.
1:15–1:55System design (40 min)"Design the Shopify ↔ ERP sync." You sketch, then five follow-ups: no loss and no duplicates, both rate limits, the ERP down for an hour, and one second scenario.
1:55–2:20Management (25 min)Two scenarios, unannounced: a date you are going to miss, and a scope change mid-project.
2:20–2:50Behavioral (30 min)Three questions. Two of your prepared stories, one you have not prepared.
2:50–3:00Your questions & closeYour three questions for them, then your closing line and next steps.

The Day 6 debrief — two hours, in this order

The mock is worth nothing without this. Do it the same day, while the discomfort is still fresh.

TimeWhat you do
0:00–0:30Play the recording back and fill in the composure counts. Numbers first, feelings second — "it went badly" is not usable, "four silences and two bluffs" is.
0:30–1:00Fill in the technical grading sheet. For every failed row write one sentence on what was missing: a fact, the structure, a trade-off, or the failure case.
1:00–1:20Sort every miss into one of four buckets: fact (I did not know it), structure (I knew it, said it badly), composure (I froze or bluffed), story (flat or too long). The bucket decides the fix — see the table below.
1:20–1:40Pick your top three only. Not ten. Three, chosen by what is most likely to come up tomorrow.
1:40–2:00Write the fixed version of those three answers, out loud, then say each twice. Stop there — the re-coding is the next block on the plan.
BucketWhat it meansThe fix
FactYou did not know the number, the name, or the mechanism.Re-read that one section, then say it from memory. Cheapest bucket to fix — do these first.
StructureYou knew it but the answer arrived as a pile.Rebuild it as: one-line answer → why → trade-off → failure mode. Say it three times.
ComposureYou froze, sped up, bluffed, or went silent.Back to the four hard moments. This bucket is the most expensive to ignore — it costs you answers you actually know.
StoryFlat, too long, or missing the result and the reflection.Cut the setup, add the number, add "what I'd do differently". Re-time it.

Mock 2 — Client styleDay 7 · 3 hours

Same length, harder shape. This one is run cold — no warm-up — with interruptions, follow-ups on your follow-ups, and "why?" asked three times deep on the technical answers. The goal is that the real round feels easier than this.

TimeSegmentWhat is different from Mock 1
0:00–0:05Cold startNo warm-up. First words of the day are your opening. This is the real condition.
0:05–0:15Open & position"Tell me about yourself", then "why should we pick you over the other candidates?", then "why are you leaving?".
0:15–1:00Live coding (45 min)A kata you have not done this week. Interruptions at minute 10 and minute 30. One requirement changes mid-way.
1:00–1:10BreakSame as before.
1:10–2:00System design (50 min)Every answer gets "why?" three times. Two questions push into territory you have not prepared — those are on purpose, and they are testing the senior "I don't know".
2:00–2:25Client scenarios (25 min)The three hardest: the date you will miss, the client who has gone silent, and the risky backfill. Pushback after each answer.
2:25–2:50Behavioral (25 min)Two stories, then follow-ups drilling into the same story: "what would you do differently?", "what did the client say?", "what if it happened again next week?".
2:50–3:00Your questions & closeFull close: three questions, your memorable line, next steps. Say it as though the offer depends on it.
The three things "why?" three times is really testing 1. Do you understand the mechanism, or did you memorise the answer? 2. Do you stay calm when an answer you were proud of gets pushed on? 3. Do you know where your own knowledge ends — and can you say so without losing your footing? Reaching the edge is not failure. Bluffing past it is.
Do not chase a perfect Mock 2 On Day 7 the temptation is to keep redoing answers until they are flawless. Do not. Fix the two or three biggest wobbles, run the whole arc twice, then stop and rest. Being rested is worth more on the day than one more polished answer — a tired candidate loses composure first, and composure is what carries the answers you already have.
You're ready when… 1. Every composure count is at target in Mock 2 — zero long silences, zero bluffs, zero bare "I don't know". 2. Twelve or more technical rows pass. 3. You get through the third hour without your pace changing. 4. You can run open → work → close without looking at a single note.
Say "mock me — mixed" for Mock 1 or "mock me — client style" for Mock 2. I run the clock, ask everything on the sheet, interrupt when the sheet says to, and hand you both grading sheets filled in at the end. Say "debrief me" afterwards and I'll sort every miss into its bucket and name your top three fixes.
