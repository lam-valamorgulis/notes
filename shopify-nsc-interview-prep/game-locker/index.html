Game Locker — The Map

Twelve lessons and six reference pages, in two parts. Part A takes you to the point where you could rebuild their platform. Part B gets you through the interview. Both are built on one page of checkable facts.

Start here, either way: Verified Facts About Game Locker — every checkable fact, where it came from, and how much to trust it. Four labels: verified reported inference gap. No lesson re-argues a fact; they cite a row there.

What changed on 2026-08-17

This sub-course was written from Game Locker's public website and the job description. That was enough to talk about them. It was not enough to build anything, and six things it taught were wrong.

Then a real integration discovery arrived — written against a live Game Locker tenant. It corrected those six things and added eighteen facts, including the shape of their API, their auth model, the three-rule pricing engine, and the one that matters most: they hold the store-credit ledger.

Part A — Build It

The goal of this part is narrow and testable: could you rebuild Game Locker? Not use it, not integrate with it. Build it. Every lesson ends closer to a schema, a job, or an endpoint.

A03 · The pricing engine (new)

The most interesting subsystem they have, and the one that is genuinely hard to rebuild.

A03 The Pricing Engine Buy price is derived, not stored. Three rule types — Sell, Buy, and Stop buy — plus a global rounding control. A rule is a filter plus two percentages. Why a rule filtered to one condition refuses mint cards and accepts played ones. The nightly run that publishes at 06:00, and why only ~4% of rows move. And the best fact in the course: the buy price does not reproduce — the ratio matches exactly, the base does not.

A04–A05 · The sync, and the counter

A04 The Sync Engine The ownership matrix — now split by field, because catalogue and price flow out but stock is authored in Shopify. The app proven by Shopify's own event log · echo loops · absolute vs delta · compareQuantity · the oversell minute · webhooks as at-least-once · reconciliation · five worked sync cases on one card.
Revised 2026-08-17: the inventory row now says what it really is — no clean owner, authored in Shopify.
A05 The Counter They Do Not Own Who owns the transaction? None of the three. A third-party till does · the two hops, and you own neither end of the first · the kiosk is customer self-service, so a kiosk order is committed, not sold · locationId baked into every SKU, and "Multi-store (Early access)" · the card you do not stock has no variant to increment.
Read alongside: POS L01 · POS L03 · POS L04 · SIS L02 · SIS L03 · the whole Data Sync sub-course

A06–A07 · The money, and the build (both new)

A06 The Buy List and the Credit Ledger A buylist is an order with the direction flipped — orderType=buy, Quoted → Reviewed → Completed, with payment status and Profit %. Then the sharpest finding in the whole discovery: Game Locker holds the store-credit ledger, keyed to the Shopify Customer ID. Why a second, Shopify-native balance is a double-spend and not a display bug. Why a trade-in is a ledger migration, not an automation.
A07 Rebuild Game Locker The capstone of Part A. Eight modules in NestJS + Postgres — catalogue, pricing, reprice, inventory, buylist, ledger, shopify-sync, api. Tables, endpoints, jobs and invariants, each with an observed behaviour as its acceptance test. Ends with a runnable proof: the pricing engine and its rounding, tested against four real values — including the one that does not reproduce.

What this sub-course deliberately does not do

  • It does not re-teach the parent course. Webhooks, app auth, bulk operations, NestJS, testing, system design — already written. This sub-course points at them.
  • It does not re-teach Liquid syntax. theme-architecture covers that; B02 applies it to many-merchants-one-theme and to performance.
  • It does not claim POS app experience. Read one closely, found real bugs, never authored one — used consistently.
  • It does not invent facts about Game Locker. Public site, the job description, or a labelled row in verified-facts. Inferences are labelled, with sample size.
  • It does not carry one word of confidential client material. No names, no budgets, no project state, no ticket keys.
