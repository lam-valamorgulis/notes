Company Brief — Game Locker

Everything checkable about them, and what each fact lets you say. Read it, then go to their actual site and click every page — this brief is a starting point, not a substitute.

Your win today: quote one number back to them (145 million cards), name three of the games, know their three tiers, and have your five questions written down.
Corrected 2026-08-06 — two facts on this page were wrong Re-read from gamelocker.co on 2026-08-06. (1) Game Locker is not a point of sale. Its kiosk is customer-facing self-service — "Let customers browse your inventory through an intuitive, self-service interface", where collectors submit orders and buylists. No till, no payment. The counter runs on a separate product; Game Locker publishes CSV import templates for Binder POS and Storepass. (2) There is no direct eBay integration — eBay is reached through a Shopify app.

Do not say "your kiosk owns the transaction" in the interview. It is wrong, and it is wrong about their own product. The corrected version is stronger anyway: the counter is a third-party system reached by import, so the widest gap in the chain sits upstream of the Shopify sync — and because eBay hangs off Shopify, Shopify is the hub rather than one channel of several. Worked through in full in the Shopify Data Sync sub-course.

3. Do this before the call

  1. Open gamelocker.co and click every page. Pricing, features, any docs. Look for a customer list or case studies — a named store is a real Shopify site you can inspect.
  2. If you find a customer store, view its source. Which theme? Which apps? Is stock shown per location? That is direct evidence about the integration you would inherit.
  3. Check the Discord if it is public. What do merchants ask for? What breaks? That is the most honest product research available.
  4. Check LinkedIn for the team. Who might be interviewing you, and what is their background? An engineer will probe differently from a founder.
  5. Search the Shopify App Store for "Game Locker". If they have a public app, its listing tells you the scopes and the shape of the integration.
One caution on research Do not open with a list of everything you found — it reads as trying too hard, and some of it may be out of date. Hold it in reserve and use one specific fact at the right moment. Depth used sparingly is more impressive than breadth deployed all at once.

4. Your five questions

Write your own versions on paper. These are the ones chosen to both inform you and show something:

  1. "Is the marketplace meant to run on Shopify, or is Shopify one channel feeding it?"
  2. "Does a buyer need one cart across several sellers?"
  3. "When a store wants something the shared theme doesn't do, where do you draw the line between a theme setting and custom work?"
  4. "Today, when a merchant's counter till and Shopify disagree on stock, how do you find out — and who notices first, you or the merchant?"
  5. "What does the first version of the marketplace have to prove?"

Plus two practical ones if there is time: "How is the team split between the platform and the Shopify side?" and "What does the first 90 days look like for this role?"

5. What they are probably worried about

Useful to guess, because addressing an unspoken concern is powerful.

Their likely worryHow you answer it without being asked
"Theme people usually can't do integrations, and integration people can't do themes. This role needs both."Show both in one answer. Talk about the data model and then how it makes the storefront filtering possible — that is one thought spanning both.
"Will they actually own things, or wait to be told?"The reconciliation instinct is the proof: "heal the explainable, alarm on the unexplainable". Nobody asks a junior to build alarms.
"Can they talk to our merchants, who are shopkeepers not engineers?"The click-and-collect pricing answer — POS Pro, billed per location — is a merchant answer, not an engineer answer.
"Will English be a problem day to day?"Short sentences, slow pace, and never apologise for it. Ask them to repeat anything you did not catch — that is what a confident communicator does.
"Do they understand cards, or will we spend three months explaining the domain?"The 3-options constraint and graded slabs. That is domain modelling and it proves you engaged with the product, whether or not you play.
