Speaking Drills

You know the material — you wrote 52 lessons on it. The gap is saying it out loud, in English, while someone waits. That is a separate skill, and it is trainable in a few hours.

Your win today: one answer shape you use every time, six phrases that buy you thinking time without sounding lost, an opener under 70 seconds, and a clean way to say "I don't know".

Ties to your 24-hour plan: blocks 19, 20 and 22. Record yourself on your phone. Listening back is unpleasant and it is the fastest fix available.

1. Why you freeze, and what to do instead

Freezing is not a language problem. It is a planning problem. When a question arrives, you try to do three things at once: understand it, decide the answer, and build an English sentence. Three jobs, no spare capacity, so the sentence collapses.

The fix is to remove the third job by always using the same shape. If the shape is fixed, you only have to think about content.

THE SHAPE — USE IT EVERY TIME ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ 1. POINT Answer the question in ONE sentence. │ │ No warm-up. No "so basically". │ ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ 2. BECAUSE Why. One or two sentences. │ ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ 3. EXAMPLE A real thing you did or read. │ ├────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤ │ 4. SO What it means for THEM. Then STOP. │ └────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ POINT · BECAUSE · EXAMPLE · SO Four beats. 60–90 seconds. Then close your mouth.
The hardest beat is the last one Stopping. When you are nervous, silence feels like failure, so you keep talking and add weaker and weaker material. The strongest thing in your answer is usually the first sentence — every extra minute dilutes it. Say your four beats, then stop and let them ask. A confident silence is a senior behaviour.

2. Six phrases that buy you time

You need three to five seconds to plan. Silence feels wrong, so people fill it with "umm" and lose authority. Use these instead — they are what native speakers actually say, and they make you sound thoughtful rather than stuck.

3. The 60-second opener

"Tell me about yourself" is the first question and the one most worth scripting, because it is the only one you can fully prepare. Keep it under 70 seconds.

The structure

Now (what you do today) → Proof (the most relevant thing you have shipped) → Why them (one specific thing about Game Locker) → Stop.

A version to adapt. Replace the bracketed parts with your own facts — do not read someone else's words:

"I'm a Shopify developer at an agency in Australia. Most of my work is theme development — Liquid, Online Store 2.0, sections and blocks — for client stores, plus the integrations behind them. [PROOF] The work closest to what you do is the in-store stock side. I've worked on themes that show shop-level availability from a third-party stock system, and I've read our POS UI Extension closely enough to find real bugs in it. So multi-location inventory and the ways online and in-store stock disagree is familiar ground. [WHY THEM] What interests me about Game Locker is that you're the system of record and Shopify is a channel hanging off you. That means the hard part isn't the theme, it's keeping stock honest between a counter and a website — which is the problem I find most interesting. [STOP]" ~55 seconds at a calm pace. Time yourself.
Why "why them" must be specific "I like your company and want to grow" fits any company, so it says nothing. "You are the system of record and Shopify is a channel" could only be said about Game Locker, and it proves you thought before the call. One specific sentence beats three general ones.

5. The four hard ones

A. When you do not know

This will happen. It is not a failure — how you handle it is itself a test, because they need to know what you do when you are out of depth on a real project.

THE THREE-PART "I DON'T KNOW" 1. SAY IT PLAINLY "I don't know that one." No waffle, no guessing, no fake confidence. 2. SHOW THE NEAREST THING YOU DO KNOW "What I do know is [adjacent thing], so I'd expect it to work something like [reasoning]." 3. SAY HOW YOU'D FIND OUT "I'd check the shopify.dev docs for the current behaviour rather than trust my memory, because that area has changed recently." ⚠ NEVER invent an API name or a limit. An interviewer who knows the platform will catch it, and then they stop trusting your CONFIDENT answers too. Guessing costs more than not knowing.

B. When you disagree with them

The JD wants someone who explains trade-offs, which means they expect disagreement. Do it in this order: credit → concern → alternative → their call.

The pattern

"That would work, and it's simpler than what I'm about to suggest. My one concern is [specific consequence]. An alternative is [option], which costs [cost] but avoids [risk]. I'm happy either way — it depends how much you care about [the trade-off]."

C. The gap on your CV

You have not authored a Shopify app or a POS extension. Say it once, cleanly, early, and without apologising twice. Repeated apology is what makes a gap look bigger than it is.

WeakStrong
"I'm sorry, I don't really have app experience, I've mostly just done themes, but I learn fast…""I haven't authored a Shopify app myself. My agency shipped a POS UI Extension and I've read it closely enough to find real bugs in it — so I know the architecture, but not the experience of getting one through review. I'd expect to be slower than someone who has for the first couple of weeks."

The strong version works because it is specific about both what you have and what you lack, and it gives them an accurate expectation. Vague optimism invites doubt; precise honesty invites trust.

D. Salary and expectations

Do not name a number first if you can avoid it. If pushed, give a range and tie it to scope:

"I'd rather understand the scope first — the role covers themes, integrations and POS, which is broad. Do you have a band in mind for it?" If they insist: give your range, then stop talking. Do not justify it or negotiate against yourself in the silence.
