The 24-Hour Plan

24 blocks of one hour. Every block has one job, one page to read, and one thing you must be able to say out loud at the end of it. Nothing here is optional reading "if there is time" — the plan already assumes there is not.

Your win today: walk into the call able to say, in your own words, what Game Locker is, where Shopify sits inside it, and how you would stop a card selling twice — and able to say it out loud without freezing.

1. What you are actually preparing for

Read this before the schedule, because it changes what "preparing" means.

Game Locker is not a Shopify store. It is an Australian SaaS platform that game stores buy. It holds their card inventory (their own metrics endpoint returns 1,122,612 items across 19 games; their site advertises 145,012,624 cards, which counts something else nobody has established — quote the 1.12M). It reprices from a market feed on a nightly batch that publishes at 06:00, though only about 4.4% of cards move on a given day, it runs a customer-facing self-service kiosk in the shop, and it integrates directly with Shopify — eBay only through a Shopify app. It is not a point of sale; a shop's counter runs on a third-party till, and Game Locker publishes CSV import templates for Binder POS and Storepass. (Corrected 2026-08-06.)

So the job is not "make a Shopify site". The job is: Game Locker is the system of record, and you own the Shopify side of it. Three surfaces:

WHERE THIS JOB SITS ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ GAME LOCKER │ │ card catalogue · daily repricing · self-serve kiosk · stock│ │ (the system of record) │ └───────────┬───────────────────┬────────────────────────────┘ │ │ (1) SYNC (2) POS products, stock, the counter takes orders, customers money — stock must │ move everywhere ▼ ▼ ┌────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐ │ SHOPIFY │ │ the game store's storefront — theme, cart, checkout │ └───────────┬────────────────────────────────────────────────┘ │ (3) THEMES + the new TCG MARKETPLACE built for their customers (game stores) and for collectors buying singles YOU OWN ALL THREE ARROWS. That is the whole role.
Why this matters more than any Liquid trivia Most candidates will prepare "Shopify facts". You are going to arrive having already understood their architecture — that they are the ERP, that a third-party till owns the transaction, that 145 million cards cannot live in one Shopify store. That is a different conversation, and it is the one that gets offers.

Then stop. Weak spots get 10 of the 24 blocks. That is deliberate.
The trap in a 24-hour plan The temptation is to spend all 24 hours reading, because reading feels safe and talking feels bad. You already know the material well enough to have written it. The gap is retrieval and speech, not knowledge. If you fall behind, cut reading blocks — never cut blocks 19–24.

4. The schedule

Each block: 40 minutes in, 15 minutes out loud, 5 minutes break. The "out loud" part is not optional — it is the only part that transfers to a live call.

After block 8, this must be automatic

"There is always a window where the till and Shopify disagree. I do not try to remove it — I make sure Shopify never promises the unit I might not have. Buffer the last unit, re-verify at checkout, push on an event instead of a batch, and decide the apology in advance."

The rule for the last hour before the call Read the one-pager, say your opener once, drink water, close the laptop. Cramming in the final hour lowers recall — it fills working memory with the last thing you read instead of leaving room to listen to the question.

6. What to have open during the call

  • The one-pager — the only page you look at.
  • A blank sheet of paper and a pen, for when they ask you to design something. Say "let me draw this" and narrate as you draw. It slows your English down, which helps.
  • Your 5 questions for them, from the company brief. Asking nothing reads as not caring.
