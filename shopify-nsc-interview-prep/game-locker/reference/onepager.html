Them, in one sentence

Game Locker is the system of record — catalogue, stock, daily repricing, POS kiosk — and Shopify is a channel hanging off it. So the hard part of this role is not the theme; it is keeping stock honest between a counter and a website.

145M cards · 18+ games · Starter 20k items / Advanced / Multi-store · Shopify + eBay channels · Australian owned · building a TCG marketplace

The three arrows

1. SYNC products, stock, prices out · orders, customers back fails QUIETLY (drift) 2. POS the counter sells from the same pool fails LOUDLY (oversell) 3. STOREFRONT themes for their customers + the marketplace fails VISIBLY (slow, broken on a phone)

The opener — 60 seconds

Now → Shopify dev at an Australian agency, themes in Liquid and OS 2.0 plus the integrations behind them. Proof → in-store stock work: themes consuming a third-party stock system, and read our POS UI Extension closely enough to find real bugs. Why them → you are the system of record, Shopify is a channel, so the hard part is stock honesty between counter and website. Stop.

The honesty line

"I haven't authored a POS UI Extension myself. My agency shipped one and I've read it closely — an inventory-transfer app — so I know the architecture, and I found real bugs in it. What I don't have is the experience of getting one through review and onto a shop floor."

The bug to name: a receive function returning null on both paths, reading userErrors at the wrong response path, success banner set inside a finally — so a failed receive showed "Transfer received".

Speaking

POINT · BECAUSE · EXAMPLE · SO then STOP Buying time: "Let me think about it for a second." "Let me answer in two parts." "Can I draw this?" "Sorry, could you say that again?" Don't know: say it plainly → nearest thing you DO know → how you'd check. NEVER invent an API name. Disagree: credit → concern → alternative → their call ⚠ NEVER apologise for your English. Slow down instead.

My five questions

  1. Is the marketplace meant to run on Shopify, or is Shopify one channel feeding it?
  2. Does a buyer need one cart across several sellers?
  3. Where do you draw the line between a new theme setting and custom work for one store?
  4. When the kiosk and Shopify disagree, how do you find out — who notices first, you or the merchant?
  5. What does the first version of the marketplace have to prove?

The close

"What makes this interesting to me is that you have two systems that both legitimately write the same number, with real money on both sides. Most sync problems are boring because one side is obviously right. Yours is not — so it can't be solved by choosing an owner, which is the tool that solves everything else."

Do not say

❌ "Real-time two-way sync so they're always consistent"→ direction of ownership, accepted lag, reconciliation
❌ "Just use Shopify POS"→ "I assume the kiosk is deliberate — what does it cost you?"
❌ "Put all 145M cards in Shopify"→ catalogue ≠ stock
❌ "Marketplace Kit would handle it"→ it is deprecated
❌ "I've worked on POS integrations"→ the honesty line
❌ "A 30-minute sync job is fine"→ on stock of 1, that is oversells every week
