Lesson 9 · Shopify Data Sync · When it is wrong

Reconciliation, and Numbers That Do Not Lie

Every event-based sync drifts. That is not a criticism of the design — it is a property of the world. The real question is never whether it drifts. It is whether you find out before Mia's customer does.

Your win today: name four causes of drift that are nobody's fault, explain the difference between a mismatch that is lag and one that is drift, and say why a reconciler that silently fixes everything is worse than one that fixes less.

Builds on Game Locker L03 §7 and stockinstore L16 §6. The new part here is §4 — classifying a mismatch by asking the queue.

1. Drift is not a bug list

Name the causes out loud, because a candidate who says "and then it drifts for some reason" sounds vague, and one who lists these sounds like they have run a system.

CauseWhose fault
The counter till's import has not run yet — so Game Locker itself is hours behind the shopNobody's, and it is by far the most common. Note this is lag, not drift — see §4
A webhook was never delivered — "delivery isn't always guaranteed"Nobody's
A deploy landed mid-batch and the job restartedNobody's
Mia edited a stock number by hand in the Shopify adminNobody's — she is allowed to
A staff member miscounted a stack of cards at the counterNobody's. Physical stock is itself only an estimate
Our subscription was auto-deleted after 4 bad hoursOurs — and it is silent, so only this lesson finds it
The first and last rows deserve a moment Everything in this sub-course quietly assumes Game Locker holds "the truth". It does not, for two separate reasons. It is hours behind the counter till, because that data arrives by import. And even the till holds only the best available record of a physical pile of cardboard in a shop. Someone can drop a card behind a shelf. A slab can be miscounted. So reconciliation is not "make Shopify match the truth" — it is "make Shopify match our best record, and notice when our best record is also wrong". Saying that out loud is a mark of someone who has worked with real inventory.

2. The nightly read-back

You cannot detect silence from inside the fast path (L07 §6). So something has to go and look.

02:00, QUIET HOURS bulkOperationRunQuery ──► every variant, every inventory level, out of Shopify as one JSONL file │ ▼ compare, row by row, against Game Locker │ ├── same → nothing to do (the vast majority) └── different → CLASSIFY IT (§4) before acting WHY BULK AND NOT 12,000 QUERIES: one job, one file, no rate-limit fight, and it does not compete with the daytime sync for the store's ~100 points per second. See Main L18.
The read-back is also the only honest test of your own system Everything else you monitor is your side's opinion of itself — queue depth, messages sent, errors. The read-back is the only measurement that asks the other system what it actually holds. A sync without one is a system that grades its own homework.

3. The two ways to be wrong about a mismatch

Before deciding what to do about a difference, notice there are two bad reflexes.

ReflexWhat it produces
Fix everything silentlyThe numbers look healthy forever while the defect causing the drift keeps growing. You have built a system that hides its own faults and reports success.
Alarm on everythingFour thousand alerts. Everyone mutes the channel in week two, and then the one real alert arrives into silence.
The rule, and it is the best line in the lesson

"Heal the explainable, alarm on the unexplainable. A job that silently corrects everything it finds also hides the bug causing the drift — so the dashboard stays green while the real defect grows. A job that fixes what it understands and escalates what it does not is the difference between a sync you trust and a sync that lies to you."

4. How you tell lag from drift — ask the queue

This is the piece missing from every other page in this repo, and it is what makes "explainable" a real test rather than a nice phrase.

A mismatch at 02:00 has two very different meanings, and the difference is not visible in the two numbers:

MISMATCH: Game Locker says 0, Shopify says 1. NOW ASK OUR OWN QUEUE AND CHANGE LOG: is there a PENDING message that would set it to 0? YES → this is LAG, not drift. The system already knows. It has not got there yet. Do NOT count it as an error and do NOT write anything. NO → this is DRIFT. Nothing in flight explains it. A message was lost, or somebody wrote by hand. THIS is what deserves attention. Same two numbers. Completely different conclusion.
What happens if you skip this step Every mismatch counts as drift. On release Saturday, when the queue is 40 minutes deep (L08), the reconciler reports thousands of "drift" rows that are simply work not yet done. The number is meaningless, so nobody reads it, so the day a genuine lost webhook appears it is one line among thousands. A metric that cannot distinguish lag from drift is not a metric.

With the classification in place, each category gets a clear action:

CategoryTestAction
LagA pending message explains itNothing. Not an error. Feed it into the lag metric instead.
Explainable driftGame Locker owns the field and the gap is a plain missing writeHeal it. Write the absolute truth. Log that you did.
Unexplainable driftOwner is unclear, or the difference is large, or Shopify is higher than Game LockerDo not write. Alarm. A human decides.
Contested fieldThe field is on the register from L03Never auto-heal. Report both values side by side.
Why the DIRECTION of the difference is a signal — with one important caveat Order matters here. Between an import landing and the push completing, Shopify higher than Game Locker is the expected state — that is ordinary lag, and §4's pending-message test catches it first. So run the lag test before reading anything into the direction.

Once lag is ruled out, Shopify holding more than Game Locker believes exists is genuinely odd — a hand edit, a failed decrement, a return processed on one side only, or our own bad write. Silently pushing the lower number down would erase the evidence. Direction is a real signal that most reconcilers throw away — but only after the lag test, or you will alarm on every import.

5. Alarm on the rate, not on the row

ONE mismatch overnight → that is life. Log it. FIFTY in an hour → that is an INCIDENT. ZERO for three days running → ALSO SUSPICIOUS. Is the reconciler even running? Alarm on the SHAPE of the number over time, never on each individual row. And alarm on ABSENCE as well as excess — a metric that goes quiet looks identical to a metric that is healthy, and only one of them is good news.

6. The unfulfillable report

Now the uncomfortable truth that makes this lesson necessary rather than merely tidy.

Go back to the 14:05 oversell. Tom sold the card at the counter, the collector bought it online at 14:07, and by 14:20 both systems say 0.

THE RECONCILER AT 02:00 THAT NIGHT Game Locker: 0 Shopify: 0 ✅ AGREE IT FINDS NOTHING WRONG. AND IT IS RIGHT NOT TO — both numbers are correct. Two cards left the shop and the counts prove it. The oversell is invisible in the STOCK data, forever. IT IS ONLY VISIBLE AS: an ORDER for a card that is not there. → the daily UNFULFILLABLE ORDERS report → which is therefore not a nice extra. It is the ONLY place this failure can ever be seen.
Say this and an interviewer will notice

"Reconciling stock levels cannot find an oversell, because after an oversell both systems agree. Agreement is not correctness. The only place it becomes visible is an order nobody can fulfil — so the unfulfillable report is not a report, it is the detector."

7. Counts agreeing is not money agreeing

One more way for a reconciler to be confidently wrong. It is borrowed from Migration L06, and it applies just as well to an ongoing sync.

What you compareWhat it catchesWhat it misses
Counts — 12,000 variants both sidesMissing and extra recordsEvery wrong value. 12,000 cards at the wrong price still counts as 12,000.
Quantities — sum of stockStock drift in aggregateTwo cards wrong in opposite directions cancel out perfectly.
Money — sum of price × quantityPrice drift, which counts cannot seeStill not per-row, but it moves when something real is wrong.
Row-level differencesEverythingNothing — but it needs §4's classification or it is noise.
The cancellation trap in row two Comparing totals is popular because it is one number on a dashboard. But one card at +3 and another at −3 sum to zero, so the total is perfect while two products are wrong. An aggregate can only prove something is broken. It can never prove anything is right. Use totals as a cheap tripwire and row-level comparison as the actual test.

8. The four jobs, and what each is for

JobWhenIts one purpose
Full read-backNightly, quiet hoursAsk Shopify what it actually holds. The only external measurement you have.
Classify & healSame jobSeparate lag from drift. Fix what is explainable, write nothing where it is not.
Drift alarmContinuousWatch the rate and the direction. Alarm on excess and on silence.
Unfulfillable reportDaily, in Mia's handsThe only detector for an oversell. Needs a human and a written apology procedure.

9. What a reconciler must never do

NeverWhy
Write to a contested fieldBoth sides are legitimate writers. Picking one at 2am is a design decision made by a cron job.
Delete anythingL05. A read-back that came back short looks exactly like a mass deletion.
Heal more than a budget per runSame guard as the deletion budget. Thousands of "fixes" means the reconciler is wrong, not the data.
Run while the queue is deeply backed upIt will classify lag as drift and produce noise. Check lag first, then decide to run.
Fix a problem and not report itA silent fix is a lost symptom. Heal and record.
