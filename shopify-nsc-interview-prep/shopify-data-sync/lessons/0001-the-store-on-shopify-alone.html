The Store on Shopify, and Nothing Else

Before any integration exists, build the card shop as pure Shopify. It works. It is genuinely good. And then at 14:05 on a Saturday it sells a card it does not have — with no bug in it anywhere.

Your win today: point at the exact object that holds the number a collector sees, explain what that number promises and what it does not, and then narrate the 14:05 failure — and say why no amount of Shopify configuration can fix it.

The shop is Harbour City Cards. Read that page first if you have not — one shop, three systems, one pile of cards.

1. Start with no integration at all

Mia opens the shop. She wants to sell singles online. She has heard of Game Locker but has not bought it yet.

So on day one there is one system. Shopify holds the cards, the prices, the stock and the orders. There is nothing to sync, because there is nothing to sync with.

This is worth doing properly, because it decides what the integration is later allowed to change.

DAY ONE. ONE SYSTEM. Mia types a card in ──► SHOPIFY ──► collector buys it │ └─► one place holds the truth No lag. No conflict. No drift. Nothing can disagree with anything, because there is only one of everything.
The idea to carry through the whole sub-course One system is always correct. Not because it is well built, but because "correct" means "agrees with itself", and it has nothing to disagree with. Every problem in the next ten lessons is created by adding the other systems — a till at the counter and a back office behind it — and the value they bring is worth the problems. But be clear that we are buying the problems on purpose.

2. Where the number on the page actually lives

A collector loads the Charizard page. It says In stock. Where does that come from?

Most people answer "the variant". That is wrong, and the wrongness matters, because the sync writes to the real place and not the imagined one.

THE TRIANGLE PRODUCT "Charizard · Base Set · 1999 · #4/102" │ └── VARIANT "NM · EN · Holo" price $480 │ └── INVENTORY ITEM ← the thing that can be counted │ │ InventoryLevel ← THE NUMBER LIVES HERE │ available: 1 │ LOCATION "Harbour City Cards · Sydney" A quantity is not a property of the variant. It is a property of the PAIR (inventory item, location).
ObjectHoldsOne shop has
ProductThe printing — title, images, description~4,500
ProductVariantThe sellable pile — its price, its SKU~12,000
InventoryItemThe countable thing behind a variant~12,000
LocationA place stock can sit1
InventoryLevelThe quantity. One per item per location~12,000

And available is only one of eight states a quantity can be in. The other seven exist for good reasons and POS L03 §3 walks all of them. For now, one of them matters:

The state that is quietly the answer to half this sub-course safety_stock is a state Shopify already has. Stock parked there is counted but not offered. Later, when we want to hold the last copy of an expensive card back from the website, we will not need to invent a mechanism — the platform has one. See POS L03 §4.

3. What "In stock" actually promises

This is the sentence the whole sub-course turns on, so read it slowly.

Say this out loud until it is automatic

"A product page does not report reality. It reports the last thing Shopify was told. When Shopify is the only system, those are the same sentence. The moment there are two systems, they are not."

And the promise gets weaker still further down the funnel. Walk what Shopify does and does not do:

The collector does thisShopify does thisIs the card now theirs?
Loads the product pagePrints its own last-known numberNo
Adds to cartNothing. No check, no hold, no reservationNo. Nothing at all
Opens checkoutStill no promiseNo
PaysDecrements available 1 → 0Yes. This is the moment
A cart is a note, not a promise Ten collectors can hold the same single Charizard in ten carts at the same time. The cart reserves nothing. update.js does not even check stock. This is not a Shopify flaw — reserving stock at add-to-cart would mean abandoned carts locking up inventory for hours. But it means the only moment anything is guaranteed is the moment money moves, which is exactly where our defences will go later.

4. Where Shopify alone is genuinely excellent

It matters that this is not a "Shopify is limited" lesson. On day one the store is good, and naming why keeps the integration honest later — the integration must not take these away.

What Shopify gives Mia for freeWhy it would be painful to build
Checkout that takes money reliablyPayments, fraud, tax, currency. Nobody should build this.
One decrement per paid order, correctlyIts own inventory is strongly consistent at the moment of sale.
A storefront that is fast and searchableThemes, CDN, SEO. See Theme Architecture.
An admin two staff can use with no trainingThe reason Mia bought it.

Read row two again. Shopify never oversells its own number. If it says 1 and two people pay at the same instant, one of them is stopped. That guarantee is real, and it is the reason the failure below is not Shopify's fault.

5. The wall — Saturday, 14:05

Mia's counter already runs on Binder POS, a till built for card shops. She has now also bought Game Locker as her back office — the card catalogue, the nightly repricing, the stock records. Nothing is integrated yet. Three systems simply exist.

ONE CHARIZARD. QUANTITY 1. $480. 14:05:00 Tom sells it at the counter, on Binder POS. The card physically leaves the shop. Binder POS: 1 → 0 ✅ correct, and ALONE Game Locker: still 1 ❌ not told yet Shopify: still 1 ❌ now a lie 14:05:30 A collector in Perth opens the product page. Shopify says IN STOCK. Shopify is NOT broken. It was never told. 14:06:10 They add to cart. Nothing objects. 14:07:00 They PAY. $480 leaves their account. Shopify: 1 → 0. Its own books are perfect. 22:00 Mia exports a CSV from Binder POS and uploads it to Game Locker. NOW Game Locker knows. Eight hours after the card left the shop. TWO PEOPLE HAVE BOUGHT ONE CARD. Every system involved behaved exactly as designed.
Find the bug. There isn't one. Binder POS recorded a counter sale correctly. Shopify decremented its own stock correctly, once, at payment. Game Locker was never told until the evening, because nobody built it a way to be told sooner. The theme printed the number it was given. No component is faulty. The defect lives in the spaces between them — and those spaces are the only thing this sub-course is about.
Count the gaps, because there are two and only one is yours Gap A is the counter till reaching Game Locker — a CSV, measured in hours, and neither end of it belongs to you. Gap B is Game Locker reaching Shopify — an API, which you can make take seconds. If you only ever work on gap B you can make your half a hundred times faster and the website will still be a day out of date. Knowing which gap dominates, and saying so, is the difference between doing the ticket and doing the job.

6. Why you cannot configure your way out of this

Candidates reach for a Shopify setting here. It is worth knowing why each one fails, because the interviewer will offer them to you as bait.

"Just…"Why it does not work
…turn off "continue selling when out of stock"Already off. Shopify thinks stock is 1, not 0. The setting is not being reached.
…check stock at add-to-cartIt would check Shopify's number, which is the wrong number. Checking a stale value faster does not make it fresh.
…make the theme show live stockThe theme reads Shopify only. Liquid has no path to Game Locker, and should not have one.
…let staff also update Shopify by handTwo humans as the sync. Works on a slow Tuesday, fails on release Saturday — which is the only day it matters.
…use Shopify POS at the counter insteadThis one would work — a Shopify POS sale decrements Shopify instantly, so there is nothing to sync. But it is not a sync fix, it is replacing the till, and a card shop's counter needs buylists, grading and condition pricing that a general retail POS has no concept of. Worth naming as the honest alternative rather than pretending it does not exist.
Why the last row deserves a real answer "Just use Shopify POS" is the obvious question and dismissing it sounds defensive. The truthful version is: it removes gap A entirely and replaces it with a worse fit at the counter. Binder POS exists because buying a collection off a customer, grading condition and pricing 300 singles at intake is the actual work at a card counter. So the trade is one integration gap against the wrong tool for the shop's hardest daily task — and stating the trade that plainly is what makes it a senior answer rather than a preference.
The reframe that makes this a senior answer

"Shopify is not wrong. It is stale. Those are different faults with different fixes. You cannot fix stale data by validating it more carefully — you fix it by getting the news to it sooner, and by being honest about what you promise during the gap."
