Lesson 2 · Shopify Data Sync · The frame

Game Locker Behind the Counter

Now there are two systems, and two copies of every fact. The instinct is to reach for a tool that keeps them the same. Do the opposite: refuse the word "sync" until you can say, for each fact, which system is allowed to be right.

Your win today: draw the three arrows out of store 17 from memory, explain why the catalogue and the stock are different things, and answer "keep them in sync" with the better question — when they disagree, who is right?

The shop is Harbour City Cards. Everything factual about the Game Locker platform on this page comes from the company brief — their public site and the job description. Inferences are labelled.

1. What Mia actually bought

Game Locker is not a shop. It is software that card shops buy — "built with game stores, for game stores". Mia's shop is one tenant on it, and in this sub-course it is store 17.

What the platform gives MiaWhy she cannot get it from Shopify
A catalogue of 145,012,624 cards across 18+ gamesShopify has no idea what a Base Set Charizard is. Somebody has to know.
Pricing automation with daily syncs from global platformsCard prices move daily on outside markets. Nobody types 12,000 prices.
A POS kiosk that knows cardsTrade-ins, grading, condition. A generic till cannot price a card.
Inventory, orders, customers, team activity, multi-storeShe wants one back office, not several.

So the shape of the relationship is fixed, and it is the single most useful sentence to have ready:

The sentence that frames the whole role

"Game Locker is the system of record, and Shopify is a channel hanging off it. So the hard part of this work is not the theme — it is keeping stock honest between a counter and a website."

2. The catalogue is not the stock

This confusion sinks candidates, and the numbers make it obvious once you see them side by side.

GAME LOCKER'S CATALOGUE HARBOUR CITY'S STOCK ─────────────────────── ──────────────────── 145,012,624 cards ~12,000 variants every printing ever made the cards in the box shared by every store store 17's own reference data sellable, priced, counted 145 MILLION vs 12 THOUSAND. Ratio of about 12,000 : 1. Mia's Starter tier is capped at 20,000 ITEMS. The 145M catalogue is not her inventory — it is a dictionary.
The mistake this prevents Never push the catalogue into Shopify. A store's Shopify holds only what it physically has. Sending 145 million reference rows to a shop that sells 12,000 of them would take months of API budget, wreck the store's search, and sell nothing. The catalogue answers "what is this card?" The stock answers "do we have one?" — and only the second belongs on the storefront.
Where this is already taught in depth Game Locker L02 §7 works through what actually goes into Shopify and why. This lesson only needs the ratio.

3. The three arrows out of store 17

Draw this on a whiteboard. Then notice how few arrows there are — that is the design goal, not an accident.

GAME LOCKER (store 17) system of record │ ┌────────────────────┼────────────────────┐ │ 1. OUT │ 2. BACK │ 3. OUT ▼ ▲ ▼ SHOPIFY SHOPIFY eBay products, prices, orders, (their own stock levels customers connector) │ └─► the theme reads SHOPIFY only. It never calls Game Locker. Not once. Arrow 1 fails QUIETLY → drift. Nobody notices for weeks. Arrow 2 fails QUIETLY → an order the shop never picks. Arrow 3 is somebody else's connector, but it proves a point: Shopify is ONE channel. Do not design a Shopify-shaped sync.
The point of arrow 3 Game Locker also has an eBay connector. So the correct internal design is "the platform publishes stock to channels", not "the platform talks to Shopify". If the same physical card is listed on a website and eBay, then the oversell problem does not have two doors — it has three. Naming this unprompted shows you understood the product and not just the ticket.

Here is the honest inventory of duplication. Every row is one fact stored twice.

Five rows. Four can differ. This is the moment the job exists.

5. Refuse the phrase "keep them in sync"

Mia will say it. A project manager will say it. It sounds like a requirement. It is not one, because it does not tell you what to build.

"KEEP THEM IN SYNC" hides the only question that matters │ ▼ "WHEN THEY DISAGREE, WHICH ONE IS RIGHT?" Until that is answered, per fact: · every design meeting goes in a circle · every bug is unfixable — because BOTH systems are behaving correctly by their own rules · every fix breaks something else, since there is no rule to check the fix against
Say this early in any sync conversation

"Before I design anything I want to name, for each piece of data, which system owns it. Sync is cheap when there is one owner and one direction. It is close to impossible when both sides can write the same field."

Why "both systems are correct" is the worst kind of bug A normal bug has a component that is wrong, so there is something to fix. Here, Game Locker recorded a real counter sale and Shopify recorded a real online sale. Neither write can be called the mistake. There is nothing to fix inside either system — which is why the answer has to be a rule agreed in advance, not a patch found afterwards.

6. One owner, one direction — and the row that breaks it

Apply the rule to the five facts and something encouraging happens.

FactOwnerDirectionHard?
Card name, imageGame LockerGL → ShopifyNo
PriceGame LockerGL → ShopifyNo
OrdersWhoever took the moneyShopify → GL for online
GL keeps its own counter sales		No
CustomersContested — pick one, per sidePrefer Shopify → GL, matched on emailA bit
QuantityBoth write it, legitimatelyTwo-way ⚠Yes. All of it.

Read the direction column. Four of five are one-way. One-way sync is close to a solved problem: you push, you retry, you reconcile, and replaying it breaks nothing.

Quantity is different, and the reason is physical rather than technical. Two doors sell out of one box. The counter is right to decrement. The website is right to decrement. You cannot fix it by choosing an owner, because choosing an owner is the tool that solves every other row.

The judgement to state out loud Because two-way is where all the difficulty lives, the correct instinct is to resist adding any more two-way fields. Each new one multiplies the conflict cases you must reason about, and each one arrives disguised as a small convenience — "it would be handy if staff could also fix the title in Shopify". That specific request is Lesson 3, and it is harder than it sounds.

7. Customers deserve a real answer, not a shrug

Both systems create customers. Shopify at checkout, Game Locker at the counter. Most candidates say "match on email" and stop, which is only half an answer.

Why matching on email is imperfectWhat it causes
Counter customers often give no emailNothing to match on at all
A household shares one addressTwo collectors merged into one person
People buy online and walk inThe same human as two records
The answer worth giving Shopify owns the online customer record, Game Locker owns the in-store one, matched on email where it exists and deliberately left separate where it does not. Forcing a merge on a fuzzy match produces wrong loyalty balances — and a wrong loyalty balance makes a customer angrier than a duplicate row ever does. Choosing the less tidy option because the tidy one fails louder is exactly the trade-off an interviewer is listening for.
