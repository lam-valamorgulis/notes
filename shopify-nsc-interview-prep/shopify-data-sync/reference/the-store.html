The Store

Harbour City Cards. One shop, one physical pile of cards — and three systems that each think they know how many there are. Every lesson in this sub-course is about this store, so keep this page open beside them.

Why one store and not an abstract diagram: "integrate two systems" is a shape you can nod along to and still not be able to build. A named shop with real numbers forces every rule to have a consequence — a card that exists or does not, a customer who is refunded or is not.
What is real and what is not Harbour City Cards is invented for this sub-course. The name, the numbers and the staff are chosen to be typical of a card shop, not copied from anyone. Game Locker is real, and everything factual about the platform on this page was read from gamelocker.co on 2026-08-06 and is quoted. See also the company brief. Anything inferred is labelled as an inference.
Correction — 2026-08-06. Read this if you saw an earlier version of this page An earlier draft called Game Locker's kiosk a POS kiosk and put it at the counter taking money. That was wrong. Their site describes the kiosk as customer-facing self-service — "Let customers browse your inventory through an intuitive, self-service interface" — and Game Locker is not a point-of-sale system at all. It names Binder POS and Storepass as platforms it accepts CSV imports from, which tells you those are the tills and Game Locker is the back office behind one. The correction makes the problem harder and more interesting, and the whole sub-course is built on the corrected picture.

The sentence to keep saying There is one pile and three counters of it. Every bug in this sub-course is a moment where two of them disagree, and every fix is a decision about which count is allowed to win — plus an honest admission about how long the news takes to arrive.

The hop that is not an integration

Game Locker's site is specific about how data comes in from an existing till, and it is worth reading exactly because it sets the size of the whole problem.

HopHow it worksTypical delay
Counter till → Game Locker CSV import. Their site says it supports imports "compatible with major industry platforms (e.g. Binder POS, Storepass)" with ready-made templates. Hours to a day. Somebody exports and uploads, or a job does it on a schedule.
Game Locker → Shopify A direct integration. "We integrate seamlessly with Shopify." Seconds to minutes, if you build it well.
Shopify → eBay A Shopify app. Their site: "Whilst we do not directly integrate with eBay, stores can install various eBay connector apps through their Shopify." Whatever the app does.
The judgement this unlocks, and it is the best line in the sub-course The widest gap is the one you do not own. You can make gap B take two seconds and the website will still be a day out of date, because gap A is a CSV. So the honest senior answer is not "I will make the sync fast" — it is "I will make my half fast and measurable, and then show you that your real exposure is upstream of me". Engineers who only optimise the part they were handed never find this.

Shopify is the hub, not one channel of several

This is worth stating on its own, because the obvious-sounding version is backwards.

WHAT IT LOOKS LIKE IF YOU GUESS GAME LOCKER ──► Shopify └─► eBay ← WRONG. There is no direct eBay integration. WHAT THEIR SITE ACTUALLY SAYS GAME LOCKER ──► SHOPIFY ──┬──► the online store └──► an eBay connector APP CONSEQUENCE, AND IT IS THE OPPOSITE OF THE GUESS: Shopify is not one channel among equals. It is the HUB every other channel hangs off. So the quality of the data you push into Shopify is LOAD-BEARING for every channel the shop adds later — a wrong stock number there becomes a wrong number on eBay too.

Three ways a card gets claimed

Two doors would be hard enough. There are three, and the third one is easy to forget because it is inside the shop.

DoorWho knows immediatelyMoney taken?Risk it creates
The counter Binder POSBinder POS onlyYesThe big one. Game Locker learns hours later; Shopify later still.
The website ShopifyShopify onlyYesGame Locker learns by webhook — fast, if you built it.
The kiosk Game LockerGame LockerNo — it submits an orderA held card that is not yet sold. Easy to double-promise.
Why the kiosk is a genuinely different case A kiosk order is a request, not a sale — the customer submits it and a staff member fulfils it at the counter. So a card can be spoken for without any money having moved and without leaving the shop. That is exactly what Shopify's committed state is for, and it is why POS L03 §3's eight inventory states matter here rather than being trivia. A design that only tracks available will offer the same card to a kiosk customer and a website customer at once.

The catalogue shape

A card shop does not sell "a Charizard". It sells one specific printing, in one condition, in one language, in one finish. That is the thing with a price and a quantity.

The model is set in Game Locker L02 and reused here without change:

PRODUCT = one printing "Charizard · Base Set · 1999 · #4/102" OPTIONS = exactly three, because Shopify allows three Condition NM · LP · MP · HP · DMG Language EN · JP Finish Normal · Holo · Reverse VARIANT = one sellable pile "NM · EN · Holo" $480 qty 1 Everything else — set, rarity, artist, card number — lives in metafields, because it ran out of option slots.

Only combinations the shop actually holds exist as variants. The shop does not create 30 empty rows per card.

Sealed product changes the rules

The shop sells two very different kinds of thing, and the difference decides how much a sync mistake costs.

SinglesSealed product
ExampleThe NM/EN/Holo CharizardA booster box, a starter deck

The judgement this unlocks You do not need the same speed for all 12,000 variants. Quantity 1 is where the money and the anger are. A design that pushes single-copy cards immediately and batches everything else is cheaper and safer than one that treats every row alike. Saying this out loud in an interview shows you are thinking about the business, not just the pipe.
