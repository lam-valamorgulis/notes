The Review Path

You already own most of this. It is just spread across thirteen pages in five different courses, where sync is never the subject — only a section. This is the route through them, in the right order, with the one line each page exists to teach.

Use this page when the goal is revision, not new material. Walk the seven steps and tick them off. Roughly two and a half hours end to end, and at the end you will know exactly which parts you had forgotten — which is the point.
How the two halves of this sub-course fit This page is the review half. It sends you to pages that already exist. The eleven lessons are the new half — they retell the core in one place around a single store, and they teach the seven things no existing page covers. If you only have one evening, do this page. If you have a week, do both.

Step 1 — What the problem actually is

Do not start with mechanics. Start with the question that makes the mechanics decidable.

✓ReadThe one line it exists to teach
☐ Game Locker L03 §1–2
Refuse to say "sync" · The ownership matrix		 "Keep them in sync" is not a specification. The real question is: when they disagree, who is right? Answer it per piece of data and five of six rows become easy.
☐ Main L11 §1–2
Clarify first · Architecture		 The same problem as an interview question. What to ask before you draw anything.
☐ Migration L01
Migration versus sync		 Eight words sort the whole job: is the old system being switched off? "No" means you were handed a sync wearing a migration's name.

Step 2 — Where a stock number actually lives

Most sync confusion is really confusion about Shopify's inventory objects. Fix that first and half the questions answer themselves.

✓ReadThe one line it exists to teach
☐ POS L03 §1–3
Locations · Where a number lives · The eight states		 A quantity is not on the variant. It is on an InventoryLevel — the join between an InventoryItem and a Location. And available is one of eight states, not the only one.
☐ POS L03 §4
safety_stock		 Shopify has its own answer to the oversell, and most candidates have never heard of it.
☐ Game Locker L02 §2–4
The three-option limit · The model		 Three options per product is the constraint that decides the whole card model: condition × language × finish, and the rest to metafields.

Step 3 — Writing safely, outward

This is the highest-value step. If you remember nothing else, remember this one.

✓ReadThe one line it exists to teach
☐ Game Locker L03 §4
Absolute or delta · compareQuantity		 Absolute writes are naturally idempotent, so replaying one is harmless. A lost delta is permanent drift; a duplicated delta is an oversell you caused yourself.
☐ POS L03 §5
Three mutations, one safe to retry		 The same conclusion reached from the platform side rather than the design side.
☐ stockinstore L02 §5 and §5½
Dedupe key + staleness guard · Snapshot vs delta		 A feed that sends only changes can silently miss a sell-out. A full snapshot cannot. Know which one you are receiving.
☐ Migration L03 §1–3
The second run · Candidate keys · The custom ID		 The question is never "how do I write this record?" but "how do I find the record this already became?"

Step 4 — The channel back

✓ReadThe one line it exists to teach
☐ Main L02 §2–4
Four rules · Retries and auto-removal · Ack fast		 A slow handler becomes a failed handler, and enough failures removes the subscription — so you stop receiving orders and nobody tells you.
☐ stockinstore L16 §1 and §3
Four streams · Delivery rules		 It is not one webhook. It is four separate streams with different rules, and treating them alike is how ordering bugs start.
☐ Main L12 §4–5
Delivery guarantees · Dead letters and ordering		 At-least-once is the normal guarantee. Exactly-once is a story you tell yourself with an idempotency key.

Step 5 — Where two-way goes wrong

Everything up to here is one-way plumbing. This step is the actual hard part.

✓ReadThe one line it exists to teach
☐ Game Locker L03 §3
The echo loop		 Mark where every write came from, or one sale drops stock by two and it only happens under load.
☐ Game Locker L03 §5
The oversell minute		 The defect is not the slow sync. Shopify promised a unit it did not have — so the fix belongs at the promise.
☐ Main L11 §5½
Two-way sync & conflicts		 The conflict rules, stated as an interview answer.
☐ stockinstore L08 §2 and §4
Source of truth per fact · Dual write & the outbox		 Writing to your database and to Shopify in one step is two writes that can half-fail. The outbox is the standard answer.

Step 6 — Proving it works

✓ReadThe one line it exists to teach
☐ Game Locker L03 §7
Reconciliation		 Heal the explainable, alarm on the unexplainable. A job that silently fixes everything also hides the bug causing the drift.
☐ stockinstore L16 §6
Three-way reconciliation		 Two systems agreeing does not mean they are right. A third reference makes disagreement visible.
☐ Main L02 §6
Reconciliation as the safety net		 Webhooks are a hint. The nightly read-back is what makes the hint safe to rely on.

Step 7 — At size

✓ReadThe one line it exists to teach
☐ Main L11 §4 and §6
Rate limits on both sides · Scale & ops		 The other system has limits too. A sync that only respects Shopify's will break the ERP instead.
☐ Main L18 §1 and §4
A job, not a request · When to use it		 For 12,000 rows you submit a job and come back later. For one row you do not.
☐ stockinstore L02 §6
Freshness without hammering		 How to be current without asking constantly.
☐ stockinstore L09
Scale, multi-tenancy, failure modes		 Many tenants on one platform, and the failure modes that only appear when there are many.

What none of those pages answer

This is the honest result of the audit. Seven questions came up repeatedly and no existing page in this repo answers them. They are why the eleven lessons exist.

The questionAnswered in
What does the shop actually need, before any integration exists? Every page starts at the integration. None starts at the shop. L01, L02
What if the widest gap is not yours? Every page in this repo assumes you own both ends of the sync. Here the counter till is a third-party system and its data arrives by CSV — so the biggest improvement available is a conversation, not code. L02 §4, L11 §3
Who owns a single field? Every page owns data per record. Real arguments are about one field — the owner edits a title while the platform owns the price. L03
What happens when a card is gone? Delete, archive, draft, or stock 0? Nobody names the difference, and delete is the most dangerous verb in sync. L05
Whose clock is right? Two systems, two clocks, and "latest wins" quietly assumes they agree. L06
What do you do when the sync is 40 minutes behind? Replay every message, or keep only the newest per card? L08
Who agreed to the lag? The delay is a business promise, and nobody writes it down until a customer is angry. L11
