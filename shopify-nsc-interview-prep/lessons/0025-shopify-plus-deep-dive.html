Shopify Plus Deep Dive

Plus is the enterprise tier. As an engineer, "understanding Plus" means knowing which powers it unlocks, which limits it lifts, and which features simply do not exist on lower plans.

Your win: answer "What does Shopify Plus give you as a developer?" — with a concrete list, not just "it's the expensive one."

Ties to your mission: the client wants an engineer with a good understanding of Shopify Plus. This lesson gathers the Plus-only powers and connects L1 (rate limits), L18 (bulk ops), L23 (checkout), and L24 (Functions).

1. The one idea: same platform, more doors and higher ceilings

Plus is not a different Shopify — it is the same platform with more capabilities unlocked, higher limits, and enterprise tools for running many stores and big catalogs. A senior knows which of a client's requests need Plus, so you never promise a feature the plan cannot deliver.

3. Rate limits by plan (the numbers to say)

The GraphQL Admin API uses calculated query cost, measured in points per second:

PlanGraphQL Admin (points/sec)
Standard100
Advanced200
Plus1000
Enterprise (Commerce Components)2000

So Plus gives roughly 10× the throughput of a standard store. That matters for a big ERP sync: on Plus you can pull and push far more per second before hitting the leaky bucket — and for the truly large jobs you still use bulk operations (L18) to avoid the limit entirely.

Why does Plus get ~10×? Plus stores run on dedicated, enterprise-grade infrastructure, so Shopify allows a higher throughput. See L1 for how a single query's cost is calculated (each field adds points).

Reconcile this with the model you learned in round 1 Lead with the model, not one exact number. The limit is a cost bucket (about 1000 points) that refills at a restore rate. The restore rate scales by plan — older docs show ~50/s on standard, the current per-plan figures above are higher — and a single query is capped at 1000 points no matter the plan. Shopify's own docs show both 50/s and 100/s in different places, so quoting one fixed number is fragile. Say the shape: "a points bucket that refills at a plan-scaled restore rate, with a 1000-point cap per query."

4. B2B — the model to know

B2B is the biggest Plus-only domain. The shape:

Company ── has ──▶ Company contacts (the people who log in and buy) └─ has ──▶ Company locations ── linked to ──▶ Catalogs └─ price lists (negotiated prices) Contacts buy against their location's catalog; orders/draft orders flow via the Admin API.

In plain terms: a company is the customer, with several people and locations, each seeing its own catalog and negotiated prices. You build B2B integrations (order management, invoicing, CRM sync) through the GraphQL Admin API. This is exactly the kind of enterprise sync work the role involves.

The trap: assuming a feature exists on a non-Plus store The classic mistake is scoping work that needs Plus for a client who is not on Plus — bespoke custom-app Functions, B2B, full checkout customization, the 10× limits. Before promising any of these, confirm the plan. Getting this wrong wastes hours and breaks trust; getting it right is a senior signal.

Plus also raises the GraphQL Admin limit to about 1000 points per second — roughly 10× standard — which matters for large ERP syncs, though I still use bulk operations for the really big jobs.
