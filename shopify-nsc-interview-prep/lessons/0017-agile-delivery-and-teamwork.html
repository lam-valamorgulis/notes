Agile Delivery & Working as a Senior Engineer

The interview tests how you work, not just what you know. For a remote services company, this is a real scoring round.

Your win: answer "How do you work in a team and handle changing project priorities?" and "How do you estimate?" — calmly and like a senior.

Ties to your mission: the JD says you will "implement customer requirements based on project priorities set during planning sessions" and "collaborate with frontend, product, and DevOps teams". NSC delivers projects for global clients — they want someone who fits an agile, remote delivery process easily.

1. Agile in one minute

Agile means: deliver working software in small pieces, get feedback, and adjust — instead of building everything in one big batch and hoping it is right at the end. You ship a little, show it, learn, and repeat. Two common ways to run Agile are Scrum and Kanban.

2. Scrum vs Kanban

ScrumKanban
Shape of workFixed sprints (usually 1–2 weeks)Continuous flow, no fixed sprint
BoardSprint backlog → In progress → DoneTo do → In progress → Done, with WIP limits
PlanningPlan the sprint up frontPull the next task when free
Best forFeature projects with a roadmapSupport, bugs, steady ongoing work

Scrum ceremonies (the meetings) are worth naming out loud:

  • Sprint planning — pick and size the work for the sprint.
  • Daily standup — a short sync: what I did, what I'll do, what blocks me.
  • Sprint review / demo — show the finished increment to the client/stakeholders.
  • Retrospective — the team reflects: what to keep, what to improve.
Say this if asked "Scrum or Kanban?" "It depends on the work. Scrum fits feature delivery with a roadmap; Kanban fits support and a steady stream of tasks. Many teams blend them — sprints with WIP limits."

3. Estimation — the senior way

Juniors give one confident number and miss. Seniors estimate honestly:

  • Break the task down first. Small pieces are far easier to estimate than one big blob.
  • Use story points (relative size) or a time range, not a single fixed number.
  • Flag the unknowns and risks out loud ("if the ERP API is slow, this doubles").
  • If something is too unclear, propose a short spike (time-boxed research) before committing.
  • Re-estimate as you learn. An estimate is a forecast, not a fixed promise.
Why this scores points Naming risks and giving a range shows judgment and honesty — exactly what a client wants in a senior they can't see every day.

4. When requirements change mid-sprint

They will. The wrong move is to silently absorb the extra work and then miss everything. The senior move is: acknowledge → assess impact → offer options → let the owner choose.

Recall — cover the page You're mid-sprint and the client adds a feature. Say your full response out loud before you scroll to the soundbite.
Say it like this

"I don't just say no, and I don't silently absorb it. I say: 'Yes, we can do that. It will push the payment feature to next sprint, or we keep both but drop the reporting task — which do you prefer?' Then the product owner or client decides with the full trade-off in front of them."

This is called "say no with options". You protect the timeline and the team without being difficult.

5. What "senior" looks like day to day

  • Ownership — you drive a piece end to end and raise risks early, not on the deadline.
  • Small, clear pull requests — easy to review, with a short description of the "why".
  • Kind, specific code review — explain the reason, not just "change this". You lift the whole team.
  • Mentoring — break work down for juniors, unblock them, review with patience (see L15).
  • Write things down — decisions, docs, and clear status updates.

6. Remote & cross-timezone work

NSC works with global clients across time zones, so the process leans on async communication:

  • Over-communicate in writing. If someone is asleep when you finish, they need a clear written update to pick it up.
  • Use the overlap hours for live discussion; do focused work outside them.
  • Small, frequent updates beat one big silent week. Make your progress visible on the board.
  • Unblock yourself — work independently, but ask early and clearly when truly stuck.
