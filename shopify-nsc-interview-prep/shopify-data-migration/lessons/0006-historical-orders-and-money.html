Lesson 6 · Shopify Data Migration · The hard case

Historical Orders & Money

Orders are the only record type with no CSV import, and the only one where a default value can quietly rewrite history. Five default behaviours in orderCreate will each produce an order that looks fine and is wrong.

Your win today: import a historical order with the right date, the right price, and the right status — and name the trap that sends one email to the whole team for every order you import.

Ties to your mission: order history is the part clients care about most and check first. "Where are my orders?" on launch day is the phone call this lesson prevents.

1. One route in, and it is not a file

From Lesson 2: products and customers have documented CSV routes. Orders do not. The documented routes are migration apps, the Order API and the Transaction API.

So this is code, and the mutation is orderCreate. Its documented purpose is precisely this job — creating orders that bypass a normal checkout, such as importing from external systems.

ConstraintDocumented asWhat it means for you
ScopeRequires write_ordersA protected scope. Expect an approval conversation for a public app.
Token type"only accessible to apps authenticated using offline tokens"A background importer, not a user session. Matches how a migration runs anyway.
Dev store cap"If you're using the orderCreate mutation with a trial or development store, then you can create a maximum of five new orders per minute."You cannot load-test on a dev store. 88,000 orders at 5/min is 12 days. Plan a rehearsal on a real plan.
Discounts"doesn't support applying multiple discounts, such as discounts on line items. Automatic discounts won't be applied unless you replicate the logic of those discounts in your custom implementation."One discount code per order. Historical orders with stacked discounts must be reconstructed as final amounts.

Quotes from shopify.dev · orderCreate, read 2026-08-03.

The dev-store cap is a planning trap, not a technical one Five orders a minute sounds harmless until you try to rehearse. A full-history rehearsal is how you find the other four traps in this lesson — so budget a paid test store, or rehearse with a representative sample and accept that timing data from a dev store means nothing.

2. The five defaults that rewrite history

Every one of these produces an order that imports successfully and is wrong. That is what makes them dangerous — there is no error to catch.

FIVE SILENT WAYS TO RUIN AN ORDER IMPORT ┌───────────────────┬──────────────────────┬─────────────────────────┐ │ you omit… │ Shopify's default │ the damage │ ├───────────────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤ │ processedAt │ now │ 4 years of history all │ │ │ │ dated cutover day │ ├───────────────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤ │ line item price │ the variant's │ 2019 orders priced at │ │ │ CURRENT price │ 2026 prices ← money │ ├───────────────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤ │ fulfillmentStatus │ unfulfilled │ 88,000 orders look like │ │ │ │ an unshipped backlog │ ├───────────────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤ │ transactions │ status derived from │ paid orders show as │ │ │ what you sent (none) │ unpaid │ ├───────────────────┼──────────────────────┼─────────────────────────┤ │ notifications OFF │ they are ON │ one email PER ORDER to │ │ before you start │ │ the whole team │ └───────────────────┴──────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┘ none of these throws an error. all five are visible only if you look at the RESULT, not the response.

Default 1 — the date

This one has an explicit sentence in the docs, and it exists for exactly this job:

shopify.dev · OrderCreateOrderInput · processedAt "The date and time (ISO 8601 format) when an order was processed. This value is the date that appears on your orders and that's used in the analytic reports. If you're importing orders from an app or another platform, then you can set processed_at to a date and time in the past to match when the original order was created."

Read the middle sentence: it is "used in the analytic reports." Omit processedAt and you do not just get wrong dates in a list — you get a store whose entire sales history happened on one day. Year-on-year comparisons, seasonality, customer lifetime value: all gone, and not fixable by editing later.

Default 2 — the price (the money bug)

Shopify's own B2B import guide states the behaviour:

shopify.dev · Import B2B orders "When a merchant doesn't add prices to their line items, the API populates the price automatically based on the variant price" — which "can result in inaccurate order data." The guide recommends implementing validation logic.

This is the most expensive default in the mutation. A 2019 order for a product that cost $19 then and costs $34 now imports at $34. The order total no longer matches what the customer paid, refunds get calculated against the wrong figure, and the reconciliation of total order value from Lesson 1 is the only thing that would have caught it.

Why reconciling money, not just counts, is the whole point Count reconciliation passes cleanly here: 88,204 orders in, 88,204 orders out. Money reconciliation is what fails. This single default is the reason the Lesson 1 report has a total-value row — and a good interview answer names it as the reason.

Defaults 3 and 4 — status

FieldDocumented asDo this instead
fulfillmentStatus"Will default to unfulfilled if not included."Set it explicitly for every shipped historical order, or the client opens Shopify to a fake backlog of tens of thousands of orders.
financialStatus"The financial status of the order. If not specified, then this will be derived through the given transactions."Send the transactions — "the payment transactions to create for the order" — and let the status derive itself. That way the money and the label agree.
Prefer derived over asserted You can set financialStatus directly. Better not to. Send the real transactions and let Shopify derive it, so the label matches the underlying money. Asserting "paid" with no transaction behind it produces an order that reports as paid and has no payment record — a reconciliation problem you have manufactured yourself.

Default 5 — the email storm

help.shopify.com · Migrate to Shopify "When you migrate your historical orders, any staff member, including the account owner, that is set to receive new order notifications will receive a new order email for each imported order." The guide's advice: deactivate the notifications before importing, then reactivate afterwards.

88,204 orders means 88,204 emails, per staff member. It floods inboxes, can get the sending domain rate-limited, and it is the first thing the client's owner notices about your work. It is also completely avoidable with one setting.

Cover the screen Name all five defaults and what each one breaks. Then say which single field protects the analytics history, and which one is the money bug.

3. Order the passes, again

Orders are pointers, so their dependencies must exist first. Shopify's B2B guide says it plainly for its own case: a merchant "must import or create all relevant companies, company locations, company contacts, and products in Shopify before they can import B2B orders." The same rule holds generally.

WHAT AN IMPORTED ORDER NEEDS TO ALREADY EXIST products + variants ──┐ ├──▶ orderCreate ──▶ order customers ────────────┘ │ ├─ processedAt ← the real date (B2B: companies, ├─ lineItems ← WITH prices locations, contacts) ─────────┤ (never let them default) ├─ transactions ← lets status derive └─ fulfillmentStatus ← set it explicitly and BEFORE the first call: · switch off new-order notifications · decide how far back you are going
How far back? Shopify's guidance: at minimum, migrate all historical orders inside the business's return policy window, so customers can still see orders open for return or exchange. Their recommendation is 2 to 5 years. This is a scope lever worth using — going back 3 years instead of 12 can halve the project.

4. Gift cards, store credit, and saved cards

The other money records. Each behaves differently and each surprises someone.

RecordWhat is possibleThe catch
Gift cards giftCardCreate accepts your own code. Without one, "the system generates a random 16 character alphanumeric code." Supplying the original code is the whole point — otherwise a customer holding a physical card cannot spend it. You are importing the remaining balance, not the history behind it.
Store credit Listed by Shopify as migratable alongside gift cards. Same shape: you move a balance, not a ledger. Agree with the client which figure is authoritative before you write it.
Saved payment methods Credit cards from Stripe; credit cards and Apple Pay from Braintree. PayPal and Google Pay are not supported. Moving raw card numbers needs Plus or Enterprise plus Shopify Professional Services; moving payment tokens is available on all plans.

Sources: giftCardCreate · Migrate to Shopify · Migrating payment methods, read 2026-08-03.

Where this becomes a business problem If the client runs subscriptions and their saved cards are held by an unsupported gateway, those subscriptions cannot simply move. Every subscriber has to re-enter a card, and a share of them will not — that is churn caused by your migration. Raise it in week one. It is a revenue decision, so it belongs with the client, not with you.

Remember this
  • Orders have no CSV import. Migration apps, the Order API and the Transaction API only.
  • orderCreate needs write_orders and an offline token, and caps at 5 orders per minute on trial or development stores.
  • processedAt is the field that saves the analytics history — it "is used in the analytic reports", and the docs say to set it in the past for imports.
  • Omitting a line-item price silently uses today's variant price. This is the money bug, and only a value reconciliation catches it.
  • fulfillmentStatus defaults to unfulfilled — set it, or the client sees a fake backlog.
  • Send transactions and let financialStatus derive itself. Never assert "paid" with no payment behind it.
  • Switch off new-order notifications first — otherwise one email per imported order, per staff member.
  • Gift cards keep working only if you supply the original code.
  • Saved cards barely move: Stripe credit cards, Braintree credit cards and Apple Pay. Raw card numbers need Plus or Enterprise plus Professional Services.
  • 2 to 5 years is the recommended history, with the return-policy window as the minimum.
